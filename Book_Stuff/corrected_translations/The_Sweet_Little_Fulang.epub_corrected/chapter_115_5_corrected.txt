Chapter 115 Part 5


Shen Xuanqing, who had been bending over to look at the pregnant rabbit, released his hands from his knees, stood up straight, and said, "Well, I'll sell the rest."

He was really tall, and when he straightened up his shadow fell over Lu Gu beside him.

"Do you want to raise the sheep?" Shen Yaoqing asked again.

"Keep it for now. I'll see if I can catch an ewe," Shen Xuanqing replied, taking the bamboo basket from Lu Gu's hand.

"Okay, then you two go first. We will go to look at the land in the afternoon," Shen Yaoqing said, picking up the shovel to keep working.

The black-hoofed sheep tied in the corner seemed to have accepted its fate. It had been tied up on the mountain for more than half a month, and now it had grown used to being fed grass, lowering its head to eat and gaining weight at the same time.

There were not many animals for sale today—seven pheasants and eleven rabbits. Carrying them in a bamboo basket would be quite heavy, so Shen Xuanqing put them in the cart. The road to the town was smooth; whether pushing or pulling the cart, it wasn’t too strenuous, and it gave his shoulders a break.

He was tidying up in the yard while Lu Gu counted his own chicken and duck eggs in the kitchen. He had eight hens, and all the eggs laid before going up the mountain were taken away by them. Exactly one month had passed; now he had 178 eggs.

Raising autumn chicks was like this: when it was time for them to grow up, it was winter, and there were few insects and earthworms to eat. They grew smaller and couldn’t lay as many eggs as Wei Lanxiang's big hen. Otherwise, eight well-raised, strong hens might lay more than two hundred eggs in a month.

However, after saving so many eggs, this was something Lu Gu had never seen before—178 eggs.

It was the season for laying eggs, and the seven ducks also laid a lot of eggs—153 eggs.

Shen Xuanqing quickly packed up the cart, came over, and heard him counting in a low voice. He smiled and stood beside him without disturbing him.

"How should we handle it?" After counting, he asked Lu Gu without interrupting.

"Hmm." Lu Gu thought for a moment and said, "I'm going to sell 100 chicken eggs and 100 duck eggs. That will leave 78 chicken eggs. This time we can bring more when we go up the mountain. We can leave the rest for my sister-in-law. There are 53 duck eggs left, so I will ask my mother to help pickle them into salted eggs."

"That's great. You can sell them for money and eat them to nourish your body," Shen Xuanqing praised him generously. As he spoke, he went outside to get a bamboo basket. This was the one Wei Lanxiang used to sell eggs, padded with thick straw.

Eggs were easy to break, so they had to be careful when placing them in the bamboo basket. One layer wasn't enough to hold all the eggs, so they added another layer of thick straw on top.

Wei Lanxiang heard the noise and came over to help. She hadn't seen her second son and his fulang for a long time, so she couldn't help but chatter more, sounding a bit nagging. She said that luckily she had raised a lot of chickens and ducks last year—more than a dozen of each—and they could lay ten eggs every day, something unimaginable in the past. Then she noted that all the chickens at home could lay eggs, though the price of eggs was too low.

"When I was your ger's age, raising five hens at home was a big deal. We could only eat eggs a few times a year. Only the rich could afford them." She helped spread the straw in the bamboo basket and reminded Shen Xuanqing, "Remember to walk slowly and steadily."

"I know." Shen Xuanqing agreed, placed the bamboo baskets on the cart, and went out with Lu Gu.

They walked steadily all the way. They left late today, and the morning market was almost over, but Shen Xuanqing was not in a hurry at all. The wheels of the cart rolled slowly across the street. Lu Gu walked along the street calling, "Eggs, eggs for sale."

His voice was neither too loud nor too soft; it sounded gentle and mild, so people passing by did not feel it was noisy or ear-piercing. Moreover, he was dressed neatly and looked good, so passersby couldn’t help but pay more attention to him. He actually closed a few deals, selling ten or eight at a time.

There were more poor people than rich people. To make money, women and fulangs had to show their faces. The world was not peaceful. People who looked good were easily targeted by bad guys, but with a tall man like Shen Xuanqing by Lu Gu's side, the ruffians who looked around dared not have any ill thoughts at all.

There were clouds today, intermittently covering the sun, so it wasn’t too hot. They walked to the market, intending to stay in town for a while to see if they could sell them all. Ger.
