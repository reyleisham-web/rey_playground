Chapter 45

During the autumn harvest season, chaff and rice bran lay everywhere outside Qingxi Village. The ground outside the village was speckled with chaff and bran; these leftovers were not discarded. Every family swept them up and saved them to feed chickens, ducks, cattle, and sheep. For the poor, rice bran was something that could stretch a meal and sate the belly.

After waking early and finishing breakfast, Lu Gu chopped a large dustpan’s worth of grass, mixed it with the chaff, and carried it to the backyard to feed the chickens and ducks. The pup followed him, but Lu Gu knew it would bite the chickens and ducks, so he had to ask Shen Yan to take it away to play in the front yard.

Autumn rice harvest was keeping the household busy. Before going down the mountain, Shen Xuanqing told him he would stay at home for half a month. He worried about the chickens and ducks. When he went down the mountain, he locked the chickens and ducks in a bamboo cage and pulled them down with the cart. Now the flock was only half-grown, no bigger than a hen, and could fit in a bamboo cage.

The hens were cooing and the ducks were quacking. They were kept separately in two enclosures. After he poured feed for the chickens, he poured feed for the ducks. Finally he turned over the bamboo dustpan and patted it on the back to remove the residue.

With an empty dustpan in one hand, he counted the chickens and ducklings who were pecking at their food, and he breathed a sigh of relief when he saw that none were missing, though he kept an eye on a limp chick in the corner. Why was there blood on its body?

Lu Gu grew anxious. He went inside, caught the chick, and found wounds on the back of its neck and the base of its wings, with its feathers stained with blood.

What should he do? Should he fetch some medicine? He looked carefully at the other chickens and ducklings. Fortunately, only this one was injured, so he hurriedly grabbed the chick and carried it to the front yard.

Shen Xuanqing was entering the door with Shen Yaoqing. They had just gone out to inquire about the price of new rice and had negotiated to bring some new rice to town to sell in the next two days when the price was favorable. When they looked up, they saw Lu Gu’s anxious expression.

“What’s the matter?” Shen Xuanqing’s eyes fell on the chicken in his hand, and he stepped forward to examine the injury, finally saying, “It’s okay, it’s just a small injury. It must have been pecked by a bigger chicken. Don’t worry—get some medicine and it will be all right.”

Lu Gu also thought that it must have been the big chicken bullying his little chick—the big dog wouldn’t bite, and the puppy had been in the front yard today, so it could only be the big chicken.

“Can I separate them?” Lu Gu asked in a low voice. The chickens were not raised in the same litter, so he thought it would be better to separate them.

Shen Xuanqing couldn’t help but answer, “Okay, I’ll go set up a fence. The old fence I pulled out before hadn’t been burned, so it’s good to simply surround it. It doesn’t need to be too much trouble, and we’ll go up in a few days.”

Shen Yan was sitting on the eaves, doing needlework, and the puppy was gnawing at the leg of the stool she sat on. Hearing what they said, she interjected, “Brother Guzi, there’s medicine for bleeding up the hillside behind, and I’ll pick some for you.” Lu Gu nodded quickly, put the chicken in a bamboo basket, and said, “I’ll go with you.”

When the two of them came back from picking the medicine, Shen Yan mashed it with a stone to make a green medicinal paste, and Lu Gu carefully found a rag, because when he applied the paste to the chick the chick would tremble and the medicine would fall off, so wrapping it around seemed safer.

After they finished, Shen Xuanqing also set up a small fence in the backyard, drove the chickens and ducks into it, and separated them from the bigger birds. Lu Gu put the injured chick inside as well.

He had originally wanted to keep the injured one alone in the front yard, but seeing the lively and sturdy pup that could only bite the leg of the stool, he had no choice but to give up.

Speaking of which, the cub was quite close to Lu Gu. Once Shen Xuanqing left for the mountain, there would be only the two of them. Compared to the chickens and ducks, the cub looked like a child. It followed Lu Gu wherever he went, with a pair of bright black eyes wide open, it tilted its head and barked a few times, its fat little paws would sometimes step on his shoe laces, and its fat, round body was utterly adorable.

When Lu Gu returned to the front yard, the pup, having been scolded by Dahui, did not dare go near Dahui again. It found a corner and lay down, its small head resting on its two front paws, its eyes looking pitiful and aggrieved.

Seeing this, Lu Gu walked over, knelt in front of it, patted its head, and lightly squeezed one of its ears. “If you are a good boy, Dahui won’t be mad at you.”

No one spoke to him in the mountains; sometimes the cub was too naughty, and he couldn’t help but hug it and murmur a few words. The cub wasn’t human, and even if he didn’t understand what he was saying, it would wag its little tail and tilt its head to listen.

As Lu Gu said, he picked the pup up from the ground, turned around, and saw Shen Xuanqing coming out of the main room. He stood there a little embarrassed, thinking that no one would talk to a dog like him; if anyone overheard, they would surely laugh.

The pup seemed moved and was hugged, and its grievance disappeared. It barked twice in his arms, licked his fingers, played with him, and nibbed him a few times. It was ticklish but didn’t hurt.

Shen Xuanqing did hear Lu Gu talking to the pup. Lu Gu usually spoke in a soft voice, or lowered his head as he spoke, whispering to the pup, but Shen Xuanqing felt something different this time, so he looked at Lu Gu a little more closely.

Compared to him, Lu Gu seemed more comfortable with the puppy.

After noticing Lu Gu’s embarrassment, Shen Xuanqing narrowed his eyes, thought for a moment, and said, “It’s been so long since we carried it back; we should give it a name.” He didn’t mention what had happened earlier, and he didn’t laugh at him, letting Lu Gu collect his composure. He thought for a moment but couldn’t decide, so he said, “I don’t understand this; you name it.”
