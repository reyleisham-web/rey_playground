Chapter 20 Part 26


While Shen Xuanqing watched Lu Gu, he thought Lu Gu was lazy and had made a mistake, so he lowered his head quickly and sprinkled the medicine powder, not daring to look at the dog again.

There was realgar in the powder, along with other remedies to ward off snakes and insects that could keep pests out of the courtyard. If Shen Xuanqing were alone, there would be no need to buy them, but when buying the medicines, he wanted Lu Gu to come along. No matter what, Lu Gu’s skin and flesh were gentler than a rough man’s, so it was prudent to take extra precautions.

It took four whole packets of powder to finish this round. Shen Xuanqing was quick at filling the soil; after Lu Gu finished, he would tread the filled soil so that the loose earth could settle, ensuring the powder wouldn’t be washed away by rain.

When they finished their work, the sun moved to the western sky, and Lu Gu, without doing anything else for the day, went to the kitchen to cook.

Autumn was here, and in the mornings they usually ate rice porridge. The soft, warm white rice sat heavy and comforting in the stomach. This morning, because they were hungry, they didn’t cook rice and only prepared vegetables. Yet this meal did give them time to make some rice porridge.

There wasn’t much water left in the tank, so Shen Xuanqing carried the wooden barrel to the river to fetch more. There was a low place about a mile from the house—the Qingxi River that flowed from a deeper place—so fetching water was convenient.

After the white rice was put into the pot, Lu Gu set the drawer back, placed in the steamed buns and four slices of pumpkin, lit a fire beneath the pot, then turned and walked to the counter to chop vegetables, busy on his own.

Shen Xuanqing ran a few times to fill the waist-high water tank. By then the pot was steaming, the vegetables were cut, and he wiped his hands on his apron, crouched on the ground, and added two sticks of firewood, letting the fire burn a little longer.

Shen Xuanqing, who had put down the barrel, glanced at the cutting board. There was only one vegetable, the winter melon he had picked at his third uncle’s house yesterday. He had brought back two large ones; one would be enough to fry into a big bowl. Lu Gu had also cut three green chilies, planning to fry them with the dish.

They had eaten stewed chicken yesterday, but today it was back to vegetarian, which was common in the countryside—meat was eaten only occasionally. Yet for Shen Xuanqing, a hunter, he was different from the man who worked in the fields. In the past, he often skipped meat to sell it on the mountain, cooking wild greens instead. Now with Lu Gu, they preferred to have something meaty available—if not all at once, at least a piece of pork or sliced meat when cooking.

He was so tall that it was hard for people to ignore him, but Lu Gu had work to do, so there was no need to worry about being stared at.

The pumpkin was steamed, and the steamed buns were piping hot. When he took out the drawer, he saw that the rice porridge was ready as well. It was so hot it burned to touch. Just as Lu Gu was about to stir-fry the vegetables, he scooped out some rice porridge first.

Shen Xuanqing wasn’t idle, but his eyes were sharp. After Lu Gu scooped the porridge, he poured a gourd of water into the pot.

Lu Gu scrubbed hurriedly, wiped the water from the pot with a cloth, and added some more firewood to the bottom of the stove. Once the fire burned hot, the pot dried visibly, and the cooking went smoothly in one go.

Seeing that there was only half a pot of oil left in the clay pot, Shen Xuanqing thought to himself that they would refill it next time; there were only the two of them, and this should be enough for a while. If it grew colder, a can of lard would be delicious for cooking. Wild boars were deep in the forest now; if they were lucky they might meet one. If they didn’t see any in half a month, they could go to town to buy some fat meat and bring it back.

Lu Gu didn’t know what was on his companion’s mind, but he saw the dishes fried to not stick, and a sense of peace settled in his heart.

During the meal, Lu Gu also took a small bowl of pickled vegetables, along with steamed buns, rice porridge, steamed pumpkin, and stir-fried vegetables—a complete meal for the two of them.

They ate early today, and the sun was just about to set when Lu Gu finished cleaning up the kitchen.

Shen Xuanqing hadn’t planned to go out to check the traps. First, it was tiring to drive the cart along the mountain road, and second, there was a selfish reason: he wanted to be close to Lu Gu. But when it came to what to do after getting closer, he couldn’t think of a reason, so he gave up for the moment.

“I’m going to set up a net in the river. Will you come?” Shen Xuanqing asked, then added, “So you can learn the nearby road.”

Lu Gu came out of the kitchen, thinking about what to do, and, upon hearing those words, he moved a little, nodded, and followed him outside.

The river was not far away; you could see it in the west from the gate of the courtyard. Two pepper trees grew on the west side of the courtyard wall, and when those trees grew tall enough they would form a barrier; the two were not small when joined.

Early autumn, the green peppers would turn red in time. Coming close, they could still smell a faint, biting aroma.

“There are more than thirty miles in there,” Shen Xuanqing said, pointing north as he walked, “and you’ll go deeper into the old forest. Don’t go there unless you have something urgent. The old forest is dense and easy to lose your way without a dog. I don’t go there often, so don’t go alone.” 

Shen Xuanqing carried the net on his back, and, pointing north while walking, instructed Lu Gu. Finally he added, “If you have something urgent and you must return, you won’t get lost if you follow the river. But some parts of the river are hard to walk, and you’ll have to detour through the mountains before it flows down.”

“Yeah, I see,” Lu Gu nodded seriously.

Dahui and the little dog were eating in the courtyard. They smelled the hot bran and flour mixed with leftover soup in the basin, looked up, and hesitated to eat, but Shen Xuanqing had gone out, and there was no bone or stick today, so they had to bow their heads and eat what was there.

Shen Xuanqing never treated the dogs badly. After all, if he wanted them to hunt with him, they needed to stay fit. He could raise the three of them to keep their fur smooth, but he wouldn’t let them gnaw bones all day.

After he cast his net by the river, the three dogs came running over after finishing their meal, circling around him.

Having been frightened by the black dog before, Lu Gu did not dare to go near them, but Dahui was different; he followed beside him to offer protection on the mountain. He seemed very loyal, even daring to touch Dahui’s head.

Shen Xuanqing tied the edge of the net to a large rock on the riverbank. If he came back tomorrow morning, perhaps some fish would have been caught.

The river was rushing, and the afterglow of the setting sun shone on the water, making it look particularly beautiful. The mountains and forests were quiet, occasionally punctuated by a mellow or piercing birdsong, and it felt as if one could forget the troubles of the world, as if standing in a paradise far from human entanglements.
