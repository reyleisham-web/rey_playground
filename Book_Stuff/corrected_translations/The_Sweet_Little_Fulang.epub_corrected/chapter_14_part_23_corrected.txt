Chapter 14 Part 23


As for Lu Gu, when he heard about eight taels of silver, he felt it was unbelievable. A small deer could be sold for so much money, and if it were converted into copper coins, the pile would be beyond his counting. Unbelievable at the same time, he also knew that it belonged to Shen Xuanqing, and he had nothing to do with it. But this did not dampen his joy. It was his selfishness speaking—he knew the Shen family would be happy to have so much money, and they wouldn’t beat and scold him when they were in good spirits.

When they passed the pastry shop, the shop had just opened, and the clerk was delivering a square wooden tray, placing all kinds of pastries on the counter one after another, the sweet scent wafting from inside. Lu Gu followed Shen Xuanqing to a stop.

“Pack six pieces of Baiyun Cake.” Shen Xuanqing withdrew a handful of copper coins from his sleeve, counted twenty, and handed them to the clerk, then took the oil-paper bag from him. Baiyun Cake was expensive—ten copper coins for three pieces. It was as delicate as curd fat, as white as clouds in the sky, sweet but not greasy after a bite, and it melted on the tip of the tongue, leaving an endless aftertaste. This item had sold well in the town for the past two years. Those with a little money in the countryside would learn to eat and wear like people in town, which made other villagers envious. If someone could afford this and take it back, it would be a matter of face.

Lu Gu had once seen Lu Wen eat this: a cup of tea and three pieces of Baiyun Cake. Lu Wen would cover his mouth with a handkerchief while eating, the gesture of raising his hand was elegant and beautiful, and he ate it. He was guarded by Du Hehua; even a single glance could get him sent to wash clothes by the river. In truth, he was not greedy; greed was useless and only made people uncomfortable, so it was better to think about other things.

Shen Xuanqing turned around from the pastry-shop door, glanced at his thin and quiet husband, then handed over the oil-paper bag and said, “I’ll share it with my mother when I go back.”

Lu Gu was flattered. Even after they left the town, he still couldn’t quite believe it, carefully holding the oil-paper bag. It was very light, but it had cost twenty copper coins, so he became increasingly cautious.

On the way back, Shen Xuanqing walked slowly, so Lu Gu did not have to hurry. When they reached Anjia Village, they were coming from the town side and could only enter through the front of the village, the few houses they passed belonged to Lu’s clan.

The sun had already come out, and those who should have gone to the fields had already left for work. Most of the houses were filled with women, children, and the elderly. The door of Lu’s courtyard was ajar, but no one was inside, and no sound could be heard from within. Lu Gu restrained himself after a glance, lowered his eyes, and remained silent. No one knew what he was thinking.

Shen Xuanqing noticed his expression, but seeing that he did not linger or hesitate, he felt relieved. People like Du Hehua and Lu Daxiang were not worth dealing with; the more alienated they were, the better.

When the people of Anjia Village saw Lu Gu, they all knew he had been sold to Qingxi Village. The chatter stopped abruptly, and they watched him as he walked past with their eyes.

Lu Gu had a timid personality; though he wasn’t generous, he wasn’t awkward or odd either. In the village, whenever he met any aunts or grandmas, he spoke in a low voice. In the eyes of the villagers, he was not very talkative and a little dull, but he remained well-behaved. Now, being watched in this way, he hesitated even to call out, and since he had already walked up, how could he speak?

In the end Aunt Qin, who was always straightforward, asked him where he came from. It was no different from how he’d been treated in the past, so he spoke up and said he was from the town. Aunt Qin couldn’t help glancing at Shen Xuanqing. Because of Lu Gu, Shen Xuanqing had called Aunt Qin along with him. Aunt Qin was a little surprised, but she still answered with a smile. Then she heard someone calling her from home and hurriedly told the two of them, “Auntie has something to do, so I won’t talk now.”

Such a homely exchange eased Lu Gu’s earlier uneasiness. Wang Langzhong was drying medicinal materials in his courtyard and was pleased to see him remembering the days and at least showing concern for Lu Gu’s injury. The country people had little money, and injuries and illness were what they dreaded most. It was one thing to recover from minor illnesses and misfortunes, but there were always people who delayed healing, which made him, a barefoot doctor, shake his head.

After removing the linen cloth from Lu Gu’s head, Wang Langzhong nodded and said, “It’s okay, the wound has healed, so you don’t need to wrap it up. Just take the medicine for a few days.”

“Don’t get injured again.” He told Lu Gu while mixing the medicinal materials.

This time they took the medicine for five days. According to Wang Langzhong’s instructions, they would come back to check the pulse later. If there was no deficiency of Qi and blood inside, they wouldn’t need to take the medicine.

The wound medicine applied to Lu Gu’s head was gathered by Wang Langzhong himself from the mountains; it wasn’t precious. It cost only eight copper coins for two packs. And Lu Gu had to be charged for the medicines he took, because in the prescriptions, besides the medicinal materials he picked himself, there were also fees for the materials gathered from others.

Whenever farmers came to see him, Wang Langzhong tried to use cheap medicines with good effects to keep costs down, but this time Lu Gu’s forehead wound had bled quite a bit, and he had to add an expensive component to the medicine; one day of that medicine cost 70 copper coins, and the seven days last time had cost 490 copper coins. However, for these five days of medicine, he removed the expensive ingredients and substituted cheaper ones.

People often say that seeing a doctor can bring down a family. When Lu Gu saw how much money Shen Xuanqing had spent, he was frightened, at a loss, and dared not vent his anger.
