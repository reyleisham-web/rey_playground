Chapter 43


Lu Wen left, and the Shen family did not linger on the street for people to keep watching.

Shen Xuanqing walked on and said: “Mother, sister-in-law, there’s no need to be angry with him. It’s best if this kind of person isn’t married.”

Hearing this, Wei Lanxiang began to chant Buddha: “Amitabha, isn’t it? Thank heaven we didn’t marry him.”

Lu Gu followed behind and didn’t dare to speak; Shen Yan stood next to him, and the smile vanished the moment she saw Lu Wen just now, still glaring at him. This man was good-looking, but his heart was truly bad.

Just now Ji Qiuyue was so angry she didn’t care about proprieties. Now that she heard Lu Wen’s words, being a country girl after all, she asked worriedly: “Mother, is there something wrong?”

Wei Lanxiang was still thinking; Shen Xuanqing spoke first: “It’s okay, leave it to me, you don’t worry about it—just live as usual. These days I’ll stay in town to manage things.” He spoke with such reliability, not the slightest hint of worry, which made Ji Qiuyue’s heart drop back to her stomach, and she nodded in response.

Walking back out of town, under Shen Yaoqing’s jokes, the whole family smiled again; Wei Lanxiang also chatted with a smile. If there were real trouble, those who go barefoot were not afraid of those who wear shoes—at worst they would fight for their old bones. [the barefooted are not afraid of those who wear shoes; fig. the poor, who have nothing to lose, don’t fear those in power]

Shen Yaoqing agreed with her to show his determination, which made his mother happy anyway.

Speaking of which, the Shen family really did have a marriage contract in their hands. It was a private contract, not an official one. This was done according to Qingxi Village’s customary rules. The private contract only needed to invite a matchmaker and a prestigious elder who could read and write. It was not easy for rural people to get married. Once the marriage contract was written and they married, they would live together.

When Wei Lanxiang married, it was a private marriage contract, and Ji Qiuyue too. It could be said that every family in Qingxi Village was like this, and other villages were also similar. Besides, before the Lu family showed their fox tails, how could they have thought they wouldn’t marry? So even if Shen Xuanqing had studied, because of this vulgar agreement, he never thought about the government at all when he got engaged.

At the beginning, Shen Xuanqing didn’t make a fuss with the government, but then Shen Xuanqing learned that the Li family had a good relationship with the county magistrate. The people in the village weren’t very familiar with the law. It would be fine if the county magistrate were reasonable and law-abiding, but if he helped the Li family, he wouldn’t be able to fight with his mud legs, and he would implicate the family. After seeing the face of the Lu family, he was unwilling to get involved with them again.

As for Lu Gu, Shen Xuanqing said that when he bought him, there was no time to speak plainly, and he asked Bao Zhiru and several people in Anjia Village to testify. He pressed his finger on the deed of sale, to prevent the Lu family members from making trouble one day, so they could have a slave contract to talk about.

The deed of sale was clearly written with the money and signed, and it was pressed with the buyer’s and seller’s consent, not forced to buy and sell. Lu Gu had a father who didn’t love him and only a stepmother, so no one would trouble themselves for him.

After bringing back Lu Gu, Shen Xuanqing only asked someone to inquire about Li Mingshan, and he didn’t take any other actions. He was unable to deal with the town’s people by himself; he had to spend money to invite people to drink and eat, because he had to pay off his debt, so this matter was put on hold.

After being with Lu Gu for a long time, he felt better. He closed the door to earn money to live. What was Lu Wen? But since Lu Wen had threatened them first, he wouldn’t be polite.

The rice had to dry for two days before threshing. Before getting busy, Shen Xuanqing went to town again the day after dining at the restaurant.

The brothel was located on West Street, and at night the lights were always bright. People came and went during the day, but it wasn’t as lively at night.

Shen Xuanqing didn’t enter the brothel; he turned into the alley behind the brothel. This alley was narrower, and the door looked a little more messy and dilapidated than other places. He stopped in front of a black lacquered door, diagonally opposite to the brothel’s backyard.

After knocking on the door a few times, he heard a man’s rough voice from inside, asking who was outside the door. Shen Xuanqing spoke to him, and soon the door opened, and a slovenly man came out; he had just risen from bed, his clothes not well dressed, his hair in a mess.

“I said, who is it? It’s you, come in quickly.” Luo Biao greeted him, catching the wine jar Shen Xuanqing threw toward him with both hands, opened it and smelled it, then smiled: “This is good wine.”

Shen Xuanqing also carried an oil-paper bag in his hand; when he opened it, there was a roast chicken. Luo Biao rolled up the bedding, moved the table in the middle of the kang, and the two sat on the kang to eat and drink.

The strong wine was spicy; Luo Biao took a swig, set the cup down, and sighed, “Good wine!” He asked Shen Xuanqing how his family was and how his business was going, and while drinking, he tossed out a few bits of gossip. After four or five cups of wine and meat, he was full of joy. Luo Biao said: “Second Brother Shen, if you have anything to say, just ask; brother will never refuse.” He couldn’t even eat properly; his mother was dying of illness, his father had died, and he couldn’t even buy a coffin. He had dug a hole with a broken mat and buried him. He took his old mother and knelt on the street to sell himself to save her, but the remedy would cost a lot of money, except for the rich and benevolent people who wanted to hire a long-term handyman and bring back a sick burden.

Shen Xuanqing had passed through town to work as a coolie. Seeing that his old mother was dying and crying out for food, he had stopped and hesitated, giving Luo Biao two cold steamed buns in his arms. This was the ration Wei Lanxiang had brought for him for a day. He gave them and was about to leave. Luo Biao stopped him and asked his name. He shook his head and didn’t say much. Luo Biao turned around and broke a piece of cold steamed buns to feed his old mother. Shen Xuanqing didn’t see why he carried his old mother on his back to bury her. He had to catch up with the merchants when he went to work carrying the big bags.

Hearing this, Shen Xuanqing was not polite to him, and said: “Do you still remember Li Mingshan I asked you to inquire about?” Luo Biao thought about it for a moment before saying: “It’s that grandson. I saw him in the building a few days ago. Don’t look at him like a man on the outside; his eyes when he’s staring at the girl in the building are straight, but his old lady is so strict that he can only sneak around if he’s greedy, and dare not come here often. You haven’t seen his dirty face; as soon as he enters the room, he takes off his pants and arches toward them, and his legs tremble when he’s in a hurry, and Sister Yao laughs behind his back.”
