Chapter 62 Part 13


After a while, they talked about the soles of Shen Xuanqing’s shoes. Wei Lanxiang talked about her son even more. She said that Shen Xuanqing had worn shoes since he was little; he ran around all the time, and the other girls his age, as well as little Shuang’er, avoided him and dismissed him for being so wild. The new shoes he had just put on were dirty and torn within two days. Even now Wei Lanxiang couldn’t understand how he managed to walk back then. The shoes wore out in two days. Even Shen Yaoqing didn’t waste shoes so much—she was angry, thought it was funny, and even grabbed Shen Xuanqing’s ears. Not only that, Shen Xuanqing was so strong that when she was ten years old, she couldn’t hold him.

“At that time, Da Chen was a rascal too. He dragged Erqing out to play all day long. He would fill weasel holes, or fish and poke honeycombs. When they played, he didn’t care about anything. He was wild. I wouldn’t let Erqing go; he’d run off in a hurry, and I’d let him go rather than hold him back.”

Since arriving at Shen’s house, Lu Gu had seen Shen Xuanqing as a calm, steady person; he never would have guessed how different he had been as a child. He kept listening and forgot about chopping the wood.

“The two of them went to play in the quagmire near Heqian Village. When they came back, they were covered in mud and dirt. They smeared mud on their faces and heads, their hair stuck with mud, and they couldn’t wash it off. I was so angry that I beat him with a rolling pin. Da Chen dragged Erqing to his house to look for his mother—you don’t know, Da Chen was beaten up that day and howled like ghosts and wolves. After that lesson, he was honest for the next half year.”

Wei Lanxiang spoke of Shen Xuanqing with a smile that showed how much she had endured and how much she still cared for those two rascals.

Lu Gu couldn’t help but smile slightly as he listened, then Shen Yan drove the ducks out from the backyard after they had chopped the chicken grass and led them to swim outside. The ducks quacked, and the puppy stood up and barked at them.

At this time, Ji Qiuyue took a sewing basket and sat next to Wei Lanxiang, intending to mend Shen Yaoqing’s torn old clothes, but when she was distracted, she turned toward Ji Qiuyue and said something.

Lu Gu began to chop firewood again, still thinking about Wei Lanxiang’s words, and a slight smile crept onto his face.

In the evening, Lu Gu and Ji Qiuyue walked around the paddy fields and the dry land. When they returned, he kept glancing toward the road to the town. The puppy ran back and forth with them. The villagers said the dog was really strong.

The sun was sinking, and Shen Xuanqing’s figure hadn’t appeared; Lu Gu thought he wouldn’t be back tonight. Ji Qiuyue teased him with a smile, “Why, you miss Erqing after just one day away?”

Lu Gu reddened and mumbled, “No, no.” Seeing his blush, Ji Qiuyue smiled like a flower, but she knew he was thin-skinned, so she didn’t press him.

The two of them strolled through the village, meeting people along the way. Ji Qiuyue’s talk was lively; she could chat and laugh with everyone, and they finally arrived home.

As soon as Lu Gu entered the courtyard, he saw a young woman talking to Wei Lanxiang. Wei Lanxiang handed her a bowl of white flour. “Go back quickly, and repay us when you have it tomorrow,” Wei Lanxiang said.

“Thank you, Aunt Lanxiang,” the young woman replied, then turned and left. After she turned around, Lu Gu saw bruises on her face, as if she had been beaten, and he was a little startled—having been beaten himself, he knew how painful it could be. The woman seemed familiar with Ji Qiuyue; she gave a smile and said something before leaving.

“Old Zhang’s family really did commit crimes, and she beat the good daughter-in-law.” Wei Lanxiang sighed after the woman left.

Lu Gu often went up the mountain with Shen Xuanqing, so he wasn’t fully familiar with village affairs. But he had seen that woman twice—she was Zhang Zhengzi’s daughter-in-law, living a few houses away. Zhang Zhengzi’s old lady—everyone in the village called her Old Zhang—was known for her strict rules.

“That’s it,” Ji Qiuyue replied. She felt lucky. If her mother hadn’t found Shen Yaoqing and Wei Lanxiang for her back then, she was afraid she would have died of anger. Old Zhang’s rules were severe: the daughter-in-law ate in the room, and the daughter-in-law brought the meals in without a word; she had to kneel to greet; she would be beaten and scolded if Old Zhang didn’t like something.

It wasn’t uncommon to serve the parents-in-law this way. Some families did require kneeling to greet, though few were as strict as Old Zhang. Everyone in the farming households worked hard; spare time for the daughter-in-law was hard to come by.

Others in the village didn’t say that Wei Lanxiang had ever suffered such anger from her mother-in-law. Although Shen Shunfu was a rough man, he treated Wei Lanxiang well and protected her, so she treated Ji Qiuyue well too. After Lu Gu came, he hadn’t beaten or scolded him, and the rules hadn’t been formally established. Besides, she had Shen Yan, and she worried that her little daughter would be tortured by someone else in the future, which made her uncomfortable.

Not to mention others’ daughters-in-law, Ji Qiuyue was loved by her parents, and even Lu Gu was loved by his mother when he was little. How could she treat others harshly when they came to their home? Ji Qiuyue was in the kitchen boiling water for the family to wash. She said there wasn’t much firewood left, so Lu Gu went inside with the firewood and listened to her talk about the Zhang family. As he gradually grew accustomed to the Shen family, Lu Gu seldom thought about the days when he had been beaten. Hearing this, he felt a mix of fear and gratitude—glad he had met Wei Lanxiang and the Shen family.
