Chapter 89

Laba

Laba porridge was cooked in large quantities and would last for days. Snow covered everything; the twelfth lunar month and the cold winter could not dim the joy of the approaching New Year. Lu Gu was busy all winter, cooking, doing laundry, sewing, and reading, and Shen Xuanqing still wanted to teach him boxing. Now he could wash clothes with hot water, and tang pozi warmed his hands and feet when he did needlework or writing, and there were no chilblains anymore.

“Okay, that’s enough. There’s no need to resize it,” Wei Lanxiang said as she adjusted Shen Yan’s new clothes. Seeing how lovely she looked in them, she smiled. “Take it off for the moment and wear it again during the New Year.”

“Mother, let’s go to the big market tomorrow. Can I buy some hairbands and silk flowers?” Shen Yan asked as she began to take off the clothes.

“Okay, I’ll buy them for you.” Wei Lanxiang agreed, then turned to Lu Gu. “I’ll buy them for you too.”

Lu Gu had tried on his new clothes; they fit well and had already been folded and put away. Hearing Wei Lanxiang’s remark, his eyes curved in a slight smile, and he nodded: “Yeah.”

Shen Xuanqing had bought him a hairpin and a bracelet, but Wei Lanxiang’s generosity shone even brighter—yet how could Shen Xuanqing resist this good intention? Moreover, during the New Year, no matter how many ornaments a woman wore on her head, they would wear some colorful silk flowers. They were bright and beautiful, and sometimes even men wore them in their hair, making everyone look festive and lively.

“When Erqing comes back, let him try on his new clothes, and if they don’t fit, we’ll resize them.” Wei Lanxiang said.

“Understood, mother. I’ll resize them for him later.” Lu Gu nodded. Wei Lanxiang always stayed at home when making New Year’s clothes, and he had learned a few things from her. Resizing clothes wasn’t too difficult, and he should learn how to do it.

While they talked, Shen Xuanqing returned from outside and had a chat with Ji Qiuyue. After Shen Yan changed, Lu Gu picked up the two outfits and walked out with them.

“Mother, the third uncle said he will lend us his ox cart the day after tomorrow. We’ll just use his family’s cart. It’s ready-made.” Shen Xuanqing said.

“Well, that’s easy.” Wei Lanxiang nodded, then turned to Ji Qiuyue. “Go to Liangzhang Bridge the day after tomorrow. Do you want to go too?”

Ji Qiuyue answered with a smile: “Naturally. I’m going to take in the bustle of the big fair, and I’ll join in.”

Since she had become pregnant, Shen Yaoqing had watched her closely and wouldn’t let her go out because of the cold and slippery roads. She stayed at home in the village most of the time, but the big fair was too lively to miss.

Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing went back to their room to try on their clothes. Shen Xuanqing was tall, and even if he wanted to help Lu Gu undress, it wouldn’t be convenient, so he stood to the side and watched.

“It’s pretty good.” Shen Xuanqing stretched his arms and legs after putting on the clothes; he wasn’t particular about dress as long as it was warm and clean.

Lu Gu helped smooth the fabric along the back, then stood a few steps away and looked over. Shen Xuanqing’s broad shoulders, narrow waist, and tall, strong body made even plain clothes look imposing or stylish, and everything fit just right.

“Then there’s no need to resize it.” Lu Gu said after a moment.

“Is yours done too?” Shen Xuanqing asked, taking off his new clothes.

Lu Gu smiled softly and replied, “Yes, I’ve tried them all.”

His smile lit his eyes like stars, and his cheeks were bright and fair, which made Shen Xuanqing’s heart flutter. He reached out, touched Lu Gu’s face for a moment, then bent his head and whispered a few words in his ear.

Lu Gu was shy and happy at once; every time Shen Xuanqing came close, it put him at ease. Earlier in the day, Shen Xuanqing’s words sounded earnest in his ear, but then, seizing the moment, he pressed a kiss to the side of Lu Gu’s neck, which was somewhat bold.

The big fair at Liangzhang Bridge lasted several days. Today was the day the second room of Shen’s family went to the fair. Once the gate was shut behind them, the six members of the family headed out, leaving only the dogs at home to guard against thieves. It was almost New Year, and they still had hope for the chickens and ducks they kept.

Shen Shunwang got the ox cart ready early in the morning, and when they arrived, they quickly boarded it.

“Third Uncle, let’s go first,” Shen Xuanqing said, holding the ox rope.

Shen Shunwang reached for the cigarette pouch at his waist, and after hearing the words, he nodded: “Go ahead, second sister-in-law. Take your time on the road.”

“Oh, I got it.” Wei Lanxiang smiled.

Although the ox cart moved slowly, the ox was powerful enough to pull loads, so there was no need to walk to Liangzhang Bridge with bamboo baskets on their backs. Shen Shunwang’s ox was at the prime of its strength, and fortunately its temperament was gentle; it recognized the two brothers—Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing—and walked forward obediently.

As they set out, the sun was often hidden behind clouds and the wind blew from time to time, so they all wore badger fur collars. The collars were light yet warm, making the cold wind less biting.

To look after Ji Qiuyue, they walked at a leisurely pace and exchanged a few words with villagers they met along the way. Grandma Ajin appeared in her yard and hurried out to ask, “Lanxiang, are you going to the fair?”

“Yes,” Wei Lanxiang replied with a smile.

Grandma Ajin pressed, “Then you bring me a bag of zaotang back. Xiao Shunzi is a glutton—he kept eating zaotang and there’s only a little left.”

Wei Lanxiang promised, “Okay, I’ll call you at the door when I come back.”

Grandma Ajin laughed gently. “That’ll be fine.” She didn’t have to walk far to buy zaotang anyway.

The ox cart creaked as it rolled out of the village. After a while, the ground grew muddier under their feet. Shen Xuanqing urged the ox to slow down and said, “If you’re tired, climb into the cart.”

The journey to Liangzhang Bridge was long; Shen Yaoqing helped Ji Qiuyue into the ox cart. Wei Lanxiang preferred to walk and didn’t climb aboard until she could no longer stand. Lu Gu and Shen Yan rode with the others, while the two brothers led the ox and held the cart, walking happily.

Shen Yaoqing felt buoyant; the sky was wide and distant. He raised his voice and sang a folk song that others could not quite understand. The tune was thick and lingering, straight from the heart, and listening to it was a different kind of pleasure.
