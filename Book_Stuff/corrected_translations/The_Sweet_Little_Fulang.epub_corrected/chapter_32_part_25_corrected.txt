Chapter 32 Part 25


The autumn vegetables planted in the front yard were no longer wilting, and they were watered every two days to keep them plump. Tonight’s meal was excellent—a simple stir-fry with mushrooms, mixed with watercress and purple belly; the dishes and rice wine were already prepared in the pot, still warm when brought out.

When Shen Xuanqing came back in the evening, Lu Gu had already cooked. He was washing his hands in front of the kitchen door as Lu Gu appeared with a bowl of small fresh crabs he had caught from the mountain. Shen Xuanqing smiled. “There was this. Did you run far today?”

“I just caught them,” Lu Gu replied in a low voice, “and there are still some in the bucket.” The harvest had been unusually good today, and happiness bubbled up in his chest. He hesitated, then added, “There are more—for example, I found sweet buns and watercress. The pickle jar is empty, so I’ve pickled some sour cress to eat.”

As soon as Shen Xuanqing stepped inside, he saw the washed watercress laid on the bamboo plaque. He nodded and smiled. “Okay, I’ll listen to you.”

For some reason, that remark made Lu Gu feel a bit of unspeakable complexity—like he could decide certain things, but he was cautious, and he didn’t know how to ask.

Because sweet buns were hard to come by, when Lu Gu put away the bowl and went to the kitchen to fetch steamed buns, he noticed the washed red fruit in the basket. He lowered his head and handed two of them to Shen Xuanqing, whispering, “Here you are.”

Shen Xuanqing saw the fruit in his hand and was momentarily stunned, then took them with a smile; something melted in his heart—he felt warmth.

The watercress looked fresh and tender, the fried small crabs were spicy and numb, and with the fried vegetables with mushrooms and rice wine, both of them felt full and refreshed.

Dusk fell, and Shen Xuanqing was about to close the courtyard door after feeding the dogs when Dahui, who was eating, suddenly raised his head and barked outside. The two small dogs joined in, and Lu Gu, who was washing dishes in the kitchen, was startled by the sound; after living here so long, he had never heard the dogs bark like this at night.

Fortunately, it wasn’t some wild beast. A figure stepped out from the woods in front of the courtyard, and Shen Xuanqing recognized him carefully.

“Amitabha, Benefactor Shen. I went to the mountain to collect herbs, but I couldn’t make it down the mountain at night, so I came to disturb your quiet again.” Huitong stood before the courtyard and did not rush in.

Shen Xuanqing saluted him and said, “Master Huitong, please come in.”

“Thank you, Benefactor Shen.” The monk Huitong entered.

Seeing Shen Xuanqing about to lift the gate post, Huitong helped to move the thick tree trunk before the bamboo basket was unloaded, for though he was not as tall as Shen Xuanqing, he still had some strength, being a former monk.

He turned around and saw Lu Gu, chanted a Buddhist name, and asked, “I wonder what the honorable name of this benefactor is?”

“My fulang’s surname is Lu and his name is Lu Gu,” Shen Xuanqing smiled.

“It turned out to be the benefactor Lu, respectfully. The poor monk Huitong is a monk of Jingxin Temple.” Huitong gave the Buddhist salute again.

Lu Gu quickly folded his palms and said, “Master Huitong.”

He had met the monk twice, but had never spoken to him, and thus was understandably nervous.

Shen Xuanqing was familiar with Huitong. When the other party went up the mountain to collect medicines and could not come down, he would stay the night and leave some medicinal materials when he departed. He asked, “Master, have you eaten? There are vegetarian dishes here, and the oil is vegetarian oil.”

“That’s it, thank you very much.” Then Huitong took a pack of dry food from the bamboo basket and, without needing help, went to the kitchen to heat water and the dry food.

Shen Xuanqing grabbed a large handful of watercress from the bamboo plaque and brought it in. Huitong knew how to cook vegetables; after a day spent running in the mountains, it was always good to have hot vegetables and rice.

Seeing two bowls of red sweet bun fruits on the chopping board, Lu Gu picked quite a lot. Shen Xuanqing took only a smaller bowl and said, “Master can take these fruits to eat.”

“Thank you.” Huitong, who was tending the fire, nodded his thanks without ceremony.

Lu Gu had just driven the chickens and ducks in from the backyard. Shen Xuanqing carried the bowl of sweet bun fruits to the main room and said, “I’ll let Master Huitong eat this bowl; if you want to eat later, I’ll prepare another for you when I come back.”

This batch had been brought back by Lu Gu after a long journey, and he was eager to share it with the monk as soon as he returned. How could he spoil this intention, so he had to explain.

And the monk Huitong, who used to help the old hunter as a langzhong and provide medicinal materials, did not take any money. It was the same for the poor, and such generosity showed his good heart.

“It’s fine. We can’t finish it anyway, so giving some away is fine.” Lu Gu said. The sweet bun fruits would go bad if kept too long after they ripened; the yellow, astringent fruit could be eaten after two days of ripening.

Hiding a smile, Shen Xuanqing knew that his fulang was a rare good fulang in the world. To reassure Lu Gu, he also told about Huitong’s medical treatment of the old hunter.

Lu Gu felt that he should give more food to such a good monk; how could he be stingy with this little gesture?

As night fell, and the extra monk Huitong settled in for the night, Shen Xuanqing laid a mattress in the east room. He lit the oil lamp and, by its light, took out the items in the bamboo basket. The stupid rabbits that had been caught were already confined in a cage in the woodshed. There were chestnuts wrapped in large leaves in the bamboo basket, harvested earlier, and they would taste even better once the sun came out tomorrow.

He peeled some for Lu Gu to eat; no one had peeled chestnuts for him except his mother, who had once given him a sweet chestnut. He lifted his head, watching Shen Xuanqing peel chestnuts by candlelight, with his eyebrows drooping and his eyes lowered.
