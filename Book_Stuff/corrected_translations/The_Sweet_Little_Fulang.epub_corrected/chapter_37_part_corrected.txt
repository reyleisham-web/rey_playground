Chapter 37


On the way to the mountain, they met several villagers carrying bamboo baskets on their backs, making their way toward the hillside to pick chestnuts. When they reached the forest, the crowd thinned and they couldn’t see the others anymore. Sometimes they could hear a parent calling after the wild boy to stop messing around, a faint sound of running in the distance.

Shen Xuanqing followed along. Originally Wei Lanxiang had thought about him walking up the mountain road yesterday and didn’t want him along. She had said that if something happened, or if Shen Yan and Lu Gu couldn’t carry everything, they would have to call him.

Lu Gu had been tired yesterday, and Wei Lanxiang didn’t let him carry the bamboo basket today; he took it up with Shen Yan and left it in Shen Yan’s basket. Even so, it might not fill it up, since people from several villages had gone up the mountain to gather chestnuts.

When days were better, they didn’t have to hurry to pick them before others who came up the mountain. The five of them could walk more slowly. They found a cluster of edible mushrooms, quickly picked them, and put them into a bamboo basket.

Shen Yan was a girl and young. She didn’t dare go into the mountains by herself without an adult accompanying her. At most, she dug wild vegetables and gathered mushrooms on the hillside not far from her home, but people often passed by there. There were wild greens, but not many mushrooms, so when she picked a bunch at this moment, she was naturally happy.

Lu Gu had never been to the Qingxi Village mountains before, but the Shen family often came here and knew where the chestnut trees were, so he simply followed along. Usually he was accustomed to carrying the bamboo basket on his back, but today there was nothing on his back, which made him feel much lighter.

Their house was the closest to the mountain, and they set out quite early. When they arrived at the chestnut forest, there were two figures in the distance. They were from the same village. Wei Lanxiang greeted them through the trees, and the other party smiled back before continuing to gather.

Ripe chestnuts fell to the ground; Lu Gu bent down to pick up a few and had to search under the fallen leaves in the grass. Someone else had picked them here, but there weren’t many; they walked deeper into the woods while collecting.

While gathering, Lu Gu moved to Shen Yan’s side, unable to hold all the chestnuts in his hands. He looked up and saw Shen Xuanqing picking up chestnuts beside him. He tucked the chestnuts that he couldn’t hold into Shen Xuanqing’s clothes, which made Lu Gu feel something he couldn’t put into words. He didn’t think about it and focused on picking chestnuts.

Shen Yan came over with a bamboo basket. Shen Xuanqing dumped the chestnuts from his clothes into it and walked forward with Lu Gu. There weren’t many chestnuts here; he remembered there were plenty deeper in the forest that day, but he was busy hunting and had only picked a little for Lu Gu to eat.

As they passed a water bun tree, it was covered with red fruits, each fruit larger than an adult’s fingertip and just big enough to pop into the mouth. Shen Yan pointed to the tree and said to Lu Gu, “Brother Guzi, these are bitter buns; you can’t eat them.”

“Yeah.” Lu Gu nodded, thinking of the sweet buns he had found and wishing to bring some for her. “I found the sweet ones in the mountains, and I wanted to bring you some. But I was in a hurry to come down yesterday, so I didn’t have time to pick them.”

“Then you bring me some next time.” Shen Yan wasn’t polite about it; sweet buns were scarce, and when she heard Lu Gu had found them, she was eager to eat. She said, “Look and see if my mother will go to the mountains to pick up chestnuts in two days. If she goes, I’ll go with you too. You just tell me where the sweet buns are, and I’ll pick them back for us by myself. I’ll pick a lot of them. Well, I still want to bring them back to brother Daqing to eat.”

When Lu Gu heard her say this, it seemed as if she had found the sweet buns and wanted to pick a lot of them back. Those childish yet earnest words caused him to smile faintly and he nodded in agreement.

“Shen Yan, come quickly, there are raspberries to eat,” Ji Qiuyue shouted from the front, as she moved toward the side of the chestnut forest. A dwarf raspberry plant stood there, its small fruits already turning red.

As soon as Shen Yan heard this, she hurried over. The three dwarf raspberry plants showed signs of having been picked; the remaining green fruits would ripen again. There were only about a dozen fruits in total. She picked one and tasted it—sweet and sour, a berry small enough to fit in the mouth.

She divided the picked fruits; Shen Xuanqing and Wei Lanxiang didn’t want any, Ji Qiuyue tasted two, Lu Gu didn’t eat much, and he gave the rest to Shen Yan.

The five of them wandered the mountain all morning and didn’t return home until noon, when it was time to eat. Shen Yan carried half a basket of chestnuts, and Wei Lanxiang and Ji Qiuyue each had about the same amount in their bamboo baskets. Not many, but they also picked some mushrooms. With the wild fruit, they fried the mushrooms when they returned and ate them while they were still fresh.

Shen Yan carried a bunch of mountain flowers in her hand and walked beside Lu Gu, saying, “Brother Guzi, I went to town and saw someone selling flowers on the street. I could sell these if I picked them. Our place is a bit far from town, so we have to go there in the morning; if they dry out by noon, we won’t be able to sell them.”

Lu Gu nodded in agreement. When he went to town with Shen Xuanqing, he had also seen someone selling flowers—the fresh, pretty ones were expensive. He followed Shen Xuanqing into the mountains; if he saw beautiful mountain flowers, he could pick them and bring them back, but they would die on the way. It wasn’t as cost-effective as dried wolfberries and soapberries.

As Shen Yan spoke, her heart woke with excitement. The next time their mother took them to town to sell duck eggs, she would pick some flowers and plant them in the yard; when the time came, perhaps she could sell them for a few copper coins too.
