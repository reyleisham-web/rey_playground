Chapter 31 Part 25


After the sun came out, the quiet mountain forest was no longer as scary as it was at night. Whenever he came to a patch of dense grass, Lu Gu used the long wooden stick in his hand to poke through it and clear a path, with Dahui following closely behind. Following the direction where he had picked mushrooms the last time, he truly found a mountain stream. The stream flowed down the mountain. The water looked incredibly clear, even clearer than the Qingxi River. Tender green watercress grew along the bank. Lu Gu pinched one and felt it fresh and tender. After a bite, he decided to pick more and wait for Shen Xuanqing to come back to eat it at night.

This watercress was more tender than the ones he had picked by the village river before. He thought it would be delicious in salads. It could also be stir-fried or fried with meat. There weren't many pickles left in the jar. The watercress could be marinated in vinegar for a day or two. You could eat it sour and crunchy, and you could eat it with sour pickles; otherwise it would be too salty. Fortunately, he came out today with a big bamboo basket on his back, so he could bring some back. He wasn’t in a hurry, there was no one else around, so he took off his shoes and socks, rolled up his trousers, and stepped into the water. Since he came here specifically to look for snails, he naturally wouldn’t go back too early. Lu Gu brought himself four steamed buns made with mixed flour and put them in a cloth bag. Now that he was in the water, he tied the cloth bag to the back rope of the bamboo basket.

There were quite a lot of stones in the stream; he pried at several and finally spotted four green snails with thin shells. They were fairly large, but the shells were too fragile and thin; some were not mature yet and would break if squeezed hard. Lu Gu’s hands were light, and he didn’t want to crush them, so he touched them and slid them gently into the bamboo basket behind his back. He walked along the stream toward the area with more rocks above, turning over every rock he could and examining them very carefully.

He even found a small crab, called purple belly in the mountains, which lived under stones or hid in the muddy water. It earned its name from the purple color on its belly. He didn’t know what people elsewhere called it. If it was like in their countryside, it would be regarded as a kind of expensive mountain delicacy. When he was in Anjia Village, he had seen someone go up the mountain to catch some; that person didn’t sell them, but took them home to fry for himself, and after eating, he boasted in the village for a while, claiming he was eating like the rich men in town. This critter could be fried with peppercorns. Since it was for eating, Lu Gu wouldn’t let it go easily, and he caught both the purple belly and the green snails together.

When he saw the purple belly, he remembered that this thing was as valuable as green snails, but this deep mountain wasn’t as good as when he was at the foot of the mountain, where he could go to town to sell what he caught. Shen Xuanqing was better and moved faster; he could reach town in an hour and a half. But he also thought that snails and crabs, once caught, were like loaches: they needed to be kept for a few days to spit out mud, and they could survive for several days in water. If you caught some and kept them in the water for two days, you could take them back with wooden barrels, and they were easier to carry than fish. Small crabs could live outside water for a while. He planned to soak the green snails in wine and keep only the snail meat, which would be even lighter. Thinking of this, Lu Gu grew eager. He wanted to find more. Shen Xuanqing and his family wouldn’t finish eating all of it, and they could even sell the catches in town for money.

Fearing that the small crabs would be crushed or the green snails eaten, he picked a few large leaves to wrap the green snails. After gathering plenty of leaves, he wrapped the snails with slender, sturdy grass stems and tossed them into the bamboo basket. No need to be afraid. The purple belly used its sharp legs to move about in the bamboo basket. Sometimes it seemed they were climbing up. Lu Gu patted the bamboo basket with the back of his hand a few times, and the little crab that had just climbed a little bit fell back to the bottom of the basket. There was a splash of water when Lu Gu scooped a purple belly from the muddy water, held it under the clear water, and washed it with his hands, and it cleaned quickly.

The stream was muddy from his movements and washing, but it cleared up again not long after he left. Dahui waded in the water for a while, then returned to shore, fluttering his body and flicking his fur, splashing water on Lu Gu. He looked up at Dahui with a slight smile in his eyes—having a dog around was truly reassuring; even if you went far, you wouldn’t feel you couldn’t find your way back.

The mountain stream stretched quite a long way. He searched along the stream for a long time, probing both upstream and downstream. When he grew hungry, he looked up at the sun high above his head. It was already noon, so he went to the bank and sat down, resting on a big rock. He slipped off his shoes and socks as he walked so he wouldn’t lose them. Once his feet were dry, he put them back on. By the grass beside him, he spotted edible red fruits and picked them all, squatting by the stream to wash them, and tasted one sweet fruit, which was a bit small.

Seeing Dahui approach, he tried to feed two to him, but Dahui unexpectedly ate them. It turned out that dogs also liked to eat fruit. Not only the grass red fruit, but just now when he waded through the water, he noticed a water bun tree. The fruits on it were all red and quite large, but he didn’t know whether they were bitter buns or sweet buns. This kind of wild fruit was small and juicy, and when ripe it would burst open like a water bun, so it was called the water bun tree. Lu Gu walked over and tiptoed to pluck a small red fruit, bit the skin lightly, and felt the sweetness of the juice. His eyes lit up with joy; it was a sweet bun.

There were more bitter buns than sweet ones; eight out of ten trees were bitter, the kind of bitterness even adults could hardly stand, let alone eat. Just biting the skin and letting the juice touch the tip of his tongue left it bitter and numb, and anyone unused to the bitter taste would retch, not to mention how uncomfortable it was. So after savoring the sweetness, Lu Gu was pleasantly surprised. He picked some red ones and took them to the stream to wash. The redder the fruit, the riper it was. The flesh inside was soft and full of juice. He popped one into his mouth in a single bite, and the sweet juice overflowed.

Seeing Dahui beside him, he broke open a sweet bun and handed it over. Dahui sniffed it, opened his mouth, and ate it, even licking the sweet juice from Lu Gu’s fingers. The two of them shared and enjoyed the sweet buns Lu Gu had picked.

Lu Gu gnawed two and a half of the four steamed buns, and handed the remaining one and a half to Dahui. Dahui spent today with Lu Gu and didn’t have time to catch bamboo rats or chase rabbits, so he shouldn’t go hungry. After eating, he found a clear spot, cupped a few sips of water with his hands, and Dahui followed to drink with his head lowered. He looked almost human, so intelligent.
