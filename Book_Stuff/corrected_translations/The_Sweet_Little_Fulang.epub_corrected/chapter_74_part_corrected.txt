Chapter 74


After the orange peels were squeezed, the fragrance was more pronounced and felt refreshing.

Lu Gu had only heard of oranges before; he had never eaten one. When Shen Xuanqing teased him, he grew a little embarrassed, lowered his eyes, and pretended to peel the oranges to ease the awkwardness.

Shen Xuanqing smiled, knowing his fulang was shy by nature. After a moment of teasing, he quickly took two pieces of orange and handed them to Lu Gu.

With a cool piece of orange near his lips, Lu Gu raised his eyes to Shen Xuanqing. Under the gaze of those beautiful starry eyes, his ears reddened, and he opened his mouth to bite.

“Eat them first, then bite into them.” Shen Xuanqing fed two pieces of orange to him.

Lu Gu did as he was told, and after biting into them, the sweet-sour juice of the orange surged in his mouth like a clear mountain spring overflowing. It turned out that oranges were delicious.

He looked up at Shen Xuanqing, his surprise evident without a word.

Shen Xuanqing also took a piece of orange. The sourness came with the first bite, and sweetness followed soon after. The orange was a bit cold in the chilly weather, but tasted wonderful.

Seeing Lu Gu’s surprised happiness, he smiled again and gave Lu Gu the rest of the orange. After a moment of hesitation, he drew another item wrapped in red cloth from his bosom.

“For you.” Shen Xuanqing seemed a little embarrassed, his lower lip slightly pressed, yet the smile in his eyes did not fade.

Lu Gu held the oranges in both hands, first placing them on the chopping board, then carefully unwrapping the red cloth. Inside lay a brand-new silver hairpin, carved with rice ears and grain leaves at the end.

[Lu Gu, 谷 gǔ: grain;]

The rice leaves were clearly defined, and the grains on the ears were etched with meticulous realism. There were two ears of rice: one straight, the other bent downward.

Shen Xuanqing fell in love with this hairpin the moment he saw it at Jinyinxuan. The grain ears were carved so realistically and carefully that it was fairly expensive, but he still bought it. He didn’t know why, but when he saw the hairpin, he felt happy. He tucked it under his arm and hurried back, forgetting to buy the youzhong ladle, and only remembered it when he got home.

Lu Gu had never seen such an expensive silver hairpin, let alone worn it. When he got married, he had only a wooden hairpin in his hair.

“For me?” He looked up at Shen Xuanqing in a low voice, eyes full of expectancy and a touch of apprehension.

“Of course, it’s for you.” Shen Xuanqing nodded.

Ear of grain is grain. When he was a child, his mother had told him this while embroidering ear-of-grain patterns on his sleeves and collar.

[谷穗 gǔ suì: ear of grain; 谷子 gǔ zi: grain]

Lu Gu pressed the silver hairpin tightly in his hand, his eyes growing softly moist.

Shen Xuanqing didn’t know what was wrong with him. He scratched his head, a little at a loss, and lowered his voice: “Do you not like it?”

No one liked a crybaby. Lu Gu blinked his eyes twice, forcing back the dampness, and shook his head. “No, I like it very much.”

He smiled again, the smile small but bright. Shen Xuanqing, tall and looming in front of him, blocked the view of others.

“That’s good,” Shen Xuanqing laughed. The fire still burned at the bottom of the stove; Shen Yan went to eat oranges and said, “I’ll light the fire.”

“Yeah.” Lu Gu wrapped the silver hairpin in the red cloth and tucked it into his bosom close to his body. The smile never left his face all day.

The sun was cloudy, but there was luckily no snow or wind. Early winter had arrived, and everyone in the village was preparing to stock up on food and firewood for the cold months. Every family had prepared plenty of rice, noodles, pickles, cabbages, Chinese cabbages, and radishes. In recent days, many people had been chopping firewood on the mountain.

The people at the foot of the mountain were farther from the town, but winter made cutting firewood relatively convenient. There were trees everywhere, and the mountain’s forest was fairly dense. If you cut it this year, it would still grow next year, and new trees would sprout in two or three years.

The Shen family’s second room was no exception. Cutting wood was usually done by men, but Lu Gu and the others could help carry the wood back as well. Ji Qiuyue stayed at home to cook and boil water, so they could eat and drink when hungry or thirsty.

Two bundles of firewood were tied with hemp rope, hung on a sturdy long branch. Lu Gu picked them up and carried them into the courtyard.

With a loud “Wow,” a puppy sprang from the nearby forest. He didn’t seem to remember how wild he had been in the forest—dead leaves clung to his fur as he ran. Lu Gu didn’t mind; as long as he didn’t run far away, he was used to roaming the mountains before, so being a little unrestrained now felt natural.

The mountain path hardly resembled a road. Trees and stones blocked the way, and the journey was rough. Ordinary people would rather haul bamboo-basket-laden loads than pull a cart, as it was extremely laborious.

In the past two days, Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing had been chopping with long axes and machetes; there had been no time to pull a cart, and the three women—Shuang’er and the children—had no use for a cart.

Shen Yan was young and still carried a bundle of firewood. Their house sat closest to the mountain, and the front hill wasn’t far away, so the woodshed filled bit by bit as they came back and forth. Of course, what Lu Gu and the others gathered were mostly thin branches, with thicker trunks piled together. The trees cut down were often thicker than a bowl’s mouth. Even so, cutting wood on the mountain with a saw was troublesome. When they were done cutting enough, they would take a bundle of hemp rope and, if a bundle was too heavy, tie fewer together and recruit a few village men to help carry it back.

Every family in the village had to prepare firewood.

Today, Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing would help the Shen family carry the wood. It was a mutual favor.

As soon as Lu Gu entered the courtyard, Ji Qiuyue set down the needle and thread in her hand and asked, “Is Guzi thirsty? This honey water is warm enough to drink.”

“Go and put the firewood down; I’ll fetch it for you.” She didn’t wait for Lu Gu’s answer and hurried to the kitchen.

“Okay.” Lu Gu trailed behind her and went to the woodshed to untie the two bundles of firewood and stack them neatly with the others.

Ji Qiuyue took a bowl and came to the woodshed. She looked inside and said, “There’s quite a lot. We’ll be able to rest after another day and a half.”

The more firewood there was, the warmer the winter would be. Seeing so much firewood, Lu Gu felt a sense of ease in his heart.

He nodded. “Well, soon.”
