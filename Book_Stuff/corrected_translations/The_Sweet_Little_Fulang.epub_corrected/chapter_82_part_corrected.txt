Chapter 82


There were eleven steamed buns left in the drawer from before, and with so many people in the family, seven or eight would be enough for breakfast. Wei Lanxiang woke up early and saw the buns; she suggested frying them instead. They hadn’t eaten fried buns in years. They had fried pancakes once before, but this was different.

Frying buns required pouring a lot of oil into the pan. Lu Gu had never done it before. He helped Wei Lanxiang cut the buns into slices. Outside, he lit the clay stove and used clay pots to boil hot water for drinking. Because this stove had been used for decoctions, they used to call it a medicine stove (药炉). But a few days ago Wei Lanxiang thought that name wasn’t auspicious to hear all the time. Since Ji Qiuyue was pregnant, they started calling it the clay stove.

“Mother, is this enough?” He poured in oil and asked, uncertain how much she wanted.

Wei Lanxiang looked over. “Two more spoonfuls. If there isn’t enough oil, the buns will burn easily. After frying, you can pour back the leftover oil when it cools.”

“Okay.” Lu Gu added two more spoonfuls as she instructed. He heated the oil until it was hot. When frying, the pan rattled with sound. He cut the eleven old buns into slices and fried them in the pan. After they were fried, he dropped them into a soup basin to drain; otherwise the bowl wouldn’t fit.

Breakfast that day was different. After the frying finished, no one shouted for everyone to hurry. Shen Xuanqing and the others went into the kitchen to watch and wait.

Lu Gu picked up a golden, glossy fried bun, took a bite, and found it crispy on the outside. The inside was warm and soft, not crispy, and a little salt made the flavor pop. The fried buns were best eaten hot, and with the oil and salt already in them, they didn’t even need vegetables—just a cup of hot tea or hot water when thirsty.

After eating a basinful, there was nothing left. The bottom of the basin was oily, and Wei Lanxiang didn’t want to wash it. She poured the oil into a separate oil pot and used a piece of tofu to wipe the oil from the bottom of the basin.

Lu Gu wasn’t idle after eating. He started cutting tofu and meat slices. Today he planned to make steamed bowls, so the tofu would need to be deep-fried first. Wei Lanxiang fried a piece of steamed bun to eat with it.

Winter was cold, so working at the dry stove would make your hands cold if they touched raw vegetables and meat, let alone getting wet. They had to set up several large baskets for steaming the buns ahead of time, so that they could steam them again for a few days’ worth of meals.

Shen Yan poured cold water into the pot outside and let it boil. Warm water was needed for kneading the dough. She wasn’t idle either; she went into the kitchen to help Lu Gu cut the meat, and the two of them moved faster together.

In the main room, Ji Qiuyue fished the soaked machicai from the wooden basin with chopsticks. The water was too cold to handle with bare hands.

Dried eggplant and dried mushrooms were also soaked. She was afraid it would get too cold at night, so she soaked them in the kitchen water to keep them from freezing, and now she brought them into the main room. Seeing that Shen Xuanqing had nothing to do, she said, “Daqing, go peel a cabbage and wash two radishes. Mother and the others can’t spare their hands, and they still need to fry the tofu and make the dough.”

Although the sun was out, the morning wasn’t especially warm. It was reasonable to roll up your sleeves and get the work done quickly.

“Okay.” Shen Yaoqing agreed. He felt that using chopsticks was too slow, so he reached out with his bare hands to fish out the dried mushrooms and eggplant.

“Aren’t you cold?” Ji Qiuyue looked up and scolded him.

“I’m not afraid of the cold. I have rough skin.” He laughed, stood, and took the two baskets away.

Lu Gu and the others were busy in the kitchen. After the dough had risen, Wei Lanxiang called her two sons to knead it. The two men were strong, and they kneaded until the dough became smooth in no time. Then she took over and rolled it into long strips. Since today they planned to make steamed buns, there was no need to knead the dough as a whole; they simply chopped it neatly with a knife, placed it in bamboo steamers, and let her sons carry them out to dry in the sun.

The three kinds of fillings for the steamed buns were chopped, and the two Shen brothers, Shen Yaoqing and Shen Xuanqing, set a brazier under the table, and the whole family sat in the main room to wrap them.

Cabbage and machicai were common winter fillings. The black machicai and white tofu were chopped and mixed with some meat. The eggplant was homegrown; this year, when they were prosperous, Wei Lanxiang bought more and dried it in the sun. In summer, steamed buns with diced eggplant and pepper were delicious. If there was no green chili in winter, they could cut some mushrooms and dipicais and mix them into the stuffing.

Seeing that Shen Xuanqing’s hands were a bit clumsy, Lu Gu placed the wrapped bun in his hand, handed him another piece of dough, slowed his own movements, and whispered to Shen Xuanqing to let him show how he wrapped it.

Wei Lanxiang smiled as she wrapped the buns: “I’ll make a few small ones. They’re not the same as the oil-fried little buns; we’ll try those later.”

When you had money and leisure, as long as you weren’t lazy, you were willing to tinker with food.

The three kinds of buns were squeezed together, and soon three bamboo steamers were ready. The eggplant buns were the fewest; the cabbage and machicai buns were more numerous. The big pots were filled with large bamboo steamers, and these six steamers would be enough for them for a long time.

Shen Xuanqing moved a large piece of firewood and stoked the bottom of the stove, and the fire caught and burned brightly.
