Chapter 33


The candlelight flickered slightly, and the dim glow fell on Shen Xuanqing’s face. His resolute, cold features seemed to soften a little. He lowered his head to peel the chestnuts. The keenness he had gained from hunting sharpened his senses, and when he looked up he found Lu Gu’s eyes dazed.

Caught off guard by those star-like pupils, the candlelight reflected in Shen Xuanqing’s eyes, bright and a touch softer. Lu Gu suddenly grew at a loss, and hurriedly looked away.

He bowed his head, embarrassed, trying to hide the rudeness of staring just now, so he stuffed chestnuts into his mouth. Raw chestnuts weren’t as sweet as roasted ones; they weren’t crunchy and sweet. He even picked up the chestnuts and peeled them himself, unwilling to wait for others to peel them for him.

Shen Xuanqing was also stunned for a moment. He didn’t understand love, but he was happy to live with Lu Gu. He didn’t know what Lu Gu was thinking when he saw him. He wanted to ask, but seeing Lu Gu so nervous, the words he wanted to say wouldn’t come out. Then he lowered his head and smiled, and passed the chestnuts in his hand back to Lu Gu, pretending not to notice anything, and said, “It’s late at night; it’s better to eat fewer raw things, and dry them in the sun for two days. If you want to eat them again, I’ll pick them up later.”

“Yes.” Lu Gu nodded obediently, chewing the one in his mouth, peeling the next from his hand, hesitating for a moment and wanting to hand it to Shen Xuanqing, but not moving yet. He saw Shen Xuanqing bow his head toward him; after peeling one and eating it, he had to steady his mind.

It was getting late, and the stomach felt uncomfortable after eating so many of these raw nuts. After a few, they went to the east room with candlesticks to make a bed for monk Huitong.

There was an extra person; it wasn’t easy for Lu Gu to wash his face in the yard, so Shen Xuanqing brought a basin of water into the room for him to wash, and brought in water for washing his feet.

After monk Huitong finished eating, Shen Xuanqing chatted with him for a while. Lu Gu hadn’t intended to listen to what he was saying, and besides the door was closed; he couldn’t hear clearly, so he lay down first after washing.

The night was dim, and the room’s light was not lit. He lay on his back and looked at the ceiling. For a moment, he thought about weaving two bamboo plaques tomorrow. He had to pickle the watercress tomorrow, and Shen Xuanqing would be able to taste it the next morning. He turned over and closed his eyes, and there was a faint sound of someone talking outside the door, as if being blocked by the door.

The emptiness around him made him a little uncomfortable, but his dullness kept him from noticing, and when he fell asleep and stopped thinking about things, he slept soundly. The habit was there, but not to the point of being intolerable or even noticeable.

In the early morning of the next day, Monk Huitong left some medicinal materials and bid farewell to Shen Xuanqing and Lu Gu. The kindly-eyed monk chanted the Buddha’s name, bowed his head slightly and gave a small salute, then raised his head again, turned, and left.

The night was dark last night; Lu Gu hadn’t dared to look at people, until now he saw Monk Huitong’s face clearly. When he met those eyes, he suddenly knew what “compassion” was.

The two of them went back to the yard. Lu Gu went to boil water to heat the steamed buns, while Shen Xuanqing laid medicinal herbs on the bamboo plaque to dry; they were common medicinal herbs for treating typhoid fever, and it would be good to have some ready. He took the chestnuts to the yard to dry for a few days.

The stupid tit in the firewood house cooed like a chicken, but the sound was duller. Although this creature was called a tit, it couldn’t fly. It looked stupid and big, with sharp claws and a long beak. It came here to peck and scratch, and blood would flow profusely immediately; most people didn’t dare to look for it, and it remained hidden deep in the old forest, rare in recent years.

The stupid tit meat wasn’t very delicious, but its feathers were bright and beautiful and quite popular among the people of Fucheng, used to decorate screens or other things.

When Shen Xuanqing caught it, he only hurt its wings, and tied it with hemp rope before approaching. He carried a small bundle of chicken grass into the firewood house, and threw it in front of the bound stupid tit. If he hadn’t been there, Lu Gu wouldn’t have been able to approach it, or else it would have pecked him.

He took another look at the hemp rope on the stupid tit’s feet, and found that it had been pecked at a little. The thing’s beak was really sharp, so he turned around and took a thicker and stronger hemp rope to tie it again.

“The door of the firewood room is closed. Don’t open it until I come back. This stupid tit has broken two strands of the hemp rope with its beak.” He bolted the door of the firewood room and told Lu Gu.

Lu Gu was timid, and he could be said to obey his words, so there was no one who wouldn’t listen.

After eating, Shen Xuanqing took the dog and left. There was no Dahui left behind today. Lu Gu watched them walk away, turned back to the yard, and remembered that the bamboo strips were all placed in the woodshed. When Shen Xuanqing locked the door, he didn’t remember it. Now he had to give up.

Since he stopped weaving bamboo plaques, he saw that there were some unsplit logs piled up in the courtyard, so he simply took a long axe to chop firewood.

This axe was heavy, but fortunately he was used to the work, so he wouldn’t be unable to swing it. He chopped wood with skill; with a swing of both hands, the weight of the axe did the work, and the wood split in one fell swoop without much effort.

He chopped all the firewood by himself in less than half an hour, and piled it neatly. Seeing that there wasn’t much firewood left, Shen Xuanqing had cast a net into the river the day before yesterday, and he might catch fish later. In the evening, he planned to stew a fish soup with a small stove. Thin firewood was better for stewing, and it wouldn’t burn dry, so he sat in the yard with a stool and a small axe, chopping the firewood into finer pieces.
