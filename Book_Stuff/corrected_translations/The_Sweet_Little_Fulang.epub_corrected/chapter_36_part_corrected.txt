Chapter 36


Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing sat in the courtyard, drinking water and resting. It was past noon, and the rest of the Shen family had already eaten. When they asked, they were told they had eaten dry rations on the road, so Wei Lanxiang hurried to the kitchen to cook.

Shen Yaoqing and Ji Qiuyue returned from the fields, seeing the cart full of fish at the door, and shook their heads with a helpless smile. The fish, plus the water, were heavy enough; thanks to the strength of the second son, others would have tired halfway down the road.

He came in and set down his hoe to greet Shen Xuanqing. Ji Qiuyue went to the house to fetch the scale, and the two pulled the cart to town to sell the fish. Most of the fish, except for river eels and loaches, had turned white. For a long time, even when they went to town, the dead fish couldn’t be sold at a good price. Even if kept at home, they couldn’t keep too much. It would be a pity if they spoiled after two days, so they pulled the cart and brought it back into the village. After passing through several villages, some could be sold by peddling along the way.

After the water boiled, Wei Lanxiang poured two bowls of brown-sugar water first, feeling sorry for them; this trip had been really hard. The brown-sugar water steamed and looked hot, and Lu Gu blew on it and took a careful sip.

Wei Lanxiang glanced at them from the kitchen window while chopping vegetables and smiled: “You two came back just in time. Yesterday I fried some dried peppers with sliced meat, and fried a winter melon to eat.”

Shen Yan came out with some freshly picked red dates, some of which were not fully red yet—half red and half green. These dates were picked by her at the third uncle’s house; they were crisp and sweet. She handed them to Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing and was called into the kitchen to help. The fried dried chilies were stored in a large clay pot, and she took a bowl and a large spoon to scoop some out.

The dried chili was bright red and oily, with thin slices of lean meat the size of a thumbnail mixed in. It was fried spicy with dried chili; the meat slices and chili were both crunchy when they came out of the pan. They put them in a jar. People who didn’t know how spicy this dried chili would be couldn’t stand it. On the contrary, the more you chewed it, the more delicious it became. The salt was also adequate. The fried meat was slightly salty and spicy. Before covering the pot, Shen Yan pinched out some sliced meat and chili for herself to eat; it was delicious.

While Wei Lanxiang was heating the steamed buns, she put two eggs on the bamboo steamer, so she boiled them for a while longer. She lifted the lid when the eggs were cooked, plated the steamed buns and eggs on a bowl, and scooped out the water from the big pot.

The winter melon was so ripe that it didn’t take long to fry it. Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing also recovered after a short rest; the dried chili was really fragrant, and it paired beautifully with rice, so Lu Gu ate a few more bites.

After eating, Shen Yan washed the dishes and tidied up. Lu Gu followed Shen Xuanqing into the room, anxious not to delay on the road back—if they delayed, the fish would die and become worthless—so he hurried to push the cart. The rope tripped and rubbed his shoulders red; it was worn out and needed medicine.

Speaking of it, although the two of them were married, it had all happened at night. When Shen Xuanqing took off his clothes, Lu Gu still didn’t dare to look at him. It was because of those things at night that he was still afraid, but at least he didn’t tremble like the last time he applied the medicine.

Shen Xuanqing’s broad back, with its clear lines, looked strong, and he was tall. Lu Gu was usually afraid of him, but whenever he went to town or into the mountains with Shen Xuanqing, he felt reliable for some reason.

Outside, Shen Yan saw the fish basket with river shrimps, called Guzi, “brother,” outside, and asked him what to do with the river shrimps. After the medicine was wiped off, Lu Gu didn’t dare touch it again, so he moved his hands and said, “Okay, put it on. I’ll go out and have a look first.”

Lu Gu carried the fish basket and set it under the eaves of the kitchen when he entered the house. Shen Yaoqing forgot to take it with him when he left. Lu Gu washed the medicine from his hands and squatted in front of the fish basket with Shen Yan. There were two large, white-shelled river shrimps in his hands. These shrimps were quite big; it had been a long time since they were caught, and they weren’t jumping around now.

After Shen Xuanqing dressed, he came out. Lu Gu asked him what he wanted, since he had forgotten to bring it, and this thing wouldn’t keep for long, so they might as well eat it tonight. Such big prawns could only be found in the deep mountains. Shen Yan could rarely catch them in the river outside the village. When she heard that they could braise prawns, she was so happy that she ran to wash them.

Wei Lanxiang fed the chickens and ducks and came out of the backyard. Seeing Shen Xuanqing sitting in the yard chopping firewood, she said, “Erqing, don’t be so reckless in the future. The fish aren’t easy to carry. If you rush, you’ll hurt yourself.”

She was a bit worried, because Shen Shunfu hadn’t made it through after seeing a doctor. In addition to being too cold for too long and hurting his roots, the doctor also said that he had worked too hard when he was young, like an old ox, without rest. Country people didn’t eat well and didn’t know how to recuperate. People often said overwork could lead to illness, but they didn’t see it until they were middle-aged. How could it be easy to walk on rough mountain roads for more than two hours and pull a cart? It was also true that country people had no other way to make money. It was good to have hunting skills to earn money and live a decent life. Although it was better than other farmers, it was still a labor-intensive job. Now there was no need to rush to pay off the debt, but to take a break.

Shen Xuanqing understood her worry; he kept working and said, “Mother, I know. Don’t worry. I will rest for two days when I come back this time, and I won’t hurry. Take it easy.”
