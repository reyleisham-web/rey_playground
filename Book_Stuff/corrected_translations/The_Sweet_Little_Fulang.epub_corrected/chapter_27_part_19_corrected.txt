Chapter 27 Part 19


He knew the money came from Shen Xuanqing, but Wei Lanxiang was the one who made the clothes, so his gratitude to the Shen family overflowed again. There was so much he didn’t know how to say it all.

It was Wei Lanxiang who saw him hold the shoes with moist eyes after he took off his old cotton shoes, smiled, and folded the new clothes, tucking them all into his arms. “From now on, as long as you and Erqing live a good life, keep harmony and joy between you, our family will get better. We’ll have what we need to wear and eat, and we won’t be hungry or cold.”

Lu Gu nodded heavily, the bundle heavy in his arms, and something seemed to lodge in his throat. He could only hum a muffled sound.

After dinner, when Shen Yaoqing returned from the field, they shared a pot of stewed mutton, enriched with medicinal herbs. The heat and nourishment made their faces rosy and gave them more strength.

As night settled, the dogs lay in the yard gnawing bones. With no one else around, they washed up and went back to the house to rest. The days were growing better, and they slept with smiling dreams.

In Shen Xuanqing’s room, a candle burned. The doors and windows were closed, and the two of them sat at a table counting money. Lu Gu set the small cloth bag on the table, pulled five taels of silver from his sleeve, and added them to the small pile of copper coins. He no longer thought about Shen Xuanqing’s copper coins; he pushed them all toward the pile.

Shen Xuanqing lowered his head to count the money, and suddenly these coins and bills appeared before his eyes. He lifted his head to look at Lu Gu and asked softly, “What is it?”

Lu Gu whispered, “The money is all inside.”

“Give it to me?” Shen Xuanqing asked, understanding at last that this was Lu Gu’s own hard-earned money, and he should not take it from him. So he pushed it back, saying, “Just keep it for yourself, and you’ll have money in hand for whatever you want to buy.”

Then he placed an ingot of five taels of silver into Lu Gu’s hand and added, “If it’s not enough, you tell me, and you won’t be short.”

The money settled, they blew out the light and the room fell into darkness. Not long after, Shen Xuanqing crawled into bed and rolled over to hug Lu Gu.

He was happy today. Except for the ten taels meant for Wei Lanxiang, what he had kept and what he had earned before now totaled twenty-nine taels, a small fortune he could gradually add to his own. The remaining five taels were given to Lu Gu, and he kept a little over four taels for himself. There were more than two hundred copper coins tucked away apart from the scattered silver, enough for everyday spendings for a long time. And since he had given his mother a significant amount of money, there would be no need to give more on his next visit. The house’s meat was basically his, and the vegetables were grown by them, so they didn’t need to spend much. Either he or Shen Yaoqing would save some for Wei Lanxiang as well, and they would decide what to buy.

The night grew darker, but the people moved forward with a quiet vigor, and the days sped past—the best things in the world.

In the evening, as the farmers returned home, the village steamed with smoke and slept in peace.

Shen Xuanqing had taken a pheasant out in the afternoon, saying he would drink with the village chief Chen and the others, but Lu Gu watched him go out without a word.

After their meal, Lu Gu and Wei Lanxiang went to Aunt Gu’s house to buy cloth and colorful embroidery threads. This was Lu Gu’s first time asking to buy something, and Wei Lanxiang agreed, coming along.

Now that he had money in hand, Lu Gu chose fine cloth and bright threads.

“What did you buy these for?” Wei Lanxiang asked casually.

Lu Gu hesitated, then spoke in a low voice, “I think Erqing’s purse is old, so I’ll make him a new one. I also want to make some sachets, so our family can wear them even if I don’t sell them.”

Erqing—he had said, and he followed along, though he hadn’t shouted like this in front of Shen Xuanqing. Seeing that Shen Xuanqing’s purse was worn down last night, he thought about it for a long time this morning. Although he was still easygoing, he wanted to help and do more; it felt right.

When Wei Lanxiang heard this, she breathed a sigh of relief and praised his sensibility for wanting to embroider sachets.

“My mother taught me,” he admitted honestly, afraid he hadn’t explained clearly, and added, “My mother.”

Mentioning this, Wei Lanxiang’s eyes grew soft with pity for his loss. A child who had lost his mother so early and endured such hardship could hardly be comforted. She tried not to let Lu Gu see her pity, and smiled, “Okay, when they’re ready, you’ll give one to your mother. She’ll wear it wherever she goes and show it to others. Our Guzi is good.”

“Yes.” Lu Gu nodded. Wei Lanxiang’s kindness extended beyond her own kindness to him; it was the kindness of the entire Shen family. So making a few sachets was no trouble, and he would continue to work hard to repay the Shen family.
