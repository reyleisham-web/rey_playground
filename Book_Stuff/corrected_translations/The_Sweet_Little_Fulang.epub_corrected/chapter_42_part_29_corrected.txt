Chapter 42 Part 29


Wei Lanxiang finished the meat, her mouth greasy with oil. She smiled at her second child. At first she had thought this kid and Lu Gu could never get along, but now he seemed to have learned to care for them.

Knowing that Lu Gu was reserved, Shen Xuanqing had to see to it that his fulang ate well for the first time when they went out to eat at a restaurant; otherwise, he wouldn’t be fit as a husband.

The big red elbow was warm and soft; the fatty meat melted, not greasy, the flavor fragrant and mellow, and the lean meat tender and smooth, very satisfying and palatable. Country folk always liked meat with fat, and it satisfied their craving; Lu Gu was no exception—he wasn’t picky about fat meat, and this elbow was especially tasty, its fat portions full of aroma.

After the meal, they drank sweet-and-sour green plum wine. These days even Shen Yan sighed with contentment. She was young and greedy, and said to Wei Lanxiang, “Mom, let’s come again next time.”

“Okay, come again next time,” Wei Lanxiang agreed with a nod.

Seeing her daughter so happy while eating, the corners of her eyes grew moist. The two Shen brothers—Shen Yaoqing and Shen Xuanqing—were doing well. A few years earlier, one was fifteen and the other twenty, but her little daughter had lost her father when she was only a little over eight. Well, even if she had eaten well when young, she was too young to remember, and she had long endured bran and vegetables with them these past years. She was happy today, and tears came for no reason.

Since Wei Lanxiang had lost her husband, she had pulled herself together and resolved to do better than in previous years. She turned her head and pretended to wipe her mouth with a handkerchief, eating and drinking happily.

The others at the table noticed, but when they saw she had recovered, they kept silent, continuing to eat meat and drink, chatting and joking from time to time, in a lively and cordial manner.

Lu Gu was more cautious as he picked up vegetables. Wei Lanxiang picked up a piece of elbow meat with her chopstick and gave it to Shen Yan. When she turned her head and saw him, the mother softened. Wasn’t Lu Gu just like Shen Yan, who had suffered under Du Hehua? She picked up another piece of meat for Lu Gu and said, “Eat quickly, Guzi.”

“Mother.” Lu Gu’s eyes were brimming with gratitude, and he whispered “Mother,” bowing his head without knowing what to say, which softened the mood even more.

Shen Xuanqing sat beside him, watching and listening; harmony at home was the best thing in the world.

Content with the wine and meat, Wei Lanxiang felt a twinge of pain when the bill came. The good meat and wine were far beyond what they usually bought in the village, and the meal cost 650 copper coins. Fortunately, she had brought enough money when they went out, so there would be no embarrassment.

The Xiaoer sent them out, accompanied by the call of “Guests, please come again,” and the whole family went home content.

The sun hung westward, the sky was full of clouds, and the faces of pedestrians glowed pink. Wei Lanxiang and Ji Qiuyue walked at the front, Lu Gu and Shen Yan followed a step behind, and Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing brought up the rear.

As they turned off East Street and left the town, Ji Qiuyue, who had sharp eyes, saw a familiar figure walking along the street—Lu Wen—followed by a servant girl and a boy, the very appearance of the town’s young master Shuang’er.

“Who is that? It’s the young master of the Lu family,” she hissed, her temper flaring. Thinking of those things she disliked, Ji Qiuyue could not hold her tongue. “If you’re looking for some other taller branch, I’m afraid you’ll regret your marriage and end up hiding in the town. Don’t blame others for poking your back.”

When Lu Wen saw them, he felt a chill. His face soured as he clutched the handkerchief in his hand. The servants behind him might not know the Shen family, but they could tell she was scolding him, and they immediately cried out, “You—look where you’re looking with such eyes! Who are you scolding?”

Shen Yaoqing and Shen Xuanqing both stepped forward; the two brothers were taller than most men, and Ji Qiuyue, in league with her man, wasn’t afraid. She sneered and said, “Who am I scolding? I’m not scolding him for being a snob; I’m scolding him for disliking the poor and loving the rich, for regretting his marriage and getting involved with a wild man!”

“Bah, you…” The boy began to retort, but Shen Yaoqing stood in front of Ji Qiuyue, his brows knitted with anger. “Shouting and scolding woman, are you still a man? Today we’re talking about those people who have no conscience and will be punished by Heaven. If you’re not convinced, come and tell me—let’s settle this.”

Although he was not as tall as Shen Xuanqing, he was taller than an average man. Naturally, his build was sturdy from year-round labor. The bully would have caved in at once, but with so many people watching the street, he could only stand there.

Lu Wen wanted to save face the most. Seeing that more and more people gathered, and Ji Qiuyue’s sharp tongue was getting louder, he could not afford to lose face, so he whispered to the boy beside him, “All right, it’s just some vulgar, slanderous words. Why bother with it? If there’s a big trouble and the officials come to investigate, won’t that harm us all?”

“I was born in a poor family, and I know that life for common folk isn’t easy. How can a family like this survive? Forget it, go back, pretend you didn’t hear anything,” Lu Wen said with feigned mercy.

Ji Qiuyue laughed angrily and spoke loudly again: “Just go. When I get to the yamen, I’ll show them the original marriage certificate so the officials can see who was engaged, who regretted the marriage, and who hooked up with a wild man.”

Hearing this, Lu Wen’s face paled; he almost tore the handkerchief in his hand. He was deeply annoyed, but with the growing crowd, he finally retreated, leaving bitterly.

Ji Qiuyue grew even angrier, and Wei Lanxiang patted her arm. Yet Lu Wen’s words shown that this restless Shuang’er girl must have some backing among the town’s powerful people—but so what? They could only curse and vent their anger, feeling the resentment rise.

Lu Gu hid behind and did not dare to speak; he had become Shen’s son-in-law thanks to Lu Wen, and he was grateful that the Shen family hadn’t beaten him; so he kept quiet.

Shen Xuanqing did not speak, but Lu Wen’s words brought Li Mingshan to his mind—the Li surnamed man. He had already asked someone to inquire about him earlier. He knew a thug from a brothel, and it wouldn’t be hard to deal with.
