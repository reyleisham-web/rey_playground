Chapter 46 Part 31


Before winter, the wheat was sown on the dry land, and qiuchai and chaidou were planted on two acres of ground. They had soaked chaidou in advance and added them to the rice porridge, cooking them together with white rice. The chaidou turned fragrant and sticky, and tasted wonderful.

When he and Wei Lanxiang packed away a big load of qiuchai, the cart was almost full. They ate early today, and it was only noon. Shen Xuanqing looked at the sky and said, “Now that it’s ready, let’s go when the sun isn’t so hot.” Wei Lanxiang nodded. She glanced at Lu Gu and asked, “Are you alone or not?” Shen Xuanqing also looked over, met Lu Gu’s gaze as he raised his head, and said, “Let’s go, and see shifu and shimu.” “Well, it’s a long way to go, and it’s good to have company. We’ll come back tomorrow night; I’ll leave some food for you two,” Wei Lanxiang said while washing her hands.

When Lu Gu heard that they wouldn’t be back until tomorrow and that he had never met Shen Xuanqing’s shifu and shimu, he couldn’t help but feel a little uneasy. Now Shen Xuanqing couldn’t bear it any longer, so she took up the embroidery frame and asked him to teach embroidery, and he got busy talking, slowly forgetting his anxiety.

After noon, the sun wasn’t so bright, so they packed up and went out with Shen Xuanqing. “Shifu and the others live in Jixing Town. After you pass Fenggu Town, it’s about twenty miles away, which is quite far,” Shen Xuanqing said as he pulled the cart. Lu Gu nodded. It really was far away. Their Qingxi Village was more than ten miles from Fenggu Town, and they had arrived in about three quarters of an hour. Jixing Town lay on the other side of Fenggu Town, a place he had only heard of.

Knowing that he had never traveled far, Shen Xuanqing comforted him again: “It’s not urgent; you don’t need to hurry. If you’re tired, rest halfway, and you’ll be there in no time.” Apart from his apprehension, the farthest places he’d been to were Fenggu Town to the south and Niujia Village to the west. He was still a little curious about Jixing Town to the south, wondering what the place looked like.

Lu Gu was very familiar with the road to Fenggu Town. He was used to walking on mountain paths. This time, only Shen Xuanqing was pulling the cart; Lu Gu wasn’t carrying anything on his back. They walked slowly, even taking a break on the road, so he didn’t get tired.

When they reached Fenggu Town, Shen Xuanqing stopped and asked Lu Gu to watch the cart at the door of a cloth shop while he went in to buy some cloth for his shimu. The old hunter wore furs, and his shimu was kind to him, so he had to make new clothes for the old lady. Lu Gu waited outside, sipped a few mouthfuls of water from a bamboo tube, and Shen Xuanqing came out after buying the cloth, pulled up the cart, and moved on again.

Headed south from Fenggu Town, Lu Gu looked around with curiosity at the places they passed. He had never been here before and felt it all very strange.

The two talked along the way. As they passed villages, Shen Xuanqing taught him the names of the places and towns, noting that although Jixing Town was far from them, it lay near Yuqingfu City. The town seemed big and lively enough for Lu Gu, but he still couldn’t imagine the more prosperous prefecture.

They walked and rested along Changxing Street in Jixing Town; the street grew more crowded with people. Lu Gu, unfamiliar with the place, drew closer to Shen Xuanqing and the cart, feeling nervous and a bit afraid of getting lost.

The street’s restaurants buzzed with activity, shops opened in all directions, and the mellow aroma of oil wafted from the oil mills—especially the sesame oil.

“We’ll go back and press some sesame oil. It will be even more fragrant when we mix it into the steamed custard,” Shen Xuanqing said, turning his head toward Lu Gu.

Lu Gu nodded and hummed, careful to keep close as a few people passed by. Shen Xuanqing noticed his timidity now; a shy smile flickered in Lu Gu’s eyes, and he let Lu Gu approach him without a word.

A tavern stood at the corner, its door marked with a wine sign, with people seated inside. When Shen Xuanqing said, “Here,” they halted in front of the tavern.

A shopboy came out to greet them, and when he saw it was Shen Xuanqing, he smiled politely and said, “So it’s Brother Shen. I’ll fetch the shopkeeper.”

Yang Xian came out quickly, and Shen Xuanqing pulled the cart away. It wouldn’t do to stop in front of the tavern and block the business. After asking whether the old hunter was at home, he led the cart toward the alley’s entrance.

“Is this the new fulang?” Yang Xian asked as he pushed the cart on the other side. He was fairly handsome, with a beard, wearing a long gown and a hat, and he looked like a businessman. “His name is Lu Gu, and this is Brother Yang Xian,” Shen Xuanqing said. Lu Gu whispered a greeting, timid but proper.

Yang Xian nodded with a smile; he was a man, and it wasn’t easy to talk to Shuang’er, so he asked Shen Xuanqing about Lu Gu later in private.

Entering a fairly spacious alley, they arrived five houses ahead. The slightly ruddy-faced old Yang Tou sat at the door smoking a pipe, tossing a few dates to entertain his grandchildren.

“Father, let’s see who’s here,” Yang Xian shouted before he arrived. “Shifu,” Shen Xuanqing returned the greeting.

Seeing his apprentice, Lao Yangtou brightened immediately, stubbing out his pipe, tucking the tobacco pouch at his waist, and standing up with a smile. Shen Xuanqing pulled the cart to the front and stopped, then called Shifu again.

Old Yangtou was about to speak to his apprentice when he saw Lu Gu looking at him timidly; he called Shifu in a softer voice, his happiness clear, the old man’s face creased with wrinkles as he welcomed his apprentice Fulang.

“Shifu, this is Lu Gu,” Shen Xuanqing said before he spoke.

Old Yangtou, though aging, reacted quickly and kept his cheerful expression. “It’s Guzi. Your shimu is inside; we’ve steamed pumpkin and dates. Go in and eat.”
