Chapter 26

Money Makes People Motivated

Money made people motivated, and compared with the joy of earning money, the crowds at the market didn’t seem so scary. After earning his first payment, Lu Gu’s shouting voice no longer trembled; he even dared to speak to the woman and fulang when they passed by.

Ma’am, look at the medlars, it’s all good.

Shen Xuanqing was selling game on the side. Seeing that Lu Gu dared to speak up after being busy, he felt relieved and his smile widened. Lu Gu was timid but not utterly cowardly; he knew what he should do, and he believed it would get better and better in the future.

A gray-haired, elderly woman with a basket on her arm leaned forward to take a look. She asked, “How much?”

Lu Gu quickly said, “Three copper coins.”

It was a habit of people to start high and then lower the price. The old woman was no exception; whatever she bought, she liked to bargain. She was used to it.

Although Shen Xuanqing told him that three copper coins was the market price, and that the goji berries he picked were big and good, which made them already cheap, Lu Gu had never run a small business before. He was a little uncertain without someone to guide him, so he looked to Shen Xuanqing for help.

“Auntie, it’s not expensive. It’s cheaper than the little ones, which sell for three copper coins.” Seeing him glance over, Shen Xuanqing felt like a reassuring pill being pressed against his heart and spoke up without thinking.

Lu Gu was grateful to have such a “reassuring helper” beside him, so he followed suit: “Ma’am, it’s not expensive.”

The old woman was a shrewd buyer who often bought things; naturally she knew the market price. After inspecting for a while, she saw the price was lower than she had expected.

Lu Gu hurriedly brought out the scales; he knew how to use them—Wei Lanxiang had taught him when she lent them to him last night. He weighed and earned another six copper coins, and when Lu Gu tucked the money into the cloth bag, he heard the clinking of the copper coins. Even though the money would be handed to Shen Xuanqing later, the sound alone was enough to make him smile.

From then on, if someone offered a lower price for the goji berries, Lu Gu could follow Shen Xuanqing’s guidance without needing help.

Compared to Shen Xuanqing’s income, Lu Gu’s money here was modest, but he was satisfied. When there was no one around, he would watch Shen Xuanqing sell game and lend a hand or do a small favor.

Today was a good day, the sun growing bigger and hotter. Because there were quite a few wild animals, there were still several unsold.

Shen Xuanqing felt hot and saw that the water in the bamboo tube had been drunk, and he was hungry, so he asked Lu Gu to watch the stall while he went to buy food nearby.

The busiest and noisiest time of the morning market was over. There weren’t many people shopping, and many hawkers who had finished their morning stalls had already left.

Seeing that no one was approaching for now, Lu Gu sat on a bench to rest. He had been standing almost the whole time, and now he finally had a moment to sit.

He scanned the few people around and soon found Shen Xuanqing’s figure; after all, such a tall man was quite rare.

Not long after, Shen Xuanqing returned with two bowls of Yangchun noodles and said casually, “This Yangchun noodles is good; I’ve eaten it twice.”

Lu Gu took a bowl. He had heard that a bowl of noodles cost ten copper coins; Lu Wen often ate it when he studied in town. After returning to Anjia Village, he sometimes spoke with Du Hehua about wanting to eat Yangchun noodles.

The two sat on stools and placed the bowls on the cart as a table. The noodles were thin and white, sprinkled with emerald-green scallions, and the clear soup was enriched with soy sauce and a bright yellow oil. It looked greasy, but the noodles were smooth and chewy. The soup was light and delicious, and you could finish the soup in one bowl. You felt satisfied and comfortable, and you thought it was worth ten copper coins.

When Shen Xuanqing returned the bowl, he also brought a bamboo tube of green plum wine. The wine had a very light brewing aroma and a taste that was sweet and sour. It was often drunk by women and Shuang’er.

Lu Gu had thought he brought the bamboo tube to ask for water, but he didn’t expect that when the lid opened, a sweet-and-sour fragrance would spill out.

“Drink.” Just as Shen Xuanqing finished speaking, someone came over and asked how to sell the pheasant.

Lu Gu held the bamboo tube and carefully tasted it. The taste was sour at first, then sweet, with a faint note of wine. This flavor was unfamiliar to him, but after two sips he found it delicious, and his eyes seemed to brighten.

After Shen Xuanqing sold two pheasants, he turned and saw Lu Gu’s joyful expression, and he was happy as well.

The sun climbed higher, making it harder to keep your eyes open.

Only two pheasants and the black-hoofed sheep remained on the cart. Shen Xuanqing took another look at the goji berries in the bamboo basket. They were halfway used up, but there weren’t many wild soapberries left, while many bundles of solid mugwort leaves had already sold.

“Go to the medical clinic to see if they’ll buy wolfberries, then go to the pork shop to sell the sheep, and you can head back.” He said, tidying the cart. He placed the benches on it, and since Lu Gu didn’t need the bamboo baskets, he left them on the cart and pushed it away.
