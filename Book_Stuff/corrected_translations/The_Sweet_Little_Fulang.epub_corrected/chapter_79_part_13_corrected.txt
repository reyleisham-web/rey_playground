Chapter 79 Part 13


“Then I’ll go and tell her.” Lu Gu sat on the edge of the bed, not even taking off his shoes, the thin quilt draped over his legs. After he spoke, he folded back the quilt and stood up.

“Well, you’ll come back after you tell her—it's too cold outside,” Shen Xuanqing replied. Since the visitor was a woman, it wasn’t proper for him to go along, so he let Lu Gu go.

Fortunately, the Shen family was behind the village, and with the wind strong at the moment, few people came outside; otherwise Lu Gu might run to the back of the house again and the news would reach Lao Zhang’s ears.

Li Wanyun hid behind the back wall of Shen’s house; the mud wall could block the wind.

Lu Gu, mimicking Shen Xuanqing’s words, told her what to do. Seeing that she was really freezing, he handed her the tube sleeves from his wrists to warm her hands. The tube sleeves were warm because he had worn them before. Li Wanyun felt that her hands were dirty, but Lu Gu didn’t seem to mind; after a moment’s hesitation, she took them and tucked her hands inside.

Her red, swollen fingers bore several scars. Lu Gu looked away and asked, “Are you going back now?”

Li Wanyun didn’t answer, only asked nervously, “Is he really gone?”

“Well, Erqing said so. He won’t lie to me.” Lu Gu nodded.

Li Wanyun hesitated, still afraid. After a moment, he spoke in a low voice: “Why don’t you hide in the woodshed secretly, at least to block the wind? I think the snow is about to fall again.”

That was a good idea, Li Wanyun thought. “His mother… My mother-in-law is afraid of the cold, so she should go back to her room now.”

Lu Gu had often been sent to sleep in the woodshed before, and he was used to staying there in cold weather; no matter how broken it was, it could still shield him from wind and snow. As for how Li Wanyun addressed her mother-in-law, Lu Gu chose to pretend not to hear it. After leaving the Lu family, whenever he thought of Du Hehua, he no longer acknowledged that stepmother. Once he mentioned it to Shen Xuanqing, he always called her by her first name; no one else in the family would talk about her.

After Li Wanyun returned the tube sleeves to him, the two of them walked around from the back of Shen’s house to the front one by one. Lu Gu looked ahead: the wind was stronger, and snowflakes were falling; no one was out at the moment, so he turned around to look at her.

“Go back quickly, I’m leaving.” Li Wanyun told him, and hurried toward Zhang’s house with light steps.

There was a gap in the door of Zhang’s house left by Zhang Zhengzi, so he could still get in after drinking. She tiptoed and gently pushed the door open, squeezing in through the slightly wider gap, her heart pounding. Fearing the wind and snow, she slipped into the woodshed with the door half-open.

If the door weren’t closed, the wind would blow in, but the door of the firewood room was old and creaked at the slightest touch, which made Li Wanyun’s face change in fright.

“Zhengzi?” Lao Zhang heard the door creak and called out from her room, thinking it was Zhang Zhengzi who had returned, but she heard no voice. It was windy outside, so she didn’t take it to heart. When she thought of Xiao Zhang again, she cursed a few words in a low voice, though she didn’t feel relieved; she would have asked Zhengzi to beat her up again.

Seeing that she went in and never came out again, and there was no sound of beating or scolding in the yard, Lu Gu entered the yard.

In the afternoon, it had snowed again; the sky was overcast, and it got dark earlier. After dark, they had nothing to do, so they went back to their rooms. Those who preferred not to light oil lamps went to bed early.

Afraid that he would fall asleep and lose track after eating, Lu Gu cooked dinner early. He still wrote the characters he learned today under the oil lamp after eating. Shen Xuanqing sat beside him, watching and correcting him whenever he couldn’t write properly.

Lu Gu was naturally shy, and at the moment when Shen Xuanqing’s hand rested on his to guide him in writing correctly, his ears and cheeks grew hot, but he did not refuse.

The candlelight trembled slightly, warm and reassuring.

It was getting dark now; the wind and snow were not heavy, and most people closed their yards to rest. But there was a staggering figure trudging toward the houses behind the village: Zhang Zhengzi.

He had drunk wine—though not good wine—and his face was flushed; he didn’t feel cold. While he walked, he kicked a stone, cursing freely, and kicked the stone far away.

In the dark, he could barely recognize the door to his house. He had drunk so much that he urgently needed to urinate, so he unbuckled his belt and stood by the mud wall to relieve himself. He drank a lot and urinated a great deal, the flow running down the wall to the side.

The drunk man squinted his eyes, his feet lightly unsteady, his hands not very strong; he staggered after finishing. He fastened his trousers without care, turned to go inside, but stepped into a large puddle of his own urine, slipped and fell.

He was slow to react after all that drinking, and he fell backward. His head hit the ground with a thud; the stone he had kicked just now lay beneath his head, the rough side upward. After the fall, his head lay there motionless.

It just so happened that Lao Zhang saw that her son hadn’t returned, so she went outside to look. Before stepping out of the yard, she heard a muffled “bang” out front and hurried out. There lay a man on the ground, just in front of the door.

Lao Zhang crouched there, trembling, and touched the rough jade pendant at Zhang Zhengzi’s waist, crying out, “Someone, help me!”

She tried to haul her son up while crying. Her son had stepped on the urine, slipped, and fallen to the ground. Her right leg was pressed by Zhang Zhengzi’s back. She was old and frail, and winter’s chill made her bones brittle; it seemed to break instantly. A sharp pain shot through her right leg, and she nearly fainted.

Even in her confusion, she knew something bad would happen if she passed out, so she grit her teeth and shouted for help, begging someone to save their mother and son.
