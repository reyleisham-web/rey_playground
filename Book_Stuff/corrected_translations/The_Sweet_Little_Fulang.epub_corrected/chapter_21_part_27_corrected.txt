Chapter 21 Part 27


Sure enough, a big fish was caught in the net. It was too large to slip free, so it thrashed and splashed the water everywhere. The fish hadn’t been caught in a long time, so its strength hadn't waned yet. The net wasn’t too big, and it could be lowered in the gentle, narrow stretch of the river where the water and grasses were lush. Lu Gu feared the big fish would break through the net and escape, so he quickly reeled it in.

He worked hard, and though he still had some strength in his hands, the fish was slippery. Afraid the net would break, it took him a while to pull the big fish free from the entangled line. Now that he had it, there was no coarse salt to pickle the fish here, and he couldn’t take it down the mountain to sell. He remembered hearing people say that fish stewed with pepper and chilies was delicious and spicy. It was time to eat something substantial, and the evening was cold, so a stewed fish would be warming.

Lu Gu glanced at the fish in the wooden tub and thought that there was water here, so it would be better to fetch a knife and kill it by the riverbank, so as not to pollute the yard. He fancied himself the only person within ten miles. He picked up the knife and approached, but the wooden barrel remained in place. This fish was too big; it wasn’t dead yet, it flipped and wriggled on the ground, slippery as an eel. Lu Gu couldn’t hold it down a second time, let alone kill it; the head of the fish was also large. He struck it several times with the knife, but it didn’t faint. So he had to pick up a fairly clean stone and smash it to death.

After he dissected it and scraped off the scales, he cleaned the big fish. He washed the kitchen knife and the blood from his hands, and went back with the fish in one hand and half a bucket of water in the other. There was plenty of meat on the fish after it was chopped into pieces. Lu Gu used a wooden basin to hold them. After he chopped, he didn’t rest. He carried the wooden bucket and ran a few times, filling the water tank until it was full.

He was busy in and out of the courtyard by himself, and the sun felt bright in the courtyard, though it was already noon. This meal should be rice porridge, but he added a small handful of rice and a ladle of water to the pot, so it would be easy to burn. He also thought rice porridge was good for people, and if Shen Xuanqing wanted to eat it, he could make more. He scooped out a large bowl of porridge and tucked it away. When Shen Xuanqing came back, he even put the bowl in the drawer and warmed it up.

In the main room, Lu Gu ate lunch alone. There wasn’t much rice in his bowl; he only scooped up the thin porridge. The dishes were simple—only half a bowl of pickles—and he ate two steamed buns. Such a simple meal was enough for him. When he could eat at the Lu family, he ate leftovers, mostly cold, which couldn’t compare to hot rice porridge and hot steamed buns.

Shen Xuanqing wasn’t around, so he didn’t dare to eat those other dishes. The vegetables they’d brought were mostly green onions, ginger, and garlic—greens such as autumn spinach, autumn cowpeas, and winter melon. They would wilt after a few days once dug up, so they couldn’t bring too many. If there were too many vegetables and they rotted, it would be a pity; he’d have to dig wild vegetables in the mountains next time. People in the countryside didn’t think it rare to eat a lot of wild vegetables; the vegetables they grew were expensive. Of course, Lu Gu didn’t dare eat too much behind Shen Xuanqing’s back.

The sky was blue and high, with thin clouds drifting by like a light mist. Lu Gu found a hoe and loosened the soil in the vegetable field in front of the yard, running back and forth three times with the wooden bucket, watering several rows of vegetables. He’d been busy most of the day by himself, and now he paused; there was a silence in his ears, no sound of another person.

He put the barrel by the kitchen door and stood there in a daze after he had no work to do. The secluded forest gave him a kind of hesitation and loneliness, as if he were the only one left in the world, abandoned by everyone. Shen Xuanqing would come back.

Lonely and helpless, he thought of Shen Xuanqing, and in his heart he was certain Shen Xuanqing would be back soon. Lu Gu made himself busy again, but there were only ten steamed buns left. Shen Xuanqing had eaten a lot; they wouldn’t last more than two days. With the pot empty, he began making pancakes.

Wei Lanxiang had specifically told him to eat pancakes in place of steamed buns, and that he would tire of eating only one thing all day long. People live to earn this mouthful of food, not only to be full, but to be happy when they can eat well. Although the thick pancakes he baked weren’t as fragrant as the oily shortbread, they were chewier and tastier, because they were baked on the bottom of the pan, round and thick, and one pancake was as big as three shortbreads. They were filling and sturdy enough to serve as dry food on the road.

There were five large pancakes and one small one, baked in a pot. If you usually ate them, you’d cut them into four. By Lu Gu’s own appetite, one-fourth of a big pancake was enough. Six pancakes plus the steamed buns would feed the two of them for several days, so Lu Gu didn’t make any more.

It was getting late, so he went outside the door to pick peppercorns, and while the fire burned at the bottom of the pot, he started stewing the fish, hoping Shen Xuanqing would come back to eat it. The autumn peppercorns here were still green, and they looked good with red dried chili peppers. The autumn peppercorns would disappear after a few days, leaving only dried chilies to be eaten in the future.

Although Lu Gu had never stewed fish before, he remembered Ji Qiuyue stewing spicy rabbit meat. He fried red and green chili peppers and Sichuan peppercorns with little oil. The numb, spicy taste made him cough and tear up. Fortunately, it would be milder once the fish meat was added and water poured in.

Soon the pot was bubbling, spicy and tangy, and it wasn’t sticky or spoiled. He was very happy; if his mother were still here, she would surely say he was doing well. The fire under the stove was turned down to a low flame. Lu Gu looked toward the gate; there was no one in the forest, not even a dog barking.

Waiting was the longest part, making him feel increasingly on edge. Lu Gu watched the gate for a long while before adding two more sticks of firewood. The sun was setting in the west; he added firewood several more times, added water to the pot twice, and set a drawer to heat the rice porridge, but Shen Xuanqing still hadn’t come back.

Hunting was like this—you came back sooner or later, he told himself, perhaps you would see someone if you looked up. The afterglow of the sunset bathed the front of the house, and Lu Gu sat on the threshold with a panicked expression.

As night fell, if Shen Xuanqing didn’t return, there would be wolves and old bears in the old forest. What if something had happened? He didn’t know the way, he might not even be able to locate Shen Xuanqing. These terrifying thoughts turned his face pale, and he had no time to think about the fish cooking in the pot. For the first time, waiting for Shen Xuanqing to return late filled him with panic.

Until a few barking dogs pierced the stillness of the evening, Lu Gu lifted his gaze subconsciously and saw Shen Xuanqing appear in the forest, already quite close. As Shen Xuanqing drew nearer, it seemed he realized he was about to approach Lu Gu, but he checked his pace and paused for a moment, then steadied himself and spoke in a trembling voice, "You came back."

Shen Xuanqing had reached the gate by then, and when he heard the words, his eyes looked guilty. He spoke softly, "You’ve been waiting for a long time."

In the forest, he saw Lu Gu sitting on the threshold, thin and gazing east with a frightened yet pitiful expression, as if he had waited there for a long time, not reacting until Shen Xuanqing came near. Shen Xuanqing, who had intended to bring back something good, felt a pang of guilt at the sight. Although he hadn’t expressed his love yet, Lu Gu’s waiting like this—any man would find it hard to be unfeeling. This was his husband, yet he hadn’t taken good care of him, which made him feel guilty. So he set down the bamboo basket, reached inside, and pulled out something wrapped in several layers of large leaves. After unwrapping, a golden honeycomb was revealed.
