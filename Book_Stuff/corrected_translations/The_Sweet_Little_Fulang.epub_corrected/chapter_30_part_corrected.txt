Chapter 30


This time they didn’t bring many things to the mountain—only a larger bathtub; the rest were food and drink, which wasn’t as laborious as last time. The tub had been crafted by a carpenter before Shen Xuanqing’s marriage. Although it wasn’t as exquisitely carved as the town carvers who made flowers and birds, it was new anyway. It had only been used by Shen Xuanqing and the new fulang, which showed how much money and effort the Shen family had spent on this marriage.

But after arriving at Shen’s house, neither Lu Gu nor Shen Xuanqing had ever used the bathtub. It was summer a while ago, and they had only boiled some water for scrubbing. What’s more, using a tub required boiling a large pot of water. Fetching water was convenient, but burning firewood like that was costly; firewood didn’t grow on trees, and you had to go up the mountain to cut it. Except around New Year’s, country folks wouldn’t bother with such a big bath.

Shen Xuanqing had scrubbed on the mountain a few days ago. Nights on the mountain were colder than off it. He was fine then. If it went on, even he might not endure it, and Lu Gu would be left to cope alone, so he moved the bathtub. If no one used it at home, it would just collect dust.

In the kitchen, Lu Gu was busy cooking rice wine, which boiled faster than porridge. He added goji berries and let them boil. Shen Xuanqing moved things down, and when the meal was ready, he even swept the fallen leaves from the yard.

It was past noon, and their legs were tired from the journey, so the two of them lay down on the bed to rest after eating and took a nap for a while.

Lu Gu was used to being hugged while sleeping, but Shen Xuanqing was too tall—broad chest, long legs, long arms—so he felt coerced and frightened whenever he mustered a little strength. Yet, being able to eat their fill and be held by his fulang who was so comfortable, Shen Xuanqing slept peacefully for a while. When he woke, his spirit had returned, and his eyes were bright.

He originally wanted to get up and go to work, but the little fulang in his arms made it hard to let go. They hadn’t shared the same bed for several days; his Adam’s apple rolled, and he couldn’t help rubbing his cheek against Lu Gu’s. Lu Gu lay beneath him, his face red and his ears flushed, clutching his clothes and sitting up slowly. When such things happened at night, Shen Xuanqing was always different from his usual self, which made Lu Gu even more fearful; he wouldn’t dare say a word, his heart trembling, his throat choked, his lips pressed tight as he remained still and silent. He didn’t dare curl into a ball or move a muscle, even if there was no sound.

If you put it to someone who understood people and understood humor, it would look awkward and dull; but Shen Xuanqing was the same. How could he understand these things? With experience, he couldn’t act on them yet, and for now, that was enough.

The mountain wind blew through the hall, blowing away heat and embarrassment, and Lu Gu gradually relaxed.

There was still a pile of dried bamboo and wooden sticks in the firewood room. Shen Xuanqing carried them to the backyard, cut them, and stacked them against two spots along the wall. They were mainly used to fence in the chickens and ducks in spring, since autumn chickens were scarce; he spent a lot of effort going to the market that day to find them—seven ducks and eight chickens—and the coop and duck coop didn’t have to be very large.

While he worked, Lu Gu boiled water in the kitchen. After it boiled, he set a bowl aside to cool, then helped Shen Xuanqing drive the bamboo poles and sticks into the ground.

“Let the chickens out during the day, and they will look for food outside. The ducks can also enter the water. They can eat grass on the ground or fish, shrimp, and earthworms in the water. If you don’t want to let them out, just soak bran. They can be eaten after being soaked in water.”

Shen Xuanqing explained to Lu Gu, who thought for a moment and then said, “It is better to fence the outside and enclose a large area, so as not to let the chickens run far into the woods and run into something a bit bigger that could snatch them away.”

Hearing this, Lu Gu nodded quickly and said, “I’ll follow you.”

These chickens and ducks were his, and they were precious to him; he must not let the things in the mountains take them away.

After the fence was completed, Lu Gu drove the chick and ducklings that were kept in the firewood shed into the enclosure, placing them separately.

The chickens and ducklings made childish calls, and he felt a little reluctant to leave. He squatted down to touch the chickens’ heads, and the birds twittered and chirped, gathering around him. When he looked up at the duck pen, Shen Xuanqing had leveled the ground with a shovel inside; as he turned his head to leave, the ducklings lined up and followed behind him, step by step, as if they were following a mother duck. Ducklings, Lu Gu thought, are more endearing than chickens when placed in water, and he smiled.

Shen Xuanqing blocked the ducklings inside and closed the fence gate. When he turned his head, he saw a smile on Lu Gu’s face.

Autumn air was refreshing; Lu Gu’s cheeks glowed a little under the sun. His skin was no longer as pale and bloodless as before—now it was radiant, and the red marks between his brows were as pretty as flowers, though he remained thin.

Shen Xuanqing couldn’t help thinking that it would be better if he were fatter, but he didn’t say anything. Even if he spoke, fat wouldn’t come to him right away.
