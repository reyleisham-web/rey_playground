Chapter 34


Shen Xuanqing looked at the money bag embroidered with lion patterns—delicate and well-made, not too small, more than enough to hold over two hundred copper coins. The peasants’ money bags were usually made of coarse cloth with few bright colors. When he was young, his family had been well off, but Wei Lanxiang’s embroidery work was not as good as Lu Gu’s. If he could have it made at home, there would be no need to buy it elsewhere. Besides, he didn’t pay much attention to these things; as long as money could be held, it was fine.

After Lu Gu handed him the money bag, he lowered his head and said, with a hint of embarrassment, “I’ll serve the food.”

The food in the kitchen was ready, waiting to be eaten. Shen Xuanqing glanced away from the money bag; the day’s hard work seemed to vanish the moment he received the bag—made for him by his fulang. He couldn’t help but smile. When he saw Lu Gu bring something out, he quickly tucked the money bag under his arm, stepped forward politely, and, overcome by surprise and excitement, snatched Lu Gu’s offering and said, “I’ll do it.”

Shen Xuanqing’s smile lingered in his eyes, and Lu Gu blushed a little at the sight. Recently he had noticed that Shen Xuanqing was handsome, but that smile made him even more captivating; he did not dare to stare for too long, fearing it would be rude or presumptuous.

The two sat down to eat, the smile still on Shen Xuanqing’s face, the money bag still in his arms. He used it to pack the twenty taels of silver he had exchanged after coming down the mountain. There were often many people at home, so it was safer to leave the money on the mountain rather than carry it back and forth.

The fish soup was delicious, and each had a bowl before them. Lu Gu tasted a couple of bites; the salt was just right. He had only stewed two small fish; the big ones had been killed and salted, kept for two days. The river was close by, and the two of them were the ones who caught the fish; once they were netted, there was little chance of missing.

Shen Xuanqing’s mind stayed on the money bag in his arms, a joy blooming in his chest. But then he remembered something and paused with his chopsticks, hesitating before asking: “When we were at Lu’s house, you and Lu Wen both knew how to do these things?”

The Lu family’s name came up, and a touch of unease crossed his features. “Money bags and sachets.” Shen Xuanqing added.

Lu Gu nodded, as if confirming something he hadn’t quite believed himself. “Well, he can do it too.”

Shen Xuanqing sensed something amiss and asked again, “Then does he often make them and sell them to subsidize his family?”

Lu Gu was stunned. He set down his chopsticks, his brow and eyes puzzled, and denied it: “He often said that he would study and copy books, and occasionally he’d make one or two. Du Hehua took his, and the ones I made together. We sold them in town.”

From Lu Gu’s perspective, Lu Wen’s behavior was hardly regular. When Shen Xuanqing had stayed at Lu’s house, Lu Wen often ignored him. In the village they seldom went out together; Lu Wen was boring and not talkative, and Du Hehua threatened him again, so Lu Wen rarely engaged with the villagers or joined in gossip. He didn’t know how Du Hehua bragged about Lu Wen to outsiders. Even if he heard others praise Lu Wen, he would bow his head and leave, too cowardly to stay. People often said he was “out of sight and out of mind,” so he wouldn’t be upset if he didn’t hear such things.

Hearing this, Shen Xuanqing frowned. At first the matchmaker had told him Lu Wen was sensible and caring, not like a young master who did nothing. It turned out to be a lie. But it made sense, if Lu Wen were really as the matchmaker claimed, how could he do such things? He hated the Lu family even more, but seeing Lu Gu’s uneasy expression made him feel a flutter of gratitude. After all, it was Lu Gu who had married into the family, not a villain like Lu Wen.

“It’s nothing,” Shen Xuanqing said lightly. “I’ll ask around. When we got engaged, Du Hehua told the matchmaker that he had made the money bag and sachet, but now it seems that’s not the case.” He paused, looking at Lu Gu in a daze, then hesitated and added, “What you made is better than what I’ve seen in town.”

Lu Gu’s face flushed with awkwardness and discomfort. It was him who had done a lot, and before his grievance surfaced, he heard Shen Xuanqing praise him as “good.” The word struck him, and he bowed his head shyly, a glimmer of joy warming his heart. He whispered, “This money bag is big; it can hold a lot of silver and copper. I bought some cloth, and I’ll make you a small money bag, one that can hold scattered silver and about a hundred copper coins, for when you go out.”

Back at the mountain foot, Shen Xuanqing had glanced at Lu Gu’s sewing basket earlier but hadn’t paid it much mind; his mother and sister-in-law often did needlework, so he assumed Lu Gu might be doing the same. Now that he heard Lu Gu wanted to make a money bag for him, he let go of every lingering grievance. There was nothing more comforting than his fulang making something for him, after all.

“Okay, when you’re done, give it to me.” He repeated the sentiment, urging him earnestly as if afraid Lu Gu would refuse.

“En.” Lu Gu nodded, and then asked one more question: “Do you have any patterns you want me to embroider?”

Shen Xuanqing said, “I’ll use whatever you decide, and I don’t understand these things.”

Lu Gu thought for a moment, then decided tiger stripes would be best; anything else would feel odd.
