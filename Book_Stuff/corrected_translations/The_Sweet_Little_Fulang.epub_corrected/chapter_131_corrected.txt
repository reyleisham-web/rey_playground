Chapter 131


Lu Gu sat and watched the rain for a while, but unexpectedly it began to pour even harder. Water roared from the eaves, a loud, rushing downpour like a white waterfall tumbling from the mountains. He and Wei Lanxiang moved their stools farther inside to keep their shoes and pants clean from the splashing.

Wei Lanxiang cast a glance at the dark, clouded sky and clicked her tongue. “This heavy summer rain is really something.”

Between the main hall and the front gate lay a small open area. The vegetables flourished in the larger backyard, while the front yard held only a few flowers. The blossoms on the vines and the delicate tendrils clinging to the trellis were battered by the rain; their petals lay scattered in the muddy water, creating a sloppy scene.

The so-called “vine flowers” was a local term. The tender red blossoms hid among the green vines and leaves, little red specks peeking out from the greenery, looking delicate and scattered. This was how the villagers referred to them.

These flowers were unassuming and small, and some particular women and fulangs in the village would plant a few. They lent a touch of novelty during the hottest days of summer.

The yellow and red petals were ruined by the rain, now stained with mud. As the rain intensified, streams formed quickly, carrying the muddy petals toward the drainage outlet. Earlier, they had removed the green brick that had blocked the outlet.

Through the curtain of rain, Lu Gu saw people running past the gate. In just this short while, he had seen more than ten, all of them workers from the fields.

The main hall was two steps higher than the front and back yards, a detail Shen Xuanqing had insisted on during the house’s construction. Normally, it required an extra step to enter, but during the rain it prevented water from flooding into the rooms.

Seeing the flower branches bent and broken by the rain, Wei Lanxiang turned her head to glance at the backyard. “I wonder how the vegetable plot is doing. We should check later. If any plants have been washed out, we’ll need to replant them.”

Lu Gu replied with a soft “Mm,” but his mind was filled with concern for Shen Xuanqing, who was on his way back. The rain was so heavy that he wondered whether Shen Xuanqing and the others had found somewhere to shelter.

Wei Lanxiang had also had a little wine at lunch. Listening to the rain pounding the world, with no sign of stopping soon, she simply said, “Since there’s nothing else to do, and there’s bedding here, why don’t we rest for a bit? We can sleep for a while and check on things when we wake up.”

So the two of them went inside and lay down on the bed together. The bamboo mat kept things relatively cool, and each of them pulled a thin blanket over their stomachs.

Outside, the four dogs had also found dry spots to lie down. Da Hui rested his head on his front paws, eyes closed, though his ears twitched now and then.

Flies would occasionally land on them, causing the dogs’ bellies to twitch or jerk, but after a while they just wagged their tails and ignored the pests.

The heavy rain lasted about three-quarters of an hour. Summer downpours like this usually didn’t last long.

Lu Gu had already woken up earlier. He yawned but stayed where he was, not wanting to disturb Wei Lanxiang, who was still sleeping. The rain had cooled things down, and this nap had been quite refreshing, easing the fatigue in his body.

As the sound of rain softened outside, Wei Lanxiang’s light snoring grew louder, a little faster than before, and it even startled her awake. She wiped her eyes and asked, “Has the rain stopped?”

“It’s stopped, Mother,” Lu Gu replied as he sat up and folded the thin blanket, placing it to the side.

They hadn’t closed the gate while they slept because of the heavy rain, but with so many dogs at home, there was no need to worry.

The flower branches and vines in the front yard were battered and disheveled, but the vegetable plot in the backyard was the bigger concern. The sky had cleared, and the rain had completely stopped. The two of them went to the backyard to check on the vegetable plot.

The soil in the vegetable plot had been moist from the morning’s watering, and after such a heavy rain, many plants had been battered down. Some with shallow roots were exposed, and a few had even been washed out completely.

Taking advantage of the wet mud and the mild sunlight, Lu Gu and Wei Lanxiang used small shovels to replant the vegetables, their hands and feet caked in mud.

“Oh dear, these little seedlings have been beaten so badly. What a shame,” Wei Lanxiang sighed as she touched the tender seedlings that had been ruined by the rain.

The house boasted a large, spacious yard, and country folk always felt the urge to plant something in any empty space. Otherwise the land seemed wasted.

With so many livestock around, there was plenty of manure. Qingyao leaves could be gathered from the mountains or bought from others who grew them. They decided to plant a patch of them themselves. Once the plants grew tall, they could be dug up, dried for two or three days, and used to repel flies and mask odors. They were truly useful.

“These plants are easy to grow. We haven’t even used up the packet of seeds we have at home. After this batch grows tall and is harvested, we can plant another round,” Lu Gu comforted her.

That was the only option. Wei Lanxiang nodded and stopped sighing.

The livestock had all retreated into their pens and coops. The newly built shelters were sturdy and reliable, effectively shielding them from wind and rain. Most of the animals hadn’t been wet or frightened.

After the rain, the ground was muddy and difficult to walk on. Between replanting the vegetables and feeding the livestock, Lu Gu was quite busy. When he finished, he returned to the main hall, leaned against the windowsill, and used a wooden stick to scrape the thick layer of wet mud from the soles of his shoes.

“Gu Zi, the tea is ready,” Wei Lanxiang called as she entered first. She had also poured him a bowl of clear tea, showing none of the airs or strictness typical of a mother-in-law.

“Got it, Mother,” Lu Gu replied. After scraping the mud from his shoes and washing the dirt from his hands, he went over.

As they were resting, Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing came in from outside.

“What a journey, truly rough,” Shen Yaoqing sighed. “We hurried as fast as we could and managed to take shelter at the Min family’s house in Old Wang Village before the rain got too heavy. You know, the family that’s related to Uncle Lin. We sat there for a while.”

As they spoke, they scraped the mud off their shoes in the yard before entering the main hall.

Shen Xuanqing, the ger among them, followed closely behind with a muddy cloak, his gaze steady as he stepped inside.
