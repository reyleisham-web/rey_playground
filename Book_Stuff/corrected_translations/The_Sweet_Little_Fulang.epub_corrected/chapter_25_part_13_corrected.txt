Chapter 25 Part 13


After they had finished the melon seeds, the rice would be ready. The rice wine went down smoothly, and the red wolfberries looked inviting. The tofu had been braised until it was brown. Let’s have another soft white-flour steamed bun. The white flour made lighter, tastier buns than the mixed flour. This meal was very satisfying.

Laughter and chatter filled the yard; every member of the Shen family wore a smile. Lu Gu didn’t have to wash the dishes after eating, and Ji Qiuyue told him to go back to his room and rest after his long trek.

This day passed happily, and the next morning Lu Gu followed Shen Xuanqing to the morning market. The official road was flat and much easier to walk than the mountain road. They pushed the cart forward. Lu Gu, who had spent the past ten days scrambling through the mountains, found the flat road suddenly easy. After all, it was better to go down the mountain.

He carried a large bamboo basket containing goji berries and wormwood; he had also picked a lot of wild soapberries, which were much cheaper than fragrant bath beads, and people in town used them too, so someone would buy them. As for the dried mushrooms, he thought there weren’t enough of them. People said they were good to eat and could be used in soups or stews in late autumn and winter. Besides the ones he kept up in the mountains, he also brought some home. If they found more mushrooms in the future, it wouldn’t be too late to sell them.

They rose early, and the flow of people grew busier after morning market opened. Because he was pushing the cart today, Shen Xuanqing brought two benches from home so they could sit and rest their legs. Lu Gu’s bamboo baskets were placed on the back of the cart, not taking up any extra space, so he only had to pay a fee. He pulled out a bundle of mugwort leaves to display, and placed the cloth bag containing the wild soapberries on the bamboo basket. It was visible at a glance.

Shen Xuanqing called out, hesitating at first, and when he saw an old woman pass by, he shouted: “Soapberries are cheaper—five copper coins per catty! Wolfberries are also available—three copper coins per catty.” Since it was a dry product, three copper coins per catty referred to the market price of goji berries. Last night, Wei Lanxiang had gone to borrow a scale from the village so he could use it when selling things, so they wouldn’t have to borrow from others. Otherwise it depended on someone’s goodwill.

“Auntie, look at these soapberries. They’re big enough to wash your hands with for two days.” Shen Xuanqing heard him, a smile flashing in his eyes, and he helped him talk to people.

Seeing the woman come closer, he added, “The ones picked from the deep mountain forest are better, and you can keep the fragrance after washing your hair.”

Hearing this, even the two passing couples and women came forward. They grabbed a handful, looked it over, and sniffed it.

Shen Xuanqing wasn’t lying—the wild soapberries Lu Gu had found were genuine; they were the kind they used in the mountains, and you could smell the fragrance after washing your hair.

“It doesn’t smell as good as fragrant bath beads.” A fulang picked some up, and even glanced at the wolfberries in the bamboo basket, pinching two to taste.

Shen Xuanqing was used to such things and replied softly, “How can wild soapberries compare with fragrant bath beads? Ours are cheaper, and they’re already more fragrant than those bath beads.”

“So I’ll buy six for six copper coins,” the fulang bargained with him. “Fulang is just joking. The things aren’t expensive at all, and they’re so good. My fulang walked three hours along the mountain road to bring them back. It isn’t cheap.” Shen Xuanqing didn’t agree.

These things were sold at a low price—one copper coin per catty—and now one copper coin wouldn’t even buy a sesame-seed cake. If the price went lower, Lu Gu would have spent days in the mountains for nothing.

The fulang seemed to really want them, and seeing that Shen Xuanqing wouldn’t back down, he kept talking, finally offering to buy six for twenty copper coins. Originally they had planned to sell them for twenty copper coins, but when he heard they were only fifteen copper coins, Shen Xuanqing grew a little annoyed, and finally asked Lu Gu what he thought: as long as he bought them for fifteen copper coins, it would be six.

Lu Gu, new to selling things, was nervous and didn’t figure out how much change to give for fifteen copper coins; he memorized it quickly and counted it in his head.

When the fulang left, he reached out to take two more; Shen Xuanqing pretended not to see. This kind of person was common in business, and the trick was to make money with kindness, so there was no need to worry too much about it.

Lu Gu understood this too. Besides, he had sold it for fifteen copper coins at once. Shen Xuanqing didn’t pocket the money—the fulang handed it to Lu Gu.

Fifteen copper coins weighed in his hand, but he had no time to think about it, because there were two women who wanted to buy them, and they had just agreed with the fulang to lower the price. Since there were six for fifteen copper coins, both of them wanted them.

After Lu Gu finished counting the wild bath beads and collecting the money, with the fifteen copper coins already on the cart, the total came to forty-five copper coins.

The farmers were jubilant to earn forty-five copper coins at once, especially Lu Gu, who hadn’t handled many copper coins; the most he had seen in his hands was ten copper coins, back when his mother was around.

The sight of this large handful of copper coins felt like a dream, but dreams fade, so he reluctantly handed the money to Shen Xuanqing. Shen Xuanqing frowned slightly in doubt and asked, “What are you doing for me? Don’t you bring a small cloth bag?”

Lu Gu thought he would put everything in the cloth bag after the sale, so he hurried to do as told and handed the money over.

While they were talking, someone came to inquire about the wild goods, so Shen Xuanqing had to stop talking, turn around, and get busy.
