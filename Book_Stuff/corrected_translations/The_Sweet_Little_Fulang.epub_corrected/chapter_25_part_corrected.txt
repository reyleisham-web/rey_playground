Chapter 25


The mountain road was rugged, and the journey was tiring and hard, but the full load of prey filled their hearts with vigor, and their legs seemed to carry them endlessly.

On the hillside behind the village, two small dogs barked and rushed home. Shen Yaoqing, who was carrying a hoe to the fields, heard the commotion and looked up, then hurriedly glanced toward the yard. He dropped his hoe and hurried over to meet them. In his haste, he rubbed the dogs’ heads twice; otherwise they would have pressed against his legs, their tails wagging furiously.

Wei Lanxiang’s group hurried out as well. Seeing Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing in good spirits, her worries faded. When they descended from the mountain, they were clean and tidy, no longer looking like bandits with tangled hair, and their smiles were broader. They looked spirited, as if they could leap into the day with energy.

In this way, even if she died now, she would be worthy of Shen Shunfu when she went to meet him in the afterworld.

“Mother, sister-in-law.” Lu Gu whispered to Wei Lanxiang and Ji Qiuyue.

“Ah, it’s good to be back,” Wei Lanxiang said with a bright smile, and together with Shen Yan, she unloaded the bamboo basket from her back.

“Brother Guzi, can you give me one of your flowers?” Shen Yan, seeing several mountain flowers in the basket, was mesmerized by their beauty. They couldn’t be named; the petals rose from pink at the base to orange at the tips, like clouds blooming in the evening sky. Because there were several flowers, she couldn’t help asking.

Lu Gu, who had noticed this on the road, plucked a few stems from a cluster and brought them out. “Here, for you,” he said. He looked at Ji Qiuyue again and handed the bouquet to her, whispering, “Sister-in-law, there are some for you and for mother too.”

Wei Lanxiang laughed from ear to ear, and she handed the flowers back to Lu Gu, but Lu Gu shook his head and whispered no, so she gave them to Ji Qiuyue. “It’s hard for you to be filial; it’s only because mother is old enough to wear flowers for what, and people will laugh at the old monster.”

The whole family chatted and laughed as they pulled the cart back into the yard.

Shen Yan had flowers braided into her hair. Although she was young, with a round face and lovely almond eyes, the flowers made her look even cuter.

She was eager to go out and show off to girls her age, but the cart held so many things that she stumbled. After some hesitation, she decided to see what Brother Erqing had brought back first. It wouldn’t be too late to flaunt the flowers in her hair later.

“This is a black-hoofed sheep? Amitabha, it should fetch a good price, and there are a lot of pheasants,” Wei Lanxiang exclaimed, surveying the goods on the cart. Each item brought a murmur of joy.

When she learned that the dried goods in Lu Gu’s bamboo basket had been dried by Lu Gu himself, Shen Xuanqing flashed a wink, and she praised: “Guzi picked so many goji berries and a lot of wormwood. It’ll fetch a good price, and tomorrow I’ll brew you some rice wine, and we’ll also grab some goji berries to cook rice wine to drink.”

Lu Gu took care of Shen Xuanqing; he worked so diligently that Wei Lanxiang didn’t show favoritism, yet the more she looked at Lu Gu, the more she felt blessed that she hadn’t married that Lu Wen. She clapped her hands and said, “I won’t wait until tomorrow—mother will go with your sister-in-law.”

Lu Gu bowed his head shyly. Before he could lift his eyes, Wei Lanxiang went out to brew rice wine with Ji Qiuyue, a basket slung over her shoulder.

Shen Yan finished inspecting the cart as well, and she plucked a few long, colorful feathers from the tail of a dead pheasant, then said to Shen Xuanqing, “Second ger, I’m going to play with Caixia and the others.”

Back home, he was glad to see her in good spirits. Shen Xuanqing didn’t object to her playing; he didn’t even let the ducks go. He handed her a small cloth bag and said, “Share it with them.”

Shen Yan took it, opened it, and found wild fruit. Her smile widened, and she went out merrily.

The items on the cart were not unloaded; only three pheasants and two hares were taken down and left for Wei Lanxiang and the others to eat; the rest would be taken to town tomorrow to sell.

Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing washed their faces and sat in the courtyard to rest. Shen Yaoqing also stopped going to the fields, came into Wei Lanxiang’s room, grabbed more than half a bowl of melon seeds, and sat under the eaves chatting cheerfully with his second ger. The melon seeds, bought two days ago, were fragrant roasted seeds and nuts; Wei Lanxiang wasn’t willing to take out more, thinking it would be an honor to serve such a treat if someone from the family came by.

Lu Gu had only eaten melon seeds twice in his life at this age, so there weren’t many; now he sat and nibbled slowly.

In Qingxi village, someone made rice wine; Wei Lanxiang and Ji Qiuyue went to the tofu shop in the next village, planning to stew tofu with meat sauce. Tofu wasn’t available in the mountains, so they would be rewarded when they came back. They hadn’t bought pork recently, so she inquired and finally asked Mrs. Lin Jinhu from the village for half a cattie, to be paid back tomorrow.

When she returned, she saw Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing resting in the courtyard. She wouldn’t let Lu Gu help and went to the kitchen with Ji Qiuyue to light a fire and cook. As for the melon seeds Shen Yaoqing had stolen, she glared at her eldest son, knowing his childish nature. Yet she laughed again. She favored Shen Xuanqing more, but she wasn’t harsh on the eldest son either—both of them were well-fed.

As for Shen Yan, the youngest daughter, she was not on par with the two sons. When they went to town to sell eggs and duck eggs, she would always be brought along, and even if Ji Qiuyue didn’t say anything, she knew that they were taking her daughter to the festival. Either a bowl of hot and sour bean curd or a shortbread would be bought for her.

Of course Lu Gu didn’t know; he had only just arrived himself.
