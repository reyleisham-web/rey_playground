Chapter 19


The two of them were still on the outskirts of the forest. People passed by here often, and wild animals were scarce. There was no need to fear bringing a dog; the ferocious wolves, tigers, and leopards lurked deeper and farther into the mountains.

After walking for a quarter of an hour, the crowded part of the forest fell away behind them. The sun would come out soon, and the night creatures would retreat to their lairs before the light fully arrived, so there was nothing to fear.

Day travel wasn’t impossible, but Shen Xuanqing was used to moving at this hour. It was only halfway through the morning when he reached the deep forest house, and the rest of the day would be plenty to accomplish many tasks. Yet at that moment, listening to Lu Gu’s movements behind him, he thought that next time he should wait until daytime to leave. Now that he didn’t have to work so hard to pay off the debt, he should slow down and not hurry so much.

“Keep going toward those piles of chopped wood, and after a few trees ahead you’ll see the axe marks on the trunks—just follow them.” Hearing Shen Xuanqing’s words, Lu Gu’s eyes tracked the direction he indicated and he wrote it down carefully.

There were still some stepped paths up the front mountains, but they disappeared as they curved behind. To make it more realistic for him, Shen Xuanqing even told him to go to the front and pointed out the road signs all the way.

The sun hadn’t risen yet, but it was brighter than before. Dragging the cart to the end was a struggle, and when they finally reached a flat clearing, they stopped to rest and ate a steamed bun to settle their stomachs.

Progress was slow. Originally Shen Xuanqing could have reached it in an hour and a half, but today it took nearly three hours because of the cart.

Lu Gu looked at the courtyard in front of him and could hardly believe there was such a tall mud courtyard wall in the deep mountain forest—taller than the wall around the house at the foot of the mountain, and even the courtyard gate was tall, giving a solid impression.

Seeing the surprise in his eyes, Shen Xuanqing opened the door, pushed the cart in, and began unloading while explaining the reason.

My master—that is, the old hunter—had a night of drinking and had gone to bed early. Before it grew dark, he heard something outside. An old bear from the depths of the ancient forest had come in and raided the kitchen, eating all the food.

The rice and flour were swallowed, the bags of flour torn open and scattered, the pumpkins and wild fruits eaten, and the live rabbits that had been caught and kept to breed were all slain by the old bear.

Meeting a bear in the mountains was no small matter, especially a bear of some age. It seemed to have become almost a spirit, able to sniff out any tasty morsel, and it looked terrifying enough to sever a head with a single claw.

The old hunter didn’t dare to make a sound inside the room. The bear wandered outside, trying to break into the room, circling around a few times, and slammed into the door. Fortunately, the old hunter had bolted the door with a thick tree trunk before going to bed, and after a few impacts it didn’t break in. Then the bear went to the window and clawed at the wooden window.

The old hunter’s bed was right under the window. When he realized the old bear was in front of the window, he quickly rolled over and hid under the bed, not moving at all.

The window was so small the bear couldn’t get in, and it didn’t scratch when it stretched its paws inside. Perhaps it was full and lazy after eating, for it chose a place outside to sleep on its belly, snoring loudly all night. The old hunter stayed hidden under the bed, and it could be described as a night of torment.

Only at dawn did the old bear have enough to eat and sleep, and then the old hunter dared to show his head and hurried down the mountain as the sun rose bright.

Fearing the old bear would come back to forage, he called his whole family—four young and strong men from the village who could build walls with yellow mud and straw—and borrowed twenty dogs from nearby villages. A group of people carried hatchets, bows, and arrows, and they marched into the mountain with great vigor.

Being a hunter was risky, but it paid more than ordinary farming. The old hunter wasn’t willing to give up this work, so he spent a lot of money to build such a high courtyard wall in the deep mountains, to block beasts wandering out of the old forest.

Not only the courtyard walls, but also some of the houses that were damaged were repaired.

This house was given to Shen Xuanqing to live in when he came to the mountains. It had remained sturdy for more than ten years, with not only the courtyard walls but also the beams and the timbers still solid. In the deep mountains, wood was indispensable, and no one could manage it easily here. All the huts were built with fine timber; otherwise, with heavy wind and snow in winter, people would descend the mountain, and if the roof collapsed, there would be no place to live when they returned in the spring.
