Chapter 23


He thought Shen Xuanqing would haul the dog into the mountains again after breakfast, but today he did not go. The tall young man lifted a long machete and said, “There’s a bamboo forest not far from here. I’ll go cut some bamboo. Come back to weave bamboo cages—will you come along?” His eyes were bright with expectation, and he lingered after speaking; it was clear he wanted Lu Gu to go with him. No matter how slow Lu Gu was, this much was obvious. After a moment’s hesitation, Lu Gu nodded.

Shen Xuanqing breathed a sigh of relief. He knew what had happened to Lu Gu last night—the movements in Lu Gu’s arms were obvious. Lu Gu could have woken him, but out of selfishness Shen Xuanqing pretended to sleep and didn’t let go; the softness of Lu Gu in his arms let him sleep soundly.

Lu Gu carried an empty bamboo basket with a hatchet inside, and Shen Xuanqing carried a machete and hemp rope. Although they were in the mountains, they still locked the door.

The dog trotted through the woods, sniffing everywhere, sometimes lifting its hind leg to urinate on tree roots to mark its territory.

After walking for about an hour and a half, Lu Gu saw the dark green bamboo grove ahead. Shen Xuanqing said there were autumn bamboo shoots in it—autumn shoots that weren’t as crisp and tender as spring shoots, but they could be stewed with fatty dove in soup, and they could be boiled to make pickles. But the two of them only had a pickle jar, and they hadn’t finished eating what they had, so there was no need to pickle yet. So, upon arriving, Shen Xuanqing went to cut bamboo by himself. He was looking for bamboo shoots in the bamboo forest. Seeing him walking away, Dahui followed, and the two dogs scattered through the trees, finding holes or something. When they dispersed, they stayed within Shen Xuanqing’s sight.

As for Lu Gu, this bamboo forest was deeper and quieter than the one on the front mountain. The autumn bamboo shoots were quite big, and he took a hatchet and swung hard several times before breaking them off. Since they would only be used in soup, one large autumn bamboo shoot was enough, but since they had come all this way, he still cut two.

He memorized the road to the bamboo forest so he could come back by himself in the future when he wanted autumn bamboo shoots.

A long bamboo stalk fell with a dull thud in the air. Lu Gu walked over with the bamboo basket on his back and whispered to Shen Xuanqing, who was watching him, “When I came in, I saw mushrooms on the outside. I’m going to look for them.”

Shen Xuanqing couldn’t help but smile. “You should shout loudly so the dog can follow you.”

Taking a dog into the mountains was reassuring, so Lu Gu followed his advice and shouted. As expected, Dahui came along.

Someone in the village had died from eating poisonous mushrooms when he was a child. Lu Gu always remembered that, so he only dared to pick mushrooms he knew.

Today he was lucky; he found a cluster of xanthella mushrooms. The florets were so big that one flake could fry half a bowl, not to mention that there were several flakes in the nest.

Looking under a tree, he could still hear the sound of chopping bamboo. He looked back from time to time, afraid he would lose sight of the bamboo forest if he walked too far away.

In unfamiliar mountains and forests, it was wise to stay cautious; otherwise, if you wandered into a dense thicket, you could get so lost that you wouldn’t know which way to go, much less find your way out.

Some mushrooms looked good on the surface, but when picked up, insects and ants gnawed them under the canopy, so he threw them away.

He walked on, hoping to find more. Perhaps there weren’t many people up in the deep mountains, and the mushrooms hadn’t rotted yet, so he found a lot. If he couldn’t finish eating them in two days, he could sun-dry them and save them for late autumn and winter, not afraid of gathering more.

When he realized he could no longer hear the sound of chopping bamboo, he hurriedly called Dahui to come back. He hadn’t noticed how far he’d wandered while looking for the mushrooms, but on the way back he realized he had gone quite a distance.

Just before entering the bamboo grove, he saw Shen Xuanqing coming out. Seeing Lu Gu return, his expression looked a little less grim, and he said, “I’ll call you.”

You could only call to locate someone in the mountains. Lu Gu brought the dog again, and Dahui’s ears were sharper than a human’s, and he ran toward the sound when called.

Lu Gu quickly said, “I didn’t pay attention while looking for the mushrooms and wandered off.” Afraid of being blamed, he also showed the bamboo basket to Shen Xuanqing, carefully asking for credit.

Shen Xuanqing glanced at the mushrooms in the basket; there were quite a lot. Seeing Lu Gu’s anxious expression, his own face softened and he said, “Well, it’s fine. The bamboo is cut; I’ll tie it up and pull it back.”

After not being reprimanded, Lu Gu felt at ease, shouldered the bamboo basket, and walked into the bamboo forest with Shen Xuanqing.

The weight of the six long bamboo stalks was not light. Shen Xuanqing tied the bamboos together with hemp rope and carried them on his shoulders. Of course, this was for him; if it had been Lu Gu, it would have been heavy enough to carry two bamboos, and the wet green bamboos they had just cut were tall, making them especially heavy.
