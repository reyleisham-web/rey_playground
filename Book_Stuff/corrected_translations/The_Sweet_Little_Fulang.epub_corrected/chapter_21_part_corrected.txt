Chapter 21


It's time to go back.

Shen Xuanqing glanced at the western sky. The sun had already sunk, and darkness would come soon. The mountains and forests at night were dangerous, so it was best to head back as quickly as possible.

Lu Gu’s eyes rolled slightly at this sentence, a flicker of fluster crossing his face. He bit his lip, lowered his head, and tried to hide the distraction that had just flashed across him.

He had been kicked out several times when he was hungry; when he searched for a long time and found nothing to eat, he would clutch his stomach and stare dazedly at the river or the mountains. People would laugh at him for being stupid, and he felt embarrassed. That mood rarely came over him again after that.

Back home, a thick tree trunk leaned against the wall beside the courtyard door. Shen Xuanqing closed the courtyard gate, then half-hugged and half-dragged the heavy trunk against the door. Not even bears could break that gate.

Ever since the old hunter’s incident, he had learned from him how to do these things, and the hunter often told him that he must press the door tight at night, or else he would die.

While it was still dark, Lu Gu boiled water for the two of them to wash and rinse their feet. It was really cold in the deep mountains after the sun went down. Fortunately, Wei Lanxiang brought him a jacket.

The crescent moon hung quietly in the sky. The windows creaked as they were closed, and the room grew much darker, with only outlines visible in the haze.

Lu Gu slept in the bed, and Shen Xuanqing closed the window and crawled into bed beside him. The quilt was pulled up and settled, and there was a warm, tall man beside him.

Lu Gu only slept beside Shen Xuanqing, and lying next to such a tall, strong man sometimes made him feel oppressed and afraid. He even wondered, could it be that all married Shuang'ers and girls were afraid of the men around them at night? But such thoughts must never be spoken to another person. He felt ashamed, and he didn’t even dare to think about it because he knew how outrageous it would sound.

Inside and outside the house was quiet. They were under two layers of quilts, warming each other as they lay, and Lu Gu gradually fell asleep.

The other person on the bed could not sleep for a long time. Shen Xuanqing didn’t know what was wrong with him; the soft, shallow breathing in his ear made him more impatient. He couldn’t quite name what he longed for—he felt as if something was missing in his hands, something in his chest that wouldn’t fill, an emptiness.

When the forest quieted, you could hear the faint rustling of the wind outside. Lu Gu’s breathing was steady; he seemed to be in a deep sleep.

Shen Xuanqing turned over gently, reached for Lu Gu’s soft hand under the quilt, and gradually tightened his grip so the palm would feel secure.

People are greedy; it’s only a matter of whether the greed is big or small.

He held Lu Gu’s hand. He should have fallen asleep like at noon, but perhaps the long night—or something else—made him draw closer. The closer he got, the more he could smell the warmth and softness of Lu Gu’s body; the bed grew hotter, the scent grew stronger.

Shen Xuanqing was almost a mess. He was an eighteen-year-old boy, brash and reckless, who had never even held Shuang'er's hand before, let alone faced this alluring thing only he could perceive.

The night blinded the eyes, intangible yet tangible; since no one could see, it gave him courage.

When Lu Gu had been at Shen’s house last night, he hadn’t slept well, worried about going up the mountain; today he’d walked the mountain road for a long while, and hadn’t woken him.

After pulling him into his arms, Shen Xuanqing let out a sigh of relief; his empty chest was filled. People often said the word “satisfied”—and that was it.

Dew lay heavy in the morning, and it wouldn’t be long before he walked into the grass; the cuffs of his pants would be damp. After Lu Gu woke, Shen Xuanqing had already dressed and left the bed. Whether they slept in each other’s arms last night, he wasn’t sure.

The usual practice of getting up early was to boil water and eat steamed buns, nothing different from life at the foot of the mountain.

“I’m late. I can’t make lunch. You can eat by yourself; you don’t need to wait for me.” Shen Xuanqing shouldered a long hatchet, a bow and arrow, and a bamboo basket on one shoulder, with hemp rope and a small cloth bag of steamed buns in the basket.

The two of them crossed the threshold; outside the courtyard, he turned to Lu Gu and said, “There are no wild animals in the forest around here, but don’t run too far. It won’t be too late to go out again after I’ve familiarized you with this place.”

Lu Gu nodded. Shen Xuanqing had nothing more to add, and after a moment’s pause, he said, “Then I’m leaving.”

With that, he headed east, and the three dogs ran toward the forest in their familiar way.

Lu Gu watched as Shen Xuanqing’s figure disappeared into the forest and the dogs’ barking faded away. A sense of emptiness and quiet pressed down on him; he was alone.

Birds fluttered about as the sun rose, bringing warmth to the world. Lu Gu returned inside by himself; the sun hadn’t fully risen yet, and he felt a twinge of unease. He closed the courtyard door, the high walls giving him a sense of safety.

The front yard was quite large, but not well tended. Long, heavy axes lay on the ground, and unsplit firewood was scattered about. Lu Gu picked up an axe, leaned it against the wall, and stacked the unsplit wood beside the firewood shed, neatly arranged. He washed or wiped down the chopping boards, stoves, pots, and bowls in the kitchen, and dusted the small jars of oil, salt, sauce, and vinegar.

Because it was difficult to transport things in the mountains, the rice and flour, along with the water tank, were kept in cloth bags on a mud platform on the other side of the chopping board. If these bags rested on the ground, moisture would seep in and spoil the rice and flour, so they were kept in a higher place.

He took a broom and swept the house and kitchen again. The mountains bore many trees, but the front and back yards had few leaves since the trees around them had been cut to let in the sun. There was enough open space, so nothing blocked the path when they went out.

When Lu Gu finished his chores, the sun rose high enough to warm the world. He dared to go out and, seeing the water in the tank running low, went to fetch water with a wooden bucket. He arrived at the river and remembered the net Shen Xuanqing had laid yesterday, not far away.
