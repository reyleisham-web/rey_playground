Chapter 35 Part 26


“I’ll make some soup tomorrow,” Shen Xuanqing said. “It’s easy to feed. It isn’t like fish—the moment you leave a bucket of them in mud and water, they might keep moving for a while. I used to raise them in a wooden tub.” He searched the river again, talking to Lu Gu. Stewing loach was indeed delicious and nourishing, so he had to make it for his fulang.

As they spoke, the fish in the river grew tipsy, no longer hiding at the bottom but swimming toward the surface, tails wagging as if drunk. This method of catching fish was called “drunk fish”; some even turned pale, looking like dead fish.

Probably because there was no one to fish here, there were quite a lot of fish. Lu Gu’s eyes widened as he watched the surface of the water. The weather this year had been so good and beautiful that even the aquatic plants were luxuriant, and the autumn fish were fatter. The net was heavy to drag up, and Shen Xuanqing himself had to exert a great deal of effort.

In the fishing net on the shore, the sober fish writhed and slapped their tails on the ground, but the drunk ones didn’t respond—because the water area where the drug had been poured was broad, and the nets weren’t finished being set yet. Shen Xuanqing asked Lu Gu to go back and fetch wooden barrels, bamboo baskets and deep bamboo baskets. He leaped into the river, caught fish, and threw them onto the bank.

Lu Gu ran back in a hurry, brought the things, and returned with a rush, full of joy and bustle. The dog barked at the swaying fat fish; seeing so many fish on the shore, the joy of harvesting a large catch showed on his face. For a moment he could only sense the beauty of the mountains and rivers—the wind at his ears while running felt carefree and joyful.

Shen Xuanqing scooped fishes from the water with a bamboo basket, the river water seeping out from under the basket. Lu Gu also fished from the water with a larger, deeper bamboo basket. Each time a basket went into the water and came out, several fish fluttered in it.

Today’s harvest exceeded Shen Xuanqing’s expectations. Hundreds of fish were dumped onto the shore. Besides grass carp, there were many expensive crucian carp and river eel, especially river eel—the autumn fattening season—great for stewing with tofu, a dish many townspeople liked to make.

He had never caught fish this way in the mountains before. The journey back would be long, and the fish could die more easily if kept out of water. The Qingxi River flowed all the way down the mountain, and fish could be caught anywhere. He hadn’t expected to work so hard and haul a cartload of fish back to sell.

Just now so many fish had appeared before him that, still young, he couldn’t help but get excited and plunged into the river, throwing the fish onto the bank. When he calmed down and looked at the glossy fish on the shore, he decided to go after river eels—better and more expensive fish. Such a haul could fetch a lot of money. It was still early in the morning, and he could go down the mountain directly.

Lu Gu had thought they would be back in two days, but now that he announced they would go down the mountain today, as a subordinate he worked even harder. Shrimps and small fish were caught in the dense net, and Lu Gu threw the small fish back into the river. The remaining white-shelled river prawns were so big and plentiful that they filled more than half of the fish basket.

To avoid wasting so many fish, they used all three wooden barrels and two bamboo baskets to load the fish onto the cart, locked the doors, and started down the mountain. River eels were as easy to feed as loaches, and they were placed in the bamboo baskets. The cheapest grass carp were also in the bamboo basket, while the rest of the more expensive fish were in buckets. Even if the grass carp died after moving two hours down the mountain road, it would be no loss. Today’s fresh fish wouldn’t be feared as dead or stinky fish. Because of the large quantity, they could be sold for many copper coins even at a low price, making this a hard-won gain.

Although they were in a hurry, both of them were inexplicably happy. Even if this cartload of fish couldn’t be sold for five or eight taels, two taels of silver would still be very good.

Shen Xuanqing, who earned money from hunting, could still bring a few hares down the mountain to exchange for copper coins when luck wasn’t with him, so two taels of silver was no small sum. Two taels of silver was pretty good.

To make things easier for Shen Xuanqing, Lu Gu shouldered the fish basket and a bamboo basket on his back, and he placed the heavy bamboo basket on the cart after walking a certain distance. The road was rough, and a lot of water sloshed in the wooden barrels. Fortunately, most of the roads they walked were not far from the river, and they could replenish some water as they went.

That unexpected joy, that gladdening astonishment, stayed with Lu Gu. Seeing his fulang’s eyes shining, he seemed so happy that Shen Xuanqing also felt good; fatigue meant nothing, and he could still move.

Eighteen years old was young and full of drive. They headed down the mountain in such a reckless manner, and when they arrived at the door, Wei Lanxiang was surprised to see the cart full of fish. Seeing them out of breath, it seemed they had rushed here; she was a little dumbfounded. She couldn’t lift such a heavy load; she hurried them home to rest.

Seeing so many fish, Ji Qiuyue hurried to the field to sell the fish while they were still fresh, and asked her husband to pull the cart into town to sell the fish.
