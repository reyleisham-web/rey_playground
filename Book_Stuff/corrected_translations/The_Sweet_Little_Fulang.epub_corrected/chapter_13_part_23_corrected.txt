13 Part 23

Unlike the hawkers who plied the streets and alleys, they woke early and found a quiet corner in the market to wait, selling their wares to collect the money. Officials from the yamen would come to collect the income. Now that peace had flourished, there were occasional miscreants; such posts were still needed here. The yamen was still fairly orderly, with no corrupt officials or grand dignitaries rampaging to oppress the common people. If you paid three pennies, you could secure a place in the morning set.

After the man who collected the money left, Shen Xuanqing tucked away the half-sized wooden plaque that had been handed over. If you had a wooden plaque, it would be easier; otherwise there would be too many people selling things and it would be hard to keep track. This wooden plaque was a pair with three numbers on it: one, seven and five, written in vermilion lacquer. He only had one of them in his hand. When he left, he would return the wooden plaque and match it with the other one in the yamen’s hand, then make a sign on the plaque as the roster.

Lu Gu was still breathing shallowly, his cheeks warm. Seeing him like this, Shen Xuanqing moved a small stone from the corner to let him sit.

This was a seller who had been around a long time, squatting and standing, tired his legs, so he made something. No one wanted to carry such a stone back and forth. Whoever came to sell something would first pick it up and sit down; when he left, someone else would use it.

“Sit and rest for a while,” Shen Xuanqing said to Lu Gu, then he stepped aside and called out, “Come and see—the rabbits and deer that were just brought in.”

Farmers were seldom bashful about selling what they had; you could only eat if you had money, so most could sell with a loud voice, and Shen Xuanqing was no exception. His voice wasn’t too loud or sharp, but his physique stood behind the prey, and everyone knew that he was a hunter. Still a young hunter with steady eyes and a handsome look. Although he was darker and thinner, his face was handsome, and his bone structure was good. Standing there, such a tall and handsome man drew extra glances.

It was such a handsome face, but Lu Gu didn’t dare to look at it, and he didn’t even have that awareness. He only had a cold, vague impression of Shen Xuanqing. It was the recent interactions that made him know that Shen Xuanqing wouldn’t hit people easily, so what he had thought of as “fierce and cold-hearted” before now only left out the “fierce.”

As he was shouting, a middle-aged man approached, looking to be in his forties, with a small beard on his chin; his slender face seemed shrewd at first glance. “How about the deer?”

Seeing that he was dressed neatly and thinking that he was the manager of a wealthy family in town, Shen Xuanqing half-squatted and replied, “I beat it; it’s still hot, it just died. If you buy it back today, the meat will be the freshest.”

“Isn’t this deer injured?” The middle-aged man touched the deer a few times; it was still warm, thinking the hunter wasn’t trying to fool anyone.

“The wild boars chased a group of deer but didn’t catch them. One of them fell off a cliff and broke its spine. After wandering the mountains these days, I happened to trap and catch it,” Shen Xuanqing said, not changing his face, with a hint of regret in his tone.

The wild boars were ferocious and hard to hunt. He had set traps by chance. If he could catch and kill them, he could sell them for money, and it would be safer in the mountains. You couldn’t tell people that the prey was picked up for nothing, or the price would be driven down.

The deerskin was still intact and uninjured. The middle-aged man examined the deer’s belly, then stood up and asked, “How much?”

Shen Xuanqing also stood up and replied, “Eight taels.”

The middle-aged man widened his eyes slightly: “You’re bold—dare to ask for that price? Eight taels for this?”

“Eight taels is already cheap. A doe can fetch thirty or forty taels. This deer is at least four months old, not less than twenty-five pounds.” Shen Xuanqing negotiated calmly.

The middle-aged man didn’t back down, but asked, “How much for the doe versus this? You’ll need eight taels for these few pounds of meat. Five taels; if you can do that, I’ll pay you on the spot. I don’t need to go back to fetch the money.”

This was quite lavish, and to carry so much silver with him, the middle-aged man looked a little smug; five taels of silver was indeed not a small amount.

“You’re perceptive. Although the doe is heavy, the meat isn’t as tender as a young deer. You and the master at your place should know this best. The deer in the mountains are quick and agile, and a doe with a cub isn’t easy to coax out, and it’s usually impossible to shoot a young deer, let alone taste it.”

“People often say that meat from a cub just turned one month old is tender, and the city ladies and gentlemen love it. We don’t know what that is like, but a four-month-old young deer is certainly more tender, so how can the price match an old deer?”

Doing business was all about being courteous and making money. Shen Xuanqing spoke calmly and politely, his words clear, neither humble nor arrogant. Seeing that the middle-aged man hesitated, he added, “If you’re worried this deer isn’t uninjured, I think you’re considering it carefully. This deer's skin is so complete that it’s enough to make a pair of deerskin boots for the mansion, and it’s suitable to wear outside as well.”

“Well, you know what I think,” the middle-aged man nodded with a calm expression, as if he didn’t care much, but he was thinking.

“The price is still too high; make it lower,” he told Shen Xuanqing.

“Eight taels of silver is already cheap. If you hadn’t asked, I would have sold it for ten taels. If you buy it, I’ll throw in this rabbit as a snack.” Shen Xuanqing seized the biggest, fattest rabbit—the size far surpassed the other two—demonstrating his sincerity.

The result of a good bargain isn’t just squeezing the price; it’s also adding something as a compliment. Besides, this rabbit was a gift for the middle-aged man, which put a bigger smile on his face, though he pretended to think it over. In the end, he produced eight taels of silver and handed them to Shen Xuanqing, displaying his wealth and status before others, then he carried away the deer and the rabbit.

Of course, Shen Xuanqing didn’t know that after he left, the master of the mansion would report that he had bought it back for ten taels.
