Chapter 26 Part 14


After leaving the market, they handed over the wooden sign, and the two of them walked toward the east of the town, arriving at the medical clinic first. The people at the medical clinic inspected the quality of the goji berries, tasted them, and bought them all at the market price of three copper coins. When they were leaving, the man from the shop told them that if there were any more such goods, they should come again, and he would buy as many as they could bring.

This undoubtedly surprised Lu Gu. Very few people ventured into the deep mountains and forests. He was the only one who picked those wild goji berries by himself. He never imagined he would be so lucky.

Because the goods were dry and he had dried them himself, there were many goji berries in the mountains, but not everywhere. The rest in the bamboo basket weighed ten catties.

There were too many copper coins to carry conveniently, so Shen Xuanqing asked the staff to exchange all the copper coins for silver, and five taels of silver went into Lu Gu’s hands. Not to mention the copper coins left in the cloth bag, Lu Gu clutched the broken silver tightly, his steps faltering, and he stared at Shen Xuanqing in a daze as they left the clinic.

“What are you staring at like that?” Shen Xuanqing asked with a smile.

Lu Gu woke as from a dream, slipping the broken silver into his sleeve, where the inner pocket seemed suddenly precious and heavy. Then they turned toward a pork shop on another street. Shen Xuanqing met a butcher named Zhao, and they finally agreed that Zhao would give Shen Xuanqing five taels of silver in exchange for the slaughtered sheep’s skin and a hind leg.

Compared with pork and chicken, mutton—nourishing and suitable for cooking with medicinal herbs—was more expensive. When pork cost twenty copper coins per catty, mutton could be sold for copper coins when prices were low, but sheep were not as fat and large as pigs, so their meat naturally yielded less. This black-hoofed sheep was a small sheep, so five taels of silver was considered fair, and Butcher Zhao stood to profit as well.

The two of them had always been on good terms, so they struck a plan: boil a large pot of water, sharpen their knives, and slaughter the sheep in the courtyard. Shen Xuanqing was a hunter and knew how to skin a sheep; killing pigs and sheep was not difficult for him, and he wanted the sheep skin, so he handled the knife skillfully.

Lu Gu and Mrs. Zhao’s wife, Ji Shi, were sitting in the shop. Ji Shi was holding their one-year-old fat boy, who drooled constantly. Ji Shi’s body was a bit plump; she loved to talk and laugh, and the child wouldn’t sit still, insisting on being carried around. She gossiped with Lu Gu about their little tiger, and she complained about the difficulties of raising children. Lu Gu listened in a low voice from time to time, his eyes lingering on the little tiger’s plump face; the little tiger giggled at him, and he couldn’t help but laugh too.

Seeing the wild soapberries in the bamboo basket on the ground beside the chair, Ji Shi asked, “Are you buying these wild soapberries to use, or to sell?”

Working in a butcher shop, it was inevitable that you’d get greasy hands, so you washed them often. Although Zhao the butcher’s family was rich enough to afford the fragrant bath beads from the powder shop, it felt like a waste to spend them on hand-washing, so they often bought some wild soapberries to rub and wash their hands with.

Knowing that Ji Shi wanted to buy them, Lu Gu saw there were only about a dozen left. After hearing Shen Xuanqing talk to Butcher Zhao, he was a little familiar with Zhao and thought for a moment before saying, “Sister-in-law, you can have them. You don’t need to pay me.”

“Hey, why are you like this? It’s not easy for you to pick these,” Ji Shi smiled and urged him to take them.

Lu Gu said again, “There are only a few left. It’s nothing.”

The ten berries were sold for three copper coins. He had earned a lot of copper coins today, not to mention Ji Shi; if he returned to the village and met people there, he would give them away. These things were picked in the mountains and cost nothing, so it didn’t matter if a few were given away.

“If that’s the case, I’ll accept your kindness. If you come to the town later, stop by for a visit if you’re free,” Ji Shi said with a wide smile, and she helped Lu Gu refill the tea.

The two chatted and laughed for a while, teasing the little tiger. After a while, because someone came to buy meat, Lu Gu helped Ji Shi hold the child. The little tiger was heavy, and when Lu Gu held him, his chubby little hands stretched out toward Ji Shi, who was chopping meat, whimpering as if talking to her.

Six years ago, Du Hehua gave birth to Lu Wu. Lu Gu was about twelve at the time. Whenever she ate, Du Hehua asked him to hug Lu Wu; she couldn’t eat well on her own, so holding the baby was familiar to him. But for Shen Xuanqing, he had never seen it before; his eyes paused slightly, and his expression looked unusual.

Butcher Zhao came in from the yard with him, weighing the meat for Ji Shi. After the work was done, Ji Shi took the children from Lu Gu, and Shen Xuanqing said goodbye. He had intended to buy a piece of meat to repay the half he borrowed from the villagers yesterday and to have some at home, but Zhao Butcher confiscated the money.

After they went out, Shen Xuanqing pushed the empty cart more easily. On it rested the bamboo basket, the sheep’s skin, and the sheep’s legs. The legs were wrapped in the sheep’s skin so they wouldn’t be exposed. When they got home, they stewed it with nourishing herbs.

It was noon and the sun was bright. Fortunately, it was autumn and not too hot. Lu Gu was thinking about the money he had earned today; perhaps after he went back, Shen Xuanqing would give him ten copper coins. But even if he didn’t, there was nothing to complain about: he had eaten well and slept well at the Shen household. Today Shen Xuanqing even bought green plum wine for him to drink, and that was more than enough.

The two of them walked out of town and toward an alley where some people were gathered, clearly trying to join in the fun. They could hear crying and a few curses from inside. When they drew closer, they could piece together a rough picture from the gossip.

It turned out there was a maid in the alley who was about to be sold by the Zhang family. They claimed her hands and feet were not clean in the courtyard. The maid had an affair with the Zhang family’s eldest son, whose wife had died. She wanted to become his wife, so Mrs. Zhang planned to sell her off.

What exactly happened wasn’t clear, but Lu Gu was surprised to learn that a purchased person could be sold again. He had been living in Anjia Village and had visited the town several times. He had only heard that people in the countryside sold their sons and daughters. As for what happened after the sale, he did not know.

Looking at Shen Xuanqing again, Lu Gu’s eyes showed a hint of fear.
