Chapter 45 Part 30


Country folks named dogs by their colors—mostly huang (黄, yellow), hui (灰, gray), and hei (黑, black)—or sometimes Wangcai (旺财: prosperous wealth). Lu Gu reminded Shen Xuanqing to tell the pup to be good just now, and he thought to himself that this pup was really tenacious, so it would be better to call him Guaizi (乖仔: obedient, well-behaved cub), so that he would be more compliant.

“Guaizi, Guaizi—call him that first,” Shen Xuanqing said, not giving much thought to a proper name. The pup had been raised in the countryside, and as he grew up he would just be called Guaizi. He had never studied, so it wouldn’t be convenient to call him by a polite name.

Guaizi—Lu Gu said the name, hugged the pup, and called out Guaizi, but the pup was busy biting at his sleeve and did not respond to the name.

Shen Xuanqing saw it and said, “He’ll know after barking for a while.”

“Yes.” Lu Gu nodded, his sleeves slick with drool, and he set the pup down.

It was You shi (5–7 pm), the sun slowly setting toward the west; a fire was lit in the yard, and the firewood crackled. Lu Gu and Ji Qiuyue sat in front of the small fire, roasting some autumn rice fishes.

Shen Yan skewered a slightly larger one with a clean wooden branch, rubbed its belly with crushed prickly ash and chili powder, and leaned forward to roast it as well. This was what Shen Yaoqing had asked for—he said he often ate stewed fish and was tired of it, so he wanted something different. Country folk sometimes catch fish in the river, and they were reluctant to fry or stew them with salt, so they simply built a fire by the river and ate them casually to fill their stomachs. They roasted fish at home as well, but if they came out bland, they would add pepper powder and chili powder as Shen Yaoqing instructed. The autumn rice fish hadn’t been eaten for two days, so they killed them to prevent spoilage. They salted them with coarse salt, and now that they were baked on the fire, there was no need to spread salt.

They grilled for a while, and Ji Qiuyue saw Shen Yan’s fish had turned black and quickly said, “It’s burnt—lift it up.”

The spicy, numbing aroma from the grilled fish was tempting, so she said, “Sister-in-law, let me taste to see whether it’s done,” but she couldn’t hold back from Shen Yan’s greed.

“It’s quite delicious on the outside.” Shen Yan tore off a piece of the burnt fish meat; it wasn’t completely black, and it was crispy with the skin, the saltiness adequate, and the pepper and chili added a pleasant numb and slightly spicy kick.

“It’s fine—eat it while you roast, and you’ll finish it after it’s roasted,” Ji Qiuyue teased with a smile.

Wei Lanxiang came out of the room when she heard the commotion, and was glad to see them talking and laughing; she brought over a stool and roasted one for herself as well.

Shen Yaoqing had caught a lot of autumn rice fishes in the field and eaten the rest today, so they didn’t take up space in the kitchen; if he wanted fish later, he would go to the river to catch fresh ones.

The pup smelled the scent and approached; Lu Gu was afraid the fire would burn it, so he stepped the pup back with his foot, but luckily it didn’t leap into the flames—it must have learned to fear the power of the fire.

Wei Lanxiang glanced outside and said, “Daqing and the others should be back.”

“Yes, when they went out, Daqing said he would be back around this time,” Ji Qiuyue said, turning the wooden sticks so the fish cooked evenly.

Although Lu Gu didn’t speak, he also glanced toward the door.

Shen Xuanqing went to town to sell the new rice. The two brothers—one pulling the cart and the other pushing it behind—managed it more easily than one person alone.

While they talked, Dahui, who had been lying down asleep, raised his head and barked at the door twice, then ran out. Wei Lanxiang knew at a glance that her sons had returned.

Shortly after, Shen Yaoqing came in with an empty cart, smelling the grilled fish, and said with a smile, “Coincidentally, I just happened to be back.”

Ji Qiuyue laughed at him, “Just worry about your greed.”

Shen Xuanqing followed, coming in from behind; he immediately met Lu Gu who was looking outside the door. Although the money from selling rice had nothing to do with him—it belonged to the eldest brother—the family had a good harvest, and his fulang was waiting for him at home, so he was in a good mood.

Shen Yaoqing took care of the land—watering, weeding, and fertilizing; he was mostly busy, Wei Lanxiang and Shen Yan were at home, and he and his wife tended to it more, so the harvest naturally rested in his hands, and the Shen family had no objections.

At that moment, he didn’t say much about money when he came back from selling rice. At night, he would pay Wei Lanxiang some money for the old lady’s public funds and save some for himself, so life would gradually improve.

“Go wash quickly, take the steamed buns and put them on the mat, this fish you can eat with the skin,” Wei Lanxiang told them.

The two brothers washed their hands and faces with water. Wei Lanxiang turned her head, the joy on her face undiminished. The whole cart of rice had sold, and the price should be good.

She turned the fish in her hand, thought for a moment, and said with a smile, “Tomorrow, I’ll go to Da Chen’s house to pick some long beans, fry them with red sauce, and fry an egg. I’ve saved some eggs these days. Let’s steam dry rice tomorrow, and steam the new rice—try it out and be happy.”

The long beans fried in red sauce were salty and delicious, best served with dry rice. They would divide the sauce, mix it into the rice, and it would be very tasty; not to mention the steamed new rice—the grains were crystal clear and plump, and the steamed rice was mellow and soft, even plain rice without vegetables was delicious.

Lu Gu had never eaten this dish, so he didn’t know how delicious it would be. Shen Yan had eaten it twice, and at the end of the meal, she took a piece of steamed bun and wiped the remaining sauce from the bottom of the bowl, unwilling to waste it. It was also because she hadn’t eaten much good food in the past two years, which made her feel that this dish was the most delicious; but now hearing that they would eat it tomorrow, she swallowed greedily.
