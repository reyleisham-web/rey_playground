Lingjun couldn’t speak yet. He was upright on Shen Xuanqing’s thighs, his tiny feet propped against the man’s legs. Realizing an adult was talking to him, he tilted his head to listen. After Shen Xuanqing finished, the child offered two indistinct “uh-uh” sounds.

“You miss me? Dad knew our Lingjun is the most well-behaved.” Shen Xuanqing pretended to understand, answering his own question with a cheerful nod.

“Woof.” Guaizi lay obediently at Shen Xuanqing’s feet, lifting his head to glance between his master and Lingjun, as if trying to mimic the child’s speech.

Shen Xuanqing had grown used to speaking softly to the child; he didn’t alter his tone now as he comforted Guaizi with a laugh, “Alright, alright, you’re good too.”

Lingjun turned his head toward the dog’s sound, and Guaizi tilted his head, tail wagging.

At noon there had been half a bowl of stir-fried winter bamboo shoots with cured meat left, so Lu Gu had only stir-fried a bowl of cabbage to serve quickly.

No matter how simple the meals at home were, they could never compare with eating out. Shen Xuanqing ate five large steamed buns in one go. When he finished, comfortably full, he took a sip of hot tea and smiled, “Everything in the shop is sold out. The old chickens and ducks were sold at market price—doesn’t make much, but we didn’t lose money either. There were forty rabbits in total. In the first two days, because the price was lower, they sold fastest; seventeen were gone in just two days.”

He set the tea bowl down and lifted Lingjun into his arms again, saying, “The pork was sold cheaply, but with the initial capital, the rabbits still turned the most profit. In just eight days, the rabbits brought in over nine qian—almost a full tael of silver.”
[1 tael = 10 qian]

Hearing this, Lu Gu’s eyes brightened. By this calculation, the rabbits earned about one qian a day, which was really good.

The shop was small, naturally not earning as much as larger stores, which could bring in more in a day. Yet Lu Gu didn’t go out often, so he wouldn’t be too surprised by these numbers. They earned steadily and reliably—enough. There was no need to compare with others.

Besides what they sold themselves, every month they also delivered seven or eight, sometimes more than a dozen, rabbits to the brothel in Fenggu Town. Those rabbits were priced higher—forty-five copper coins each, so they made at least two or three qian a month from that alone.

“Right now, we sell live rabbits for thirty-eight copper coins each, no bargaining. Skinned ones go for twenty-five copper coins, also with no haggling. But that’s for walk-in customers. For Brother Yang’s tavern, since the rabbits aren’t as plump now as in autumn and winter, I charge him thirty copper coins per live rabbit. He wants to collect rabbit furs to sell, and he’s not an outsider anyway. Besides, he’s generous and straightforward; he mentioned this openly to me. Since he’s honest, I’m not going to be stingy either.”

“Thirty copper coins is a fair round number. He’s helped a lot; this is only fair.” Shen Xuanqing kissed his little shuang’er and added with a smile, “Other taverns and eateries pay two more copper coins—thirty-two copper coins per live rabbit. Restaurant owners don’t slaughter them immediately; most take them back to raise for a few days.”

Lu Gu listened carefully, memorizing the rabbit prices so he’d know how to sell them if he ever went to town.

Lingjun didn’t want to sit still any longer, so Shen Xuanqing had to stand and pace around the main hall with him in his arms. “Since there’s nothing left to sell in the shop, I thought I might as well close up and come back. Rest tonight and go again tomorrow.”

“Mm, rest tonight. Change your clothes later; I’ll wash them for you tomorrow.” Lu Gu asked with a smile, “Where’s the other set you changed out of?”

When they had returned from Jixing Town the other day, Shen Xuanqing had been wearing a different set of clothes.

“Got dirty the day before yesterday. When Shiniang came by and saw it, she had me change, then washed it for me in the backyard,” Shen Xuanqing replied.

Lu Gu nodded in understanding. Since Shen Xuanqing had to slaughter livestock in the shop and meat scraps would splatter during chopping, he’d been wearing old clothes lately, even if they got stained with blood; it wasn’t a big loss.

Her second son had returned, so after finishing her work at the new house, Wei Lanxiang hurried back to the old home. Only after ensuring he had eaten did she relax.

The wind blew outside, and Lingjun’s skin was still tender; Shen Xuanqing couldn’t bear to take him out. He kept pacing the main hall and said when Wei Lanxiang returned, “Mother, later, could you borrow Auntie Gu’s loom to weave some hemp cloth? We can use it to cover the meat on the racks. It’s fine now, but when the weather heats up, if flies and bugs land on the meat, it’ll look filthy. Make the covers for the pork and mork (mutton) longer, and cut smaller covers for the chickens, ducks, and rabbits.”

“Of course. That’s no trouble at all. I’ll go to Auntie Gu’s tomorrow to weave,” Wei Lanxiang agreed readily, then asked, “How did you eat these past few days? Did you go to your master’s place?”

Shen Xuanqing smiled. “Mostly like that. Master would come by my place every day when he had nothing to do. At mealtime, either he or Shiniang would call me over. I also ate at the noodle shop a few times. I didn’t cook for myself, so later I thought, why not just move the rice, flour, and vegetables to master’s place?”

Since he needed to eat in town, Wei Lanxiang had packed him a sack of rice and a sack of milled white flour. Though he couldn’t knead dough well, he could at least make rice porridge. The butcher shop never lacked meat, and for vegetables, he had taken ten cabbages and a small jar of pickled vegetables—more than enough to last a while.

Young men could be like this—uninterested in cooking—so it wasn’t unusual.

Wei Lanxiang thought it was fine and said, “From now on, just go there to eat. Don’t stand on ceremony.”

She added, “Ask your Shiniang to steam some buns for you to take back to the shop. If you get hungry at night, there are the pickled vegetables too—better than going to bed on an empty stomach.”

As a mother, there wasn’t much more she could do to help, so she could only fuss over his meals and clothes.
