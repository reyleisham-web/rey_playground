Chapter 73


The chickens and ducks came down from the mountain, a little wilted from the rough journey these past two days. Lu Gu didn’t let them out immediate­ly. He steadied himself, then went out to prepare their food. He chopped up half-dried grass and mixed it with chaff, feeding it to the chickens and ducks raised at home.

When he stepped out of the backyard, he saw Wei Lanxiang return with Shen Yan, carrying a small bag of borneol and shortbread in a basket.

In the cold weather, Wei Lanxiang often carried a small cloth pad in the basket, the cotton sewn from old clothes. She used the pad today to wrap the shortbread, and it was still warm when they returned.

“Here, take these for you and your sister-in-law.” Wei Lanxiang opened the oiled paper bag, took two pastries, and handed them to Shen Yan.

Seeing Lu Gu come out from the backyard, she smiled and said, “Gu Zi, wash your hands and come to eat.”

“Got it, mother.” Lu Gu’s sleeves were rolled up from when he was chopping grass for the birds; now he lowered them after washing his hands.

Seeing Shen Xuanqing killing the last badger and Shen Yaoqing trimming the fat from the badger to render oil later, Wei Lanxiang went into the kitchen and took out the borneol, placing it on the edge of the chopping board. She said, “Stash it in the basket; you two can take it after you finish.”

“Okay.” Shen Yaoqing agreed, his hands still busy cutting fat meat. When he saw the puppy rubbing against his leg, he smiled and said, “It’s not cooked yet; you’ll eat it later.”

Fat from badgers could be used to refine oil, and the lean meat could also be eaten, but their family didn’t care much for badger meat. Anyway, they had dogs at home, and a dozen portions of badger meat would be enough for them for a while, and it would help fatten them through the winter.

When Lu Gu entered the main room, he first glanced at Ji Qiuyue’s room. Wei Lanxiang was joking with her. When he saw Shen Yan coming out, he lifted the small cloth pad from the basket and was about to take it, but he heard Shen Yan say, “Brother Guzi, take one for mother too.”

Taking out three shortbreads, Lu Gu followed Shen Yan into the east room and sat on the edge of the bed to eat them. Except for Ji Qiuyue, who ate two, everyone else ate one.

Wei Lanxiang noticed the thin red cloth on the bed that hadn’t been put away and, as she ate, said, “I’m in a hurry today; I forgot to buy another red cloth. It’s hot in the summer. Make a few bellybands for the baby to wear, otherwise the baby will get prickly heat, and a belt or bellybands would keep it cooler.”

Ji Qiuyue, who had just had a child, was pleased to see the red cloth being turned into clothes and nodded; it should be done.

Lu Gu sat beside them, mouth full of soft shortbread, feeling very content. Shen Yan leaned over and whispered, “Brother Guzi, I beg you to do something.”

No one had ever spoken to him like that before. Lu Gu was a little nervous, thinking that something had happened to Shen Yan, so he could only nod quickly around the food in his mouth.

Shen Yan scratched her head with her other hand, looked a bit embarrassed, and smiled flatteringly. “The new jacket my mother made for me is ready; can you embroider two flowers on it for me?”

“I’m just learning to embroider. It’s okay to embroider on a handkerchief, but if the embroidery on the clothes is damaged, Caixia and the others will laugh at me.” She took a big bite of shortbread, frowned, and looked very sad.

So it was for this, Lu Gu relaxed a little, frowning slightly as he replied, “Okay.”

Wei Lanxiang overheard their exchange; Shen Yan lowered her head and muttered, asking curiously, “What are you talking about with your brother Guzi?”

Having been criticized twice for her embroidery, and now asking Lu Gu to embroider for her, Shen Yan was afraid of being scolded again. “Mother, it’s nothing; I’m going to ask Brother Guzi about something.”

“This little girl still can’t tell the truth to her mother,” Wei Lanxiang teased with a smile, not pressing the matter, and turned to Ji Qiuyue to talk about putting a longevity lock on the child. The two of them chattered on while Shen Yan watched Lu Gu finish his shortbread, then stood up and signaled him to follow.

In the kitchen, Shen Yaoqing boiled the fat meat to render oil, while Shen Xuanqing tidied the yard, shoveled the soil, and covered the blood splashed outside the basin to keep their soles clean.

There were sixteen badger skins in total. He considered it: in the winter wind, when no one could go outside, it would be better to leave six pieces to make fur collars for everyone in the family—the fur would be light and warm around the necks, keeping everyone comfortable. The rest, ten skins, could simply be sold raw as hides. The price might be a little lower, but that would do.

Seeing Lu Gu come out to wash his hands again, and Shen Yan still following him, Shen Xuanqing smiled and asked, “What are you two doing?”

Shen Yan still tried to dodge: “It’s nothing, second brother. You should be busy with your work.”

“Brother Guzi, I’ll go fetch it first,” Shen Yan said after washing her hands.

“Okay.” Lu Gu agreed, washed off the white foam from his hands, and stood to wipe them. When he saw Shen Xuanqing smiling at him, he paused and found no one around, so he whispered, “Shen Yan asked me to make a few flower embroideries on her new coat.”

He didn’t think it would be a secret that couldn’t be shared; it wouldn’t hurt to tell Shen Xuanqing, and the family would see the embroidery later anyway.

“No wonder,” Shen Xuanqing replied with a knowing smile. He knew his sister all too well. Then he teased, “If you’re cold, go sit on the bed for a while—the pot is occupied right now, and you can’t boil water for tang pozi.”

“Yes.” Lu Gu nodded.

The chimney’s smoke drifted up through the chimney; the Shen family’s courtyard buzzed with chatter and laughter, punctuated by the occasional dog’s bark. The days since coming down from the mountain were still steady and pleasant.

The next morning, after eating two hot steamed buns and a small piece of salted lump, Shen Xuanqing poured a small half of the wild soapberries from the sack into his big bamboo basket; the rest would be enough for his own use. Shen Yaoqing, who was chopping firewood, noticed him tidying up and asked, “Going to town?”

He pulled out a needle and thread to make children’s clothes, scratched his head with a shy smile, and handed some money from his pocket, speaking in a low voice: “If you go, buy me a silver hairpin at Jinyinxuan. I’m quite optimistic about it. Last time I didn’t have enough money to buy it. It’s a phoenix-tail hairpin inlaid with small silver beads. If someone already bought this one, there’s another phoenix tail there that’s pretty too. Anyway, buy one and come back.”

“Okay.” Shen Xuanqing took the money and tucked it into his pocket, his eyes pausing briefly as if considering something.
