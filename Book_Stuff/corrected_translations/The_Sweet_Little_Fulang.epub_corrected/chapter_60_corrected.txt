Chapter 60


As before, when they returned, Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing sat down to rest, while Ji Qiuyue busied herself with cooking, and Wei Lanxiang brought them something to eat first.

Wei Lanxiang carried out a small bamboo tray. There were dried red dates, persimmons, and peanuts. She set the tray down for the two of them to eat, and said to Lu Gu, “In addition to what our family grows, there are mountain persimmons you picked too. Some of them are turned into sun-dried persimmons—cut and sun-dried—they’re sweet. Taste them first, don’t eat too much, save your stomach for when you eat later.” She worried the two youngsters would be greedy, so she gave them a careful warning about digestion.

They had plenty of persimmons. Last time Lu Gu came back, he picked a lot of hard mountain persimmons, as well as the persimmons at home. Dried persimmons could last a long time. In winter, they would have sweet, chewy snacks to eat.

While they were talking, Shen Yan carried in a basket of grass. When she heard the dog barking while threshing the grass behind the house, she knew Lu Gu and the others had returned. She stepped inside, her almond eyes curved in a smile, and she shouted, “Second brother, ger Guzi, you’re back.”

Shen Xuanqing replied to her, and when Lu Gu saw her coming and wanted to touch the dried persimmon with her dirty hands, he quickly held it out to her mouth. Shen Yan didn’t bother with manners; she opened her mouth, took a bite, smiled, and began chewing, then set the bamboo basket down. “Girls, you have to wash your hands,” Wei Lanxiang scolded lightly, though the smile didn’t leave her face, so one could not hear any real rebuke in her words.

Shen Yan scooped up a basin of water and squatted down to wash her hands. She saw the dried fish on the cart and said, “Brother Guzi, you two have dried so many dried fish.”

Lu Gu peeled a peanut and replied, “Well, save a little for us to eat in the winter.”

The Shen family had suffered through hard winters in the past two years, and Shen Yan was young and afraid of the cold and hunger. Hearing this, her almond eyes widened, and she realized there would be no need to starve in winter. She nodded eagerly and said, “Alright, we’ll eat them in winter, and if we want fish tomorrow, we’ll go to the river to catch some and save them all. I just saw Zhuzi and the others fishing in the river.”

Shen Xuanqing watched his younger sister’s expression and knew she’d suffered through hunger two years ago. Back then, the family’s rations were dire. Even when he and Shen Yaoqing sometimes saved a few mouthfuls for Shen Yan, it wasn’t enough. A wave of guilt washed over him, and he secretly sighed. Fortunately, this year they could eat enough and wear warm clothes, and none of the family had to suffer.

The same for Lu Gu; he didn’t have to endure hardship anymore. He turned his head to look at Lu Gu beside him and dropped a peeled peanut into Lu Gu’s hand. Even without Lu Gu asking, he knew life in Du Hehua’s hands must have been rough. He wondered if Lu Gu could even eat a few mouthfuls of hot rice.

“Shen Yan, come in and get a bowl of peppers,” Ji Qiuyue called from the kitchen; she was too busy chopping vegetables, so she asked Shen Yan to pick the peppers and light the fire.

Shen Yan ate another piece of dried persimmon before running in. Ji Qiuyue handed her a bowl and said, “Grab more; it will taste better.”

After a short rest, Shen Xuanqing got up and unloaded the cart. Lu Gu and Wei Lanxiang helped him. This time the catch didn’t seem large—eight live pheasants and rabbits—but in the big bamboo basket Shen Xuanqing carried, there were twenty-five pieces of fox fur, plus six pieces of miscellaneous fox fur. Among the twenty-five good furs, there were two silver fox skins, which were worth more than the red ones.

To hunt these fox furs, he had gone to many places in the mountains. Not only was he exhausted, but a pair of old shoes was worn out and couldn’t be mended easily. Those shoes were truly worn out, so they were simply thrown away.

Wei Lanxiang kept caressing the silver fox fur and said to Lu Gu, “I’ve heard people say that the lords and ladies in Fucheng love to wear silver fox fur. No matter how expensive it is, they can afford it.”

Shen Xuanqing thought about the shoes and asked, “Mom, I broke a pair of shoes. Do you have any more at home?”

“Yes, I’ve made you two pairs with thicker soles and one pair with ordinary soles. That should be enough for you to wear, and I’ll make another pair from the soles of your old shoes, enough for you to wear.”

Wei Lanxiang looked at Shen Xuanqing’s feet, wanting to mend them if she could.

Shen Xuanqing understood what she was thinking and said, “I threw away those pairs because they were too old. The soles were thin. I walked on the mountain roads; they were mended once. This time, the hole was so big it couldn’t cover the toes and stuck out. If it hadn’t been so close to the yard when it broke, I would have had a hard time walking.”

Wei Lanxiang nodded and replied, “It isn’t easy to mend; just throw them away, and mother will make you a few more.”

After Wei Lanxiang had looked over everything, Shen Xuanqing stuffed the fox fur back into the big bamboo basket and took it down to be sold in Fucheng another day. The price in Fucheng was higher than in Fenggu Town. When they went to the town to sell them, Lu Gu brought out two handfuls of wild soapberries to keep at home. They would last a while, so Wei Lanxiang and the others wouldn’t have to go back up the mountain to gather them.

In the kitchen, Ji Qiuyue stir-fried vegetables, letting them sizzle in the wok with a popping sound, cooking them quickly; the fried vegetables were fragrant, a distinct difference from boiled ones. In addition to a plate of fried vegetables, because Shen Xuanqing loved eggs, Ji Qiuyue also scrambled eggs for them, along with a bowl of oily and spicy fried peppers. Shen Xuanqing broke off a steamed bun and wiped it clean.

The steamed buns—white flour, soft and delicious—were served to them. The buns bore a bright yellow tint from egg residue and oil that had seeped to the bottom. Shen Xuanqing didn’t eat them, so she handed them to Lu Gu. The oil had soaked into the buns, making them salty and delicious, with a touch of egg, and the aroma was wonderful.

The morning market was still crowded, and there were few carts to pull things today, so Shen Xuanqing appeared truly relaxed. Small wild animals like pheasants and rabbits sold easily, and the wild soapberries were quick to move as well. In the end there were two pheasants and one rabbit left. Shen Xuanqing suspected the medical center had opened, so he pushed the cart toward the rice shop first.

They planned to ask the owner if he wanted wild game; if he did, they’d sell it to him, and then they’d go to the medical center to sell the goji berries, so they could go home and rest without standing around waiting for buyers.

There were still some wild soapberries left, and Shen Xuanqing cried out as he pushed the cart forward, “Wild soapberries, look at these big ones. They’re five coins for a string, quite cheap.” After his shout, someone actually told them to stop. Now, even if you bought with copper coins, you’d get three copper coins back.

As they walked forward, Lu Gu also called out. His eyebrows were up in a gentle, amused arch, and although his voice was a little softer, his face had filled out with color and looked better than before. His smile was different now; aside from a bit of timidity, the shy, cowering look from before had vanished.

In addition to rice, the rice shop bought all sorts of dried beans, miscellaneous grains, and goji berries, but Shen Xuanqing didn’t open the lid of the goji berry basket; it was two copper coins per catty, so not worth selling there, so they instead showed their wild game to the owner of the rice shop. There were only two pheasants and a rabbit in total, and the rice shop owner bought them together with the owner of the grocery store next door, even purchasing some of Lu Gu’s wild soapberries. Before leaving, Shen Xuanqing also bought some red beans and yellow rice. The family only grew chai beans; they could use red beans to make porridge, and then add some yellow rice to create mixed rice porridge. The pheasant he sold would be cheaper for the rice shop owner, so when he bought red beans and yellow rice, the shop owner scooped up a little extra for him.

After they went to the medical center to sell the goji berries, Lu Gu tucked the money bag into his pocket, and as soon as he came out, he heard Shen Xuanqing say they should buy Tang Pozi. His eyes lit up with anticipation and joy. He wanted a Tang Pozi too.
