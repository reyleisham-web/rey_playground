Chapter 67 Part 16


To guard against such beasts, deep and wide traps had been dug here. Even if rabbits, pheasants, or other small wild animals jumped into the traps, they were too light to stay on.

After a while, the grass and leaves laid over the traps dried up, but a thick layer of fallen leaves lay on top, blending with the ground around it, so Shen Xuanqing didn’t need to fuss about concealing it any longer.

There were many traps in the northern forest, and he paused before several to teach the puppy how to recognize them. Dahui gave a warning growl whenever the puppy’s thick front paws stepped onto a trap.

The puppy was also clever, with wet black eyes that blinked as he tilted his head, listening to Shen Xuanqing explain that he couldn’t stand on the trap. He lowered his head to sniff the ground, glanced over when Dahui growled, and then stepped back with a bark.

Seeing how unusually smart the young pup was and how quickly he learned, Shen Xuanqing quirked an eyebrow and chuckled. It seemed the two taels of silver had not been spent in vain. Back then, Dahui had cost only seven copper coins at the market.

Shen Xuanqing rested for five days after the rain, and when the weather cleared, he took his dogs out to hunt again. Lu Gu took the puppy outside to look for medicinal materials, and they brought back many of them. They even hauled back a bundled wormwood, separating the leaves with fine vines. When they returned, there was no need to sort them out—soaking the feet in winter would help keep out the cold. It was warm, and with so many people in the family, they had to prepare more.

He found a lot of shān zhū yú, which looked like medlars and were bright red. Picking them one by one would take too much effort, so he cut off the branches and carried them back. They dried them and sold them at the medical clinic; the price was not bad.

Lu Gu took the bamboo plaque and sat in the courtyard to pick shanzuyu fruit. Other grasses should be cut into sections, some leaves picked, some air-dried, and some sun-dried. Inevitably his hands grew dirty and black.

Although it wasn’t noon yet, hunger reminded him that it was time to cook. First he heated water to wash his hands. The mountains were getting colder, and the water was colder than before, so he thought of Tang Pozi.

There was the sound of chopping vegetables from the kitchen. Lu Gu was cooking, and the puppy was outside, gnawing a small piece of wood as prey. Shen Xuanqing had taken him into the forest to learn hunting, and now the pup was both having fun and improving his skills.

After he and the puppy shared a simple meal, Lu Gu cleaned the pot and filled it with clean water. The fire at the bottom of the stove hadn’t been completely extinguished, so he stuffed some straw into it, and it burned quickly.

After pouring the boiling water into Tang Pozi, it felt hot to the touch at first, and if he didn’t cover it with a quilt, it would cool down too quickly.

Lu Gu went to the box to rummage through cloth and cotton. This time he found some cotton and pieces of fabric. He had never endured the cold in the mountains, but Wei Lanxiang knew it, so she asked him to bring them up and sew two small, thin quilts to cover his legs, otherwise, when he sat and worked for long periods, his legs would grow cold and stiff if they didn’t move.

He took out the cotton and cloth, saw that the sun outside the window was brighter than before, and set the quilt out on a wooden rack to dry. Lu Gu unfolded the quilt, looked up at the blue sky, and thought that the weather would be good tomorrow. The bed would be exposed to the sun today and washed tomorrow, and a clean quilt would feel different and warmer.

The cotton Wei Lanxiang had brought was pressed flat at home; otherwise, the clumped cotton wouldn’t be easy to sew into the quilt. He added a thin piece of firewood to the bottom of the stove to keep the small fire burning slowly, pulled a chair over, and sat in the sun to sew the quilt. The cloth was cut, and the quilt, being small and thin, came together quickly.

After the small quilt was sewn, the water in the pot was already boiling. Lu Gu rubbed his neck, stood up, and poured the water into Tang Pozi first. He set Tang Pozi on the bench, folded the small quilt and covered it, then sat down to sew the second one, which was for Shen Xuanqing.

The puppy was running around the yard, trying to bite Qiucai again, but Lu Gu spotted him before he could bite, gave him a light scold, and the dog trotted over to rub Lu Gu’s legs.

After the two small quilts were sewn, Lu Gu spread one over his legs, even covering his calves a little, then placed Tang Pozi on his lap and unfolded the other quilt to cover Tang Pozi in the same way. Inside the quilt it was warm, and the thighs grew hot soon enough.

He couldn’t help but smile a little at such warmth in winter; the smile grew more content as he rested. After a while, he shifted the puppy away from the corner of the quilt, and Lu Gu carried Tang Pozi and the quilt back to the room to tidy up.

Tomorrow the bed would be taken apart and washed, so he would have to take the quilt out of the box and cover it at night. The wooden box containing the quilt was very large. Beneath the top quilt were several pieces of Shen Xuanqing’s clothing. After he took the quilt out, he found a book stuffed into the gap between the clothes and the box.

It had been under the quilt before, and Shen Xuanqing hadn’t told him when the book had been tucked away again. Lu Gu was puzzled, remembering that the last time Shen Xuanqing said he would teach him to read when he was free, and he wondered whether this book would be used for that. His mother had taught him, but he didn’t have any books to read, so he had only scribbled on the ground.

Perhaps he too would have books to read. Thinking of this, Lu Gu’s eyes brightened a little. He recalled that there were three characters on the book’s cover, and he wasn’t sure whether it was the Three Character Classic or the Thousand Character Classic that he had studied at school. Perhaps he would also have books to read in the ger.
