Chapter 95 Part 29


Du Hehua muttered a secret “bah,” then touched the silver bracelet on her wrist. It had been given to her by Lu Wen, so what did it matter if she was a concubine? Lu Wen, from her kin, wore gold and silver and lived in the town, while her son-in-law couldn’t compare to the muddy legs in the countryside. With that thought, the bitterness in her heart dissolved, and she squeezed herself into the crowd beside the village women who fawned on her.

When the opera began, Lu Gu didn’t notice what was happening to the others. He sat beside Wei Lanxiang and Shen Yan, watching the performance while munching cakes and sunflower seeds with obvious delight.

In the afternoon it was time to cook, and people left one after another. The play would be performed for three days, and as the weather grew cold, Wei Lanxiang stood up and said, “Go back first. We’ll come back tomorrow if we want to see it again.”

Lu Gu hadn’t taken a few steps when, as if he sensed something, he looked up and saw Shen Xuanqing looking at him from a short distance away. He instinctively wanted to go over, but restrained himself, and only the smile on his face showed his suddenly bright mood.

“Why are you here?” Wei Lanxiang wondered, knowing her second son didn’t care much for opera.

“Mother, Da Chen and the others said they’ll go to the town to watch the lanterns at night, so I’ll take Guzi to see them,” Shen Xuanqing approached and said.

So that was the reason. Wei Lanxiang replied, “These days the nights come early. You must be careful when you come back tonight.”

“It’s okay. There will be many people with us.” Shen Xuanqing handed the small bench and the empty basket in Lu Gu’s hands to Shen Yan, or else they would become a burden on the road.

The young people liked excitement, and Wei Lanxiang didn’t hinder them too much. She only warned them to be careful on the road, then went home with Shen Yan.

“We’ll have dinner in town.” Shen Xuanqing led Lu Gu in another direction, and soon they found Da Chen and the others. Lu Gu nodded at them. While watching the play, he ate cakes and sunflower seeds, so he wasn’t very hungry at the moment.

There were more men who would go to see the lanterns together, but with Shen Xuanqing by his side, he wasn’t afraid.

Wangli Village was a little closer to the town than Qingxi Village, but it was still dark when they arrived. They first ate Fuyuanzi (浮元子) at a roadside stall. It was sweet and glutinous, the soup hot, and after eating it Lu Gu didn’t feel cold. Even though there were eight balls of fuyuanzi in a bowl, Lu Gu wasn’t full, not to mention Shen Xuanqing’s appetite.

They came out to enjoy the night, so they ate whatever they wanted. Shen Xuanqing led Lu Gu to look and buy.

The lanterns along the long street began to light up one after another in brilliant colors. People gathered to guess lantern riddles. The unmarried girls and Shuang’ers, who rarely left home, also headed out to see the lanterns, their hands full of flower lanterns. They were at the age when onions were still tender and watery.

Lu Gu was dazzled by the myriad of lanterns, and he paused when Shen Xuanqing wanted to buy more. There were plenty at home already; why spend money on more?

At the lantern-riddle stall, the stall owner chatted away about the riddles, and Lu Gu paused involuntarily. Lantern riddles were an elegant pastime for a country boy. He had never guessed one before, and the riddle was difficult. He didn’t know the answer, and his eyes were blank.

It was Shen Xuanqing who thought for a moment and then whispered the answer into his ear. Encouraged by Shen Xuanqing, Lu Gu timidly told the answer to the stall owner. The stall owner glanced at him, nodded with a smile, and handed over a lantern.

Once the words were spoken, anyone who guessed correctly would receive a lantern. How could the stall owner break his promise with so many onlookers?

Lu Gu hadn’t expected his answer to be right. When he accepted the lantern, he felt a little flustered under the watchful gaze of others, and his heart was indescribably excited. The lantern itself was quite ordinary, not as exquisite as the ones Shen Xuanqing had bought at home. After all, the stall owner had to make a living and couldn’t give away something too precious for nothing.

“You guessed it right,” Lu Gu recovered from his joy and handed the lantern to Shen Xuanqing with a smile. He was so happy that his eyes shone; Shen Xuanqing knew it was worth coming tonight.

“Erqing, come and see, there’s a Phoenix Lantern,” Da Chen and his wife called out ahead. Shen Xuanqing smiled, took the lantern, and, with Lu Gu, hurried to the front.

They wandered from lantern to lantern and play until late. A dozen people gathered, counted the number of people, and then headed home.

The night thickened, but the full moon still lit the road, so it wasn’t truly dark. As they left the town, the light gradually faded. Lu Gu’s right hand, which had been hanging by his side, was suddenly grasped. He turned to look, but since there were others traveling with them, he tried to pull away. Shen Xuanqing lowered his voice. “It’s hard to walk at night. What if you get lost if you’re not careful?”

Lu Gu wanted to say he wasn’t a child and wouldn’t get lost, but the big hand holding his was too warm and strong, and he couldn’t speak. Fortunately, the darkness hid his cheeks from turning red.

The men in front were chatting and boasting; the night outside the town was lively, even in the dark.

The two fell behind, as if they had formed their own little world that no one could intrude upon.

Since they came down the mountain for the winter, there had been too many people in the family, making intimate moments difficult. Now that Shen Xuanqing could hold Lu Gu’s hand, he didn’t want to let go at all.
