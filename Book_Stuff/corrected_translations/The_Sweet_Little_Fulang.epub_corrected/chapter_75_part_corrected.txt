Chapter 75


Shen Xuanqing paused to think, and when he saw Lu Gu coming over with a broom, he asked, “Have you been keeping a good eye on things?”

Lu Gu didn’t quite understand why the question came up, but he answered honestly, “It’s been too long.”

Shen Xuanqing frowned slightly and replied, “Then pay more attention this year. Since we have Tang Pozi, use it often. Don’t think these few days aren’t so cold, but you mustn’t be careless. It’ll grow colder, and you must keep your body warm.”

Lu Gu, who had suffered frostbite for years, knew this truth well. He nodded, and because of Shen Xuanqing’s concern, he pursed his lips into a faint, almost shy smile.

“Isn’t there cotton at home? Sew thick tube sleeves these next few days, and boil water for cooking and washing vegetables,” Shen Xuanqing added.

“Okay.” Lu Gu agreed and began sweeping the sawdust on the ground into a pile so it wouldn’t accumulate later.

Shen Xuanqing started sawing wood again, neither fast nor slow. After that he picked up a long-handled axe, placed a log to the side, stood it on the ground, swung the axe down, and with the weight of the long axe, the wood split neatly in two.

“After washing your feet at night, you dry your legs, and then you can rub some badger oil on them to keep them from drying out.” Shen Xuanqing thought of this again and spoke as his hands continued to move.

Without waiting for Lu Gu to respond, he added, “I’ll fetch the old badger oil later. I left some last year and there’s still a little left. The old oil is better than new. There isn’t much, but it’ll do for a while.”

“Okay.” Lu Gu nodded, bent down, and erected another log.

The two of them worked—one sawing, the other splitting—and they chatted a few words from time to time. By late afternoon, there were only three large logs left.

Lu Gu was cooking dinner, and Shen Xuanqing put even more effort into cutting those three logs into long pieces. A year ago, steaming buns, stewed meat, and steamed meat were placed directly on the stove with a big blaze, so the fire burned quickly.

It grew dark early, and the work of sawing delayed dinner for a while; it was completely dark when they finally sat down to eat. Shen Yan lit a candle from the embers at the bottom of the stove, and the main room slowly brightened. The wick was short, and the sewing basket rested on the high table. She picked up a long needle and began to work.

After Shen Xuanqing finished the last piece of wood, Shen Yaoqing carried the three long logs into the woodshed. After a busy afternoon, his body was inevitably covered with dust and sawdust. Shen Xuanqing went into the main room, took the cloth strip hanging on the wall, and, when he came out, went to the kitchen door to wash his hands.

Lu Gu and Shen Yan placed the food on the table in turn, and when they finished tidying up, dinner was ready. A small candle flickered; though dim, it was enough to see what was on the table clearly.

Each person had a bowl of hot soup with pancakes. Wei Lanxiang had baked the pancakes two days ago, and they kept well, so she baked a little more. The pancakes were cut into thin slices, the white cabbage shredded, and the soaked dipicai and black mushrooms sliced thin.

Lu Gu dug up qiucai with green leaves from the yard, chopped it, heated oil in a pan, and tossed in a generous amount of vegetables, stir-frying them with a little oil. Then he added water and strips of pancakes to boil in one pan. The family loved spicy food, so he poured in chili oil until the sauce gleamed bright red, making the dish more appealing.

There was rice and vegetables in one bowl; in the countryside, people didn’t usually stir-fry vegetables, but tonight they cooked more, since one person could eat at least two bowls. The big pot was full, and Lu Gu had little space to cook, but he still prepared sour cowpeas—Wei Lanxiang had marinated them in hot and sour brine, which tasted a little spicy and very appetizing.

Lu Gu ate about one and a half bowls, and the second bowl nearly filled him; in total, he probably ate close to two bowls. He had chopped firewood in the afternoon, and it took a lot of effort. In winter, hot rice with just the right touch of chili oil and salt was especially satisfying. There were several other dishes as well, all appetizing.

After eating, the warmth spread through him; his hands and feet no longer felt cold. He felt comfortable and satisfied.

Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing ate more than the rest of the family, and in the end Lu Gu scooped rice for the two of them and even scraped the bottom of the pot clean—there was nothing left.

While cleaning the pot after eating, Shen Yan helped by adding some firewood to the bottom of the stove, otherwise the water for washing the pot would go cold. She also said, “Brother Guzi, your cooking skills are getting better and better. Tonight’s soup pancakes are even better than my mother’s.”

“It's just that there are many different vegetables,” Lu Gu replied, a touch of embarrassment in his smile.

He had once made soup pancakes for Shen Xuanqing when they were on the mountain, and Shen Xuanqing had said it was delicious. Today he had added several vegetables. Soup pancakes were commonly eaten in summer in the countryside, but they could be eaten in winter as well. Usually they used only white cabbage for the pancakes; it could be monotonous, and sometimes frying them in oil wasn’t done, but boiling them in plain water didn’t aroma as well.

At that moment, Shen Xuanqing had already found last year’s badger oil in the low house. He brought it to the kitchen and, overhearing Shen Yan’s words from outside, said, “When you have time, you can learn to cook from your brother Guzi—start with cutting vegetables or something.”

Shen Yan, the youngest in the family, had Wei Lanxiang once do the cooking; when Shen Yaoqing got married, Ji Qiuyue took over, and now that Shen Yan was pregnant, Lu Gu was still around, so she didn’t have to.

But she would marry someday, so embroidery skills weren’t essential. In peasant households, wives and daughters often could only sew shoes or clothes; not many could embroider, but they had to be able to cook. Otherwise they’d struggle in the future.

“I know, second brother,” Shen Yan sighed. The two brothers and their mother often spoke to her, and she took it all in stride.

Seeing his sister in this mood, Shen Xuanqing felt a little helpless. He set the candlestick on the chopping board, picked up the small clay jar of badger oil, and said to Lu Gu, “This is last year’s badger oil. After you wash up tonight, rub some on your hands and feet—feel free to use more.”

To Shen Yan he added, “You too. Didn’t you frostbite last year? You must take precautions this year as well. Don’t take it lightly, or you’ll cry when frostbite returns.”

Thinking of the pain and itching frostbite had caused last year, which had brought her to tears, Shen Yan nodded quickly in agreement: “Okay.”
