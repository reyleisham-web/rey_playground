Seeing that look, Lu Gu couldn’t help but laugh. Lingjun was generally well behaved—both he and Shen Xuanqing had taught him not to pull the dogs’ fur when they played, and Lingjun remembered. Yet the little one wore a frown, so Lu Gu did what he could to defuse the moment with a smile: “Come here. After washing up, I’ll take you out to buy peaches.”

Lingjun had just plucked a bit of fur from the big dog’s neck and was pinching it between his tiny fingers to examine it closely. When he heard about peaches, he immediately tossed the fur aside, baring his little white teeth in a grin. “Peaches,” he said.

“Yes, peaches. I’ll take you to buy some after you wash up,” Lu Gu replied, while pulling the child to squat beside the wooden basin.

Shen Xuanqing and Luo Biao had already gone to the shop. After finishing his chores, Lu Gu slung a bamboo basket over his arm and locked the courtyard gate. Lingjun, standing beside him, refused to walk and stretched out his arms to be held. Since early morning hadn’t grown too hot yet, he had no choice but to pick up the child. Guaizi followed the two of them as they headed for the street.

On the street, peddlers with shoulder poles or bamboo baskets hawked their wares. With the arrival of summer, various fruit drinks and medicinal beverages appeared, and there were plenty of fruit sellers as well.

As they walked, Lu Gu kept watch for the best peaches to buy.

“Amu, peach, peach!” Little Lingjun stretched his arm toward the big, red peaches. Though small, his eyes were sharp. Those bright red peaches looked fresh and eye-catching.

“All right, all right,” Lu Gu chuckled. “How come your eyes and ears are so sharp when it comes to food, huh? When I tell you to wash your face, you can’t hear me?”

Lingjun didn’t answer; he simply giggled and wrapped his tiny arms around Lu Gu’s neck to be spoiled.

What a clever little imp. Lu Gu couldn’t help but smile all the more.

Peaches were cheaper now, but the best, big ones cost two copper coins more. He bought a basketful and, still carrying the child, headed toward the butcher’s shop first.

Lingjun wanted to eat the peaches but knew they had fuzzy skins that could prick his fingers and mouth. His outstretched hand reached toward a peach but quickly retreated at Lu Gu’s reminder.

At the shop, an old woman had come to buy an old hen, saying it was good to eat in the heat of summer. Shen Xuanqing chatted with her for a bit at the front, while Luo Biao slaughtered chickens in the backyard. The weather was too hot, and meat couldn’t be stored long. Whether rabbits, chickens, or ducks, as long as they were alive, they kept better; if only the meat was required, it had to be slaughtered on the spot.

When Lingjun saw his father, he no longer wanted Lu Gu to carry him and clung to Shen Xuanqing instead. Free from the little furnace in his arms, Lu Gu felt instant relief. He carried the basket to the back to wash the peaches, with Guaizi following him the whole way, not sparing Shen Xuanqing a single glance. He knew exactly what their big pup wanted. The first washed peach was laid on the ground for Guaizi.

In summer, even dogs enjoyed fruit. Guaizi took a big bite and devoured it quickly. Unlike humans, he didn’t mind the fuzz pricking his mouth and didn’t need it washed.

As Lu Gu washed the other peaches, he glanced at the one already in Guaizi’s mouth. It looked juicy and sweet.

The summer heat was unbearable, but fruit every day helped them endure. Gradually they made it through the season.

To return before early winter and avoid the coldest part of the journey, the caravan planned to set off after the autumn harvest. They would depart in two days.

Shen Xuanqing and Lu Gu had returned to their hometown for a visit. With him gone, Shen Yaoqing would oversee the shop—leaving no men to handle the labor at home, so plans had to be laid carefully. Li Laiqing worked the fields, and during harvest they could hire extra hands from their village or neighboring ones.

Since the trip would last no more than three months, Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing discussed hiring a temporary laborer for the new house chores—sweeping, feeding livestock, and the like. They needed a reliable, diligent man, since men were naturally stronger than Wei Lanxiang and the others.

With Li Laiqing as the long-term laborer, hiring a temporary worker for lighter tasks like feeding livestock wasn’t difficult. Their first thought was Ge Chunsheng.

Though older, he was experienced and never slacked off. A long-term laborer earned 2,000 copper coins a year, about 166 copper coins a month. Temporary workers earned more than 200 coins a month, or two qian of silver.

Shen Yaoqing knew Ge Chunsheng helped his son-in-law farm his meager plot of land. Hiring him as a temporary worker meant paying for a full month’s labor, so he hesitated.

But both Ge Chunsheng and Li Laiqing were honest, good-hearted men who never cut corners. Hiring the right person was crucial—otherwise, there’d be endless trouble.

Ge Chunsheng was poor, his wife often ill, and they had no sons—a pitiable situation. Wei Lanxiang suggested they ask the Ge family. That meager plot barely earned them anything, but six qian of silver over three months could carry them through winter if spent frugally.

They soon received a firm answer: Ge Chunsheng was more than willing to earn the money.

Hiring Ge Chunsheng proved to be the perfect solution—he needed work, and they needed a reliable hand. Everyone benefited.

With Ge Chunsheng handling the temporary work, Wei Lanxiang, Shen Yan, and Ji Qiuyue wouldn’t have to work much. They could focus on managing the family and watching the children. Especially Wei Lanxiang, who was getting older. In the past two years, the family’s busyness had left her little rest, tending both fields and livestock. It was time for her to take it easy.

Lu Gu had no say in these matters. These past few days, he’d been cooking varied meals, wanting Shen Xuanqing to eat well before they left. During the journey, they would endure hardships—without inns or taverns, they would have only dry provisions to eat.

After several busy days, the day of the caravan’s departure arrived.
