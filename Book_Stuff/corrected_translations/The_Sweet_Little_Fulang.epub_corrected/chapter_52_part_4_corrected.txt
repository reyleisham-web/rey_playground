Fortunately, after two days apart, Shen Xuanqing returned, looking more at ease, a smile on his handsome face, and besides the fox fur he had also brought back a honeycomb. “I dug a big nest and there were several honeycombs. I took out three and left two for them. I didn’t take all of them, but it’s enough for us.” Shen Xuanqing tucked the honeycomb into the jar and broke off a large piece for Lu Gu to eat.

Seeing Shen Xuanqing smile, and Lu Gu tasting something sweet again, his brows and eyes curved slightly, and there was a faint smile too. The matter from before had been pushed to the back of their minds; neither of them spoke of it again and kept silent.

But there were still changes, at least for Shen Xuanqing, who hadn’t figured out how to have sex for a while and would never touch Lu Gu again; he was afraid of losing face in front of his fulang, so he hid it behind work. Concealment seemed the best remedy, and when hunting was busy, it was easy to divert his mind from other thoughts.

Autumn rains, the sky overcast and hazy, the mountains in the distance shrouded in rain and fog. The rain grew heavier, rushing down in torrents.

Lu Gu hurried into the yard wearing a coir raincoat and a bamboo hat. Shen Xuanqing, equally dressed, followed him with a bamboo basket filled with grass. Earlier the rain had slowed a bit; the drizzle wasn’t heavy, so the dwarf deer and pheasants, deprived of grass, went out to forage and came back with some.

As for Lu Gu’s chickens and ducks, there was no need to worry. He had just harvested some chicken grass and duck grass yesterday, plus grain bran for their feed. The old hunter kept his prey in the woodshed, so the woodshed on the mountain was quite large. After the rain, Lu Gu drove all the chickens and ducks into the woodshed and surrounded a spot with firewood to keep them from wandering; otherwise the mountain rain would make their feet cold and wet and could bring illness. Even the dwarf deer was brought into the woodshed, though Shen Xuanqing had shortened the hemp rope to spare Lu Gu from worrying about the chickens and ducks being trampled.

Taking off the bamboo hat and the coir raincoat, Lu Gu added a piece of firewood to the small medicine stove at the entrance of the main room. A fish soup simmered in a clay pot on the stove; the thick, white broth boiled quickly, its fragrance filling the room. After waking, Shen Xuanqing went to fetch the bowls and spoons.

They had already eaten by midday, and with the rain and wind outside it was really cold, so they stewed some hot soup and drank a bowl, eating a few pieces of meat to keep warm.

The puppy and the big dogs stayed in the main room; otherwise they would go out to get soaked. Shen Xuanqing tossed a roasted bamboo rat to each of them.

There wasn’t much wind outside, only the rain, dripping from the eaves like a curtain.

“There’s not much salt left,” Lu Gu remarked after draining half a bowl of soup, thinking aloud.

“Well, I’ll go down the mountain to buy some in two days,” Shen Xuanqing replied.

They drank the hot soup while watching the mountain rain outside. It felt a little leisurely, but they didn’t remain idle after finishing. Lu Gu took a sewing basket to embroider by the reflected light, and Shen Xuanqing worked on the bamboo he hadn’t finished weaving last time. The atmosphere was perfect; they spoke only a few sentences at times, yet it was gentle, and having someone with you was better than being alone.

The soil was wet after the rain, and the sun came out, but the ground remained damp and unsuitable for long walks; otherwise, your feet would be coated with wet mud, and you’d have to scratch away with branches after walking a while to avoid heavy feet.

Shen Xuanqing carried a bamboo basket on his back and went with Lu Gu to pick mushrooms and vegetables. Autumn leaves lay scattered on the ground, some still stained with cold rainwater and not yet dried.

“The day after tomorrow, it’s almost done, and it’ll be time to go down after we tidy up tomorrow,” Shen Xuanqing said while gathering vegetables. He brought back six red fox furs and one miscellaneous fox fur that wasn’t of good quality. These could be kept for the time being; after all, it was a long way to Fucheng. They could wait until more were saved to sell them. It would get colder soon, so there was no need to worry about fox furs not selling.

The pheasants, rabbits, and the dwarf deer should be sold. The dwarf deer was injured and not very strong. Although Lu Gu fed it well, it was kept confined and depressed. It would be a pity if it lost weight later, so he’d sell more while it was still fat.

Hearing this, Lu Gu thought for a moment and said, “Then bring medlars; a basket has already dried. I also found some tianwogen; let’s take them down, and ask brother Daqing to cover them with soil.”

Shen Xuanqing thought of the huge pile of tianwogens Lu Gu had accumulated, and the two of them wouldn’t be able to finish it.

Two days ago, he had asked Lu Gu why he dug so much, and Lu Gu told him it was winter rations for going down the mountain. It was hard to climb up. Lu Gu was walking when he saw dipicai; he squatted down and said, “I dried the bamboo mushrooms, saved some for rations, and sold them in town.”

They planned what to sell and what not to sell; Shen Xuanqing seldom interfered, simply nodding and agreeing.
