17 Part 24

Lu Wen had carried on with other men after he became engaged to Shen Xuanqing, and in a short time he still could not completely let go of it. His mother and his family were so furious that if the dispute dragged on too long, they might not survive it. After three years of hard life, they had just begun to wake up, and every member of the family wore a smile. He also did not want to lose money, so he moved the Lu family’s belongings and sold them, at least for some cash.

But Du Hehua’s oaths were too harsh, and Lu Daxiang slandered the innocence between him and Lu Gu in front of everyone, which made him burn with anger deep inside, and he stopped holding back.

Shen Xuanqing’s thoughts gradually returned. When he noticed Lu Gu beside him, he thought that his husband was now Lu Gu and had nothing more to do with Lu Wen. He felt a little better; he felt steadier than when he saw Lu Wen, which had left him overwhelmed with disgust.

On the way home, Lu Wen avoided crowded places and kept his head down as much as possible, trying not to be noticed. His mother took it upon herself to handle things, never imagining it would end up like this. But when he learned what had happened later, he wasn’t too afraid; even if the Shen family really went to the county government, with the Li family around, nothing would happen.

The reason he didn’t invite the Li family directly was because he wanted to save face. After all, his name was written on the betrothal papers, and he wouldn’t concede unless it was absolutely necessary. Besides, this matter was truly a disgrace. It would be a shame if more people knew about it, even the county officials.

If he regretted it, he did not admit it. He had been able to agree to Shen’s family at the time. Shen Xuanqing was really good-looking—handsome and tall; he stood out, whether in the countryside or in town. Compared with villagers who tilled the fields, the hunter was a business that earned money; otherwise the Shen family wouldn’t have given a dowry of twenty taels. After a few years of hard life, many said that it was ginseng that had saved Shen Shunfu’s life when he fell ill, but in the end he could not be saved. The Shen family owed a hundred taels or more for his treatment.

But no matter how rich the countryside hunter was, his money was still mere copper coins when compared to the town’s young masters, especially since the Li family remained a wealthy clan. If he hadn’t lived alone in town, he would have been the subject of gossip; otherwise he wouldn’t have come back and would have blamed his family for being poor and lacking good relatives in town. Otherwise he could have stayed temporarily, one day or two, at a former classmate’s house, but it wouldn’t last long. Li Lang had once said he would buy him a house to live in, but he was so proud that he could not let his reputation be tarnished by having been raised outside; no matter what, he had to become the Li family’s husband through a matchmaker.

It wasn’t that he didn’t know the village’s gossip, but he could handle it. As long as the countryside folk got a bit of benefit, they spoke as if everything slipped through their fingers.

This time Shen Xuanqing came back and stayed at home for three days, which was quite a long time. He didn’t rush into the mountains immediately. He sold the five-step snake to the medical clinic in town. The gall of the five-step snake was one of the most precious among snake galls, and that snake was large and handsome; after the gall was extracted, it could be cooked and steeped in medicinal wine.

In the medical hall, as soon as he pulled the five-step snake from his bag, a fat man with a big head slapped the table and ordered him to take it back so he could drink snake wine to strengthen his yang.

In the end, Shen Xuanqing earned two ingots—five taels of silver each—which were heavy in his hands, and he gave Wei Lanxiang one ingot upon returning home.

At noon that day, Wei Lanxiang gathered the chickens and ducks and asked Shen Yaoqing and Ji Qiuyue to look after the house. She took Shen Yan, Shen Xuanqing, and Lu Gu to her natal home and paid off ten taels of silver. After their meal, they felt lighter on the way back.

Hunters were richer than ordinary farmers, and they usually earned small sums by selling common prey and furs; nothing spectacular, but not bad either. If luck was on their side and they found something valuable on the mountain, they could sell it for a lot, but that all depended on fortune.

When they previously borrowed money, because ten taels of silver was not a trivial amount, to avoid mistakes and to give the family some relief, Wei Lanxiang cautiously promised that it would be repaid by May next year; they wouldn’t have to tighten their belts. Also, the winter wheat on the dry land could be harvested by that time, and with what they had saved during this period, it would be enough for ten taels of silver.

The next day, Lu Gu got up, and Wei Lanxiang summoned him into the room. Looking at Lu Gu, she felt that no matter how she looked at him, Lu Gu was much more pleasing to the eye than Lu Wen. Not long after he had married, the family’s bad luck seemed to be gradually lifting, and good things began to come.

Even after what happened to Shen Shunfu, she had prayed to many gods and Buddhas and kowtowed countless times that year, yet she hadn’t saved her man, and she didn’t believe in it anymore. But happiness followed happiness, and Lu Gu was obedient and well-behaved; his smile grew wider.

When Lu Gu walked out of his room and saw Shen Xuanqing in the yard, his heart began to beat faster. Mother had said she would take him into the mountains with Shen Xuanqing early the next morning, promising they would have a good time. After all, there would only be the two of them in the mountains; no one else would speak.
