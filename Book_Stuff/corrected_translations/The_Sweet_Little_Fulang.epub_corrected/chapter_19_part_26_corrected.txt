Chapter 19 Part 26


Lu Gu’s eyes widened at the sound of the old bear approaching. He knew the dangers of the mountains, but he hadn’t expected an old brute to come to the place where the two of them would live. Yet after Shen Xuanqing finished speaking, he saw that the courtyard wall was so high that, if something did come, it wouldn’t be able to break in. His heart settled again.

He helped move the dishes from the cart into the kitchen. He glanced at the mess inside but didn’t dare speak. He frowned, deciding he would tidy it after breakfast.

The two walked along the road for three hours. The mountain path wasn’t easy, and Shen Xuanqing was still dragging a cart that weighed more than two hundred jin. Now he was tired and hungry. Lu Gu saw there was nothing he could move, so he rolled up his sleeves and set to work at the stove.

He brought about a dozen steamed buns from home and could only eat them once they were warmed. He gathered ripe vegetables, washed them, fried them, and scrambled a plate of eggs.

Back at the Lu household, he hadn’t cooked in the kitchen very often. Du Hehua had always watched him closely, afraid he would steal food, and wouldn’t let him touch those things. After arriving at Shen’s house, Ji Qiuyue was the one cooking.

Eggs were precious, so when he scrambled them, he took great care not to burn them. Who knew that even with such caution, the eggs burned a little.

Lu Gu’s face turned pale with fright. He quickly pulled the eggs away, afraid Shen Xuanqing would see. But he was hungry and wanted to know if the meal was edible.

Shen Xuanqing pursed his lips slightly and stepped in. This was the first breakfast they shared, and it felt different from meals at home.

As soon as he entered, he saw Lu Gu standing in front of the stove with a pale, embarrassed look, glancing at the plate of somewhat mushy eggs, and understood the reason why.

Sometimes someone cooked sloppily; Wei Lanxiang used to burn pots dry and turn rice black in the pot, and she couldn’t bear to waste it, so she shared it with the whole family. Shen Xuanqing still remembered the bitterness of that blackened rice.

Although the eggs were a bit burned, not everything was ruined. It was still better than his mother’s time.

“Let’s eat when we’re done,” he said, taking the plate of eggs and the steamed bun basket as he stepped outside.

Not being reprimanded, Lu Gu could finally breathe and followed with the bowl of greens, chopsticks in hand.

The sun was bright, and by noon they had arrived. After eating, Shen Xuanqing didn’t linger in the mountains as before. He rubbed his shoulders, entered the room, and, after a whole morning’s journey, could not help but rest for a while.

Lu Gu was washing dishes by himself in the kitchen. In truth, there were only two pairs of chopsticks and two bowls, so it wasn’t much work. But he was careful. When he noticed a figure at the doorway, Lu Gu—who had been crouched down stacking firewood—lifted his head.

Shen Xuanqing held a porcelain bottle in his hand, gripping it tightly. Lu Gu looked up, then lowered his eyes and spoke in a low voice: “I can’t touch my back shoulder. You can rub some medicine for me.”

Lu Gu was startled for a moment, then recovered and replied softly, “I’ll wash my hands.”

With that, Shen Xuanqing went back to the room first. When Lu Gu came in, he hesitated, then began to undress. He looked calm and composed, but there was a touch of caution in his movements.

The young man before him was slender yet strong, different from the short, stocky boy he had imagined—tall and handsome, indescribably good-looking. Lu Gu didn’t dare to look at him casually. He walked behind Shen Xuanqing with his head down, took the porcelain bottle from the table, and, following Shen Xuanqing’s whispered instructions, poured the red, viscous medicine into his palm, warmed it a little, and pressed the palm onto the red mark on Shen Xuanqing’s back shoulder, rubbing to dissolve the medicine.

It wasn’t like at Shen’s house; here it was just the two of them. There was no Shen Yan or Ji Qiuyue to talk to, and because Lu Gu had fried the eggs himself, he felt a touch of emptiness in his heart. He didn’t dare to approach Shen Xuanqing any more closely, so he simply retreated to tidy the kitchen.

The application of the medicine didn’t take long, but it felt like a very long time for Lu Gu. Even if they had slept together at night, they were both clothed, and he had never seen Shen Xuanqing shirtless. Shen Xuanqing was eighteen, having worked hard all these years to develop some muscles that shouldn’t be underestimated. Yet he remained young—a green, raw youth. Even so, Lu Gu found him intimidating.

“Okay,” Lu Gu’s voice trembled slightly. “That’s enough.”

Shen Xuanqing’s Adam’s apple bobbed as he spoke, his voice deep: “Well, I’ll do the rest.”

Not only was his back shoulder red and worn, but his shoulders were also taxed from hard work. His little husband’s hands were light and soft, and that thought made him linger a moment longer to rub the wound, but when he heard Lu Gu’s trembling voice, it was clear that he was still afraid, so he stopped.

Growing up the way he did, often doing heavy labor, it was common for him to apply medicine for injuries. Sometimes he couldn’t reach a spot and asked someone else to help, but others weren’t as meticulous and gentle as Lu Gu. When Lu Gu applied medicine himself, his ears reddened at the unfamiliar, intimate contact.

Lu Gu, who could slip away, returned to the kitchen. After the mountain road all morning, his legs felt heavy, and he sat on a small stool by the fire to rest. He was terrified, yet his palms were scorching hot. The thought of touching another man’s flesh frightened him, but after a moment he realized it was Shen Xuanqing, not another man. There was no need to shy away.

Even so, he remained uncertain. In the end, Shen Xuanqing, thinking of his tired legs, finished applying the medicine and, after getting dressed, called him into the room. They lay side by side on the bed, just as they did at the foot of the mountain.
