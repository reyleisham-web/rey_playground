Chapter 76 Part 30


After Shen Xuanqing showed them how to hold the brushes, he picked up one and wrote the character "人" on the paper. Lu Gu and Shen Yan watched it carefully.

“Now you two write it,” Shen Xuanqing said after he finished.

These words made Shen Yan nervous, not to mention Lu Gu, for fear that something might go wrong. The two of them held the brush incorrectly at first, and Shen Xuanqing straightened their grips; the brush trembled slightly at the tip and then fell.

“Don’t shake.” Shen Xuanqing was helpless, and then added, “Write more, and you won’t shake in the future. Don’t be afraid—there are no outsiders at home.”

That reassurance helped. Lu Gu finished writing and looked at the first character he had written, scrutinizing it. The handwriting was ugly, but he was happy in his heart. Not only could he read the characters, he could also write with a brush on paper.

Because he was happy, he subconsciously looked up at Shen Xuanqing.

Shen Xuanqing originally wanted to ask him to write two more characters, but his words changed abruptly, and he said: “Well written; it would be better to write a few more.” Needless to say, once you’ve learned to write, you must be diligent.

Shen Yan was a little slower, and Shen Xuanqing didn’t hesitate to praise her once she finished writing, so the two of them buried their heads in their work.

When Shen Xuanqing attended school, he was among a group of boys; now, seeing Lu Gu and Shen Yan behave so well, he felt relieved.

The three of them wrote and read, and Ji Qiuyue, who was sewing clothes under the eaves, listened with her ears and whispered a sentence or two from time to time, so that Shen Yaoqing could hear it. He scratched his head, unsure whether his wife wanted to learn or not, so he simply said: “If you want to learn, I also went to study, and I will teach you.”

Ji Qiuyue stopped what she was doing, pursed her lips, and then smiled. “I still can’t; I’m stupid, I can’t read well.”

Seeing his wife like this, Shen Yaoqing said: “What’s wrong with reading like this?”

“Teacher Erqing is too busy; I’ll teach you,” she replied. He was about to go get a brush and paper, but Ji Qiuyue stopped him. “Forget it for now. They are reading. If you interrupt, what should they do if they get distracted?”

“Then let’s go to the room. If there’s no book, I’ll write it out on paper for you.” Shen Yaoqing saw that his wife wanted to learn a bit, so he didn’t delay. He went to the kitchen to fetch a bowl.

Hearing what they said, Shen Xuanqing picked up the inkstone and poured some ink into the bowl. There was plenty of paper and brushes, so he took them casually.

Ji Qiuyue was anxious about reading and writing like Lu Gu had done. She had an older brother and a younger sister, and her younger sister had never studied. As for the eldest brother, he had stopped going to school after two years, saying that reading made him feel dizzy; if you felt dizzy, it was better to work and farm at home.

The word “study” seemed very distant to her. Her parents still loved her, and she hadn’t suffered many grievances in childhood, but when it came to study, her father, like most villagers, said that girls should stay at home and that reading was what men did, so even if the family could afford a private school in town, she was not allowed to go.

Now Shen Yaoqing wanted to teach her, and she couldn’t refuse, so she pursed her lips, smiled, put away the cloth she was sewing, and went into the room to learn.

People read and write in the courtyard. Wei Lanxiang listened to the movements at home and didn’t stop them. Shen Shunfu had told her before that it was good to study; being able to read and write was a skill, and he planned to send Shen Yan to a private school in town. Now it was all right; she didn’t even need to go to a private school—she could be taught at home.

It was inevitable to feel thirsty while reading, so she poured a bowl of clear tea for everyone with a warm teapot and stood behind Shen Yan to watch.

She looked at it for a while, then lifted her head and said to Shen Xuanqing: “I see—you will read it every day in the future, and if you can’t spare more time, at least you should do it for an hour. Read this book well; it doesn’t look thick.”

Indeed, it would take hard work and long-term effort. Shen Xuanqing nodded and said, “That’s good. I don’t go into the mountains in winter, and I don’t do much fieldwork, so we can read it every day in winter.”

When Lu Gu heard that he would recite the entire book, he was very happy. He wouldn’t need others to teach him in the future; he could read it by himself while holding the Three Character Classic.

Because he was a beginner, Shen Xuanqing didn’t have him recognize too many characters at once; he didn’t want to overwhelm him. It would be enough if he could write the first six characters well over the past two days.

At night, Lu Gu lay in bed and recited the characters he had learned, even tracing them in the air with his fingers. Shen Xuanqing poured out a basin of foot-washing water, closed the door tightly, and went to bed. After sitting up, he leaned against the headboard and did not blow out the candles right away. Instead, he pulled a small bottle from his sleeve.

“Aren’t you going to sleep now?” Lu Gu asked, turning his head to look, and he also caught sight of the small blue porcelain bottle.

Shen Xuanqing’s Adam’s apple bobbed slightly; he gripped the porcelain bottle tightly in his hand and said nothing. After blowing out the candle, there were sounds of clothes being undressed. Night fell quiet, and his low voice could be heard vaguely.

Lu Gu didn’t expect that the balm in the porcelain bottle could serve such a purpose. The cool sensation made him want to shrink back, but he was still pressed close to him around the waist.

Earlier today, Shen Xuanqing had gone into town to the ointment shop after finishing the book from Luo Biao. Luo Biao had said he would give him the book; to keep it from others, he wrapped the book in oiled paper and pressed it under the bed. The book mentioned a balm that was especially interesting to use. He remembered it was quite expensive—eight copper coins for a small porcelain bottle—but he bought it anyway.
