Chapter 227


He had told Lu Gu the basics, but the truth was harsher. Escaping through the mountains had been a life-or-death struggle. He had been the fastest runner in their group, and in a desperate bid to rescue Cui Rongwei when they were pursued, he had risked his own life, nearly paying with it himself.

Later, when helping with the mission, his participation had come partly from hatred for those ruthless men who slaughtered passing merchant caravans without mercy, and partly from fear—the fear that they would target his family. So he figured he might as well contribute as much as he could.

If not for that, Cui Rongwei wouldn’t have kept him around. That dragon-patterned jade pendant had truly been earned with his life.

He hadn’t wanted to tell Lu Gu any of this, to spare him worry, but in his haste to return he’d forgotten to hide the pendant.

Their family was small and ordinary, living only in Jixing Town—what major affairs would ever require seeking officials or Cui Rongwei? Yuncheng wasn’t far from the northern frontier, and there was a military camp nearby. He and Luo Biao, along with a few others, had hidden there with Cui Rongwei, witnessing and learning many things, even picking up some skills.

When Cui Rongwei asked him to stay, he’d been tempted. Any man with even a trace of ambition would yearn for the words “achieving glory and success.”

Cui Rongwei was a good official—when they first met in the mountains, he urged them to flee while he and his two subordinates drew the pursuers away. That was why Shen Xuanqing had saved him.

But the capital was simply too far from home. Moreover, in the past six months, although there hadn’t been much bloodshed, there had been several near misses. Even a high-ranking official like Cui Rongwei had nearly been killed. The factions fought and schemed, and someone with no backing or connections could only tread carefully in such a place. How could that compare to the comfort and ease of staying home?

So even when Cui Rongwei offered to move his entire family to the capital, he refused.

They were just farmers, barely literate. If they went to the capital, they’d live in constant fear.

He did have ambition, but it was simply to improve his family’s life—to let their children study in the future. He didn’t seek great wealth or nobility, just a peaceful life.

The wind howled and the rain hammered down. Hearing the storm outside, Lu Gu didn’t stay awake until midnight like he usually did.

Lingjun might have been dreaming, whimpering softly a few times. Lu Gu rolled over and patted the child sleeping on the inner side of the bed.

Every movement of his family steadied Shen Xuanqing’s heart. He slept as soundly as he had in six months.

The next morning, Lu Gu woke later than usual. The shop wouldn’t open today; they had to wait for Shen Yaoqing to bring the mule cart from home before they could sell anything.

Because Shen Xuanqing had returned, the two of them hadn’t slept well these past six months. Even upon waking, they didn’t rise right away.

Lingjun, by contrast, woke first. After blinking his eyes open, he sat up in a daze, slowly remembering yesterday’s events when he realized the big adult now sharing the bed with him.

Lu Gu was awakened by the child climbing over him. Shen Xuanqing woke in the same way.

Lingjun crawled onto his father’s chest, but feeling shy, he didn’t say anything. Shen Xuanqing pulled him into a tight embrace.

Lingjun immediately burst into giggles, used to being teased by his father’s big, warm arms.

“Our Ling ger can even dress himself now—how impressive!” Lu Gu cheered, pleased to see the child had already put on his outer garment, though it wasn’t fully fastened.

Shen Xuanqing echoed the praise, and Lingjun brightened even more.

Shen Yan had already risen and was boiling water in the large kitchen pot. A few days earlier, Lu Gu had bought a fire starter, which was much more convenient than flint.

Knowing Shen Xuanqing was exhausted, she moved quietly. When she heard the child’s laughter and the adults waking, she called from the yard, “Brother Guzi, the sky’s clear now. What do you want for breakfast? Should I make something, or should we buy buns or a bowl of wontons?”

Back in their hometown, breakfast was usually just reheated steamed buns, maybe an egg or pickles—barely enough to fill their stomachs. Since moving to town, with its many breakfast stalls selling all sorts of things, they sometimes treated themselves.

“We’re out of eggs, but there are two duck eggs left. If we’re eating at home, I’ll boil them now,” she added.

“Wait, I’ll go buy buns and bring them back to eat at home. It’s cold outside,” Lu Gu called as he sat up.

The rain had fallen last night, and though the town’s roads were mostly bluestone-paved, there were muddy patches that made them slippery. Adults could manage, but children were unsteady on their feet and liked to run around. It was better to eat at home and head back afterward.

He reached for the clothes draped over the bedpost and dressed, then turned to Shen Xuanqing. “Brother might already be on his way with the mule cart. Let’s just eat something ready-made from outside. That way we can intercept him on the road and save him the extra trip.”

“Got it,” Shen Xuanqing replied absently, busy playing with Lingjun.

He kissed his little shuang’er’s cheeks a few times. His stubble, hastily shaved the night before in Shichuan Town, had already grown back rough, making Lingjun squirm and push his face away.

Remembering how his little shuang’er had cried as a baby when pricked by his stubble—being a delicate and timid shuang’er, not as tough as a little boy—he didn’t push his luck. On his first day back, he didn’t want to make the child cry, so he stopped when Lingjun resisted.
