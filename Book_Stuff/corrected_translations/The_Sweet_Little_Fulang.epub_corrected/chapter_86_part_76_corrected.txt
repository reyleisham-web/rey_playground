Chapter 86 Part 76


Some villagers on quiet days would sit on the threshold, chatting with neighbors and passers-by. Because Lu Gu was a young fulang, he rarely sat on the threshold; he was thin-skinned and shy.

One day he came out to wash the pot and saw a crowd of dogs approaching. Da Hui and the puppy were among them. The puppy wagged his tail, eager to play, but Lu Gu didn’t take the hint and was about to go back in with the pot in his hand when suddenly a dog goaded Da Hui, barking viciously and rushing forward to bite and fight.

The Tibetan mastiff was bigger than the other village dogs, and Da Hui had hunting and biting skills. He had fought with dogs in the village before and had become the lead dog, but because he spent a lot of time in the mountains, sometimes one or two big dogs would challenge him for the top spot when he came back.

Once the barking started, all the dogs bared their teeth, even the puppy. The movement was sudden; Lu Gu stood very close to them, and a pack of dogs began to fight. When a dogfight breaks out, powerful men don’t dare to get close, and it’s easy to accidentally injure someone.

Shen Xuanqing stood to the side and, seeing the danger, strode over, quickly protecting Lu Gu and pulling him into the house.

Two thin dogs barked on the hillside, and the long-legged dogs ran back to help Da Hui bite the other dogs, together with the puppy.

Dogs also formed gangs; even if they bit each other into a tangle, it was hard for outsiders to tell who was who, but the dogs themselves knew who to bite.

The fight was very loud, and Lu Gu worried for the puppy, although the puppy was braver than ordinary dogs. Shen Xuanqing wasn’t overly worried; he didn’t rush forward to drive the other dogs away—he had to fight for the position of leader himself, and the aid of others wasn’t counted.

Many idlers from the village heard the commotion and gathered at the door to watch; some who liked to see the excitement came closer for a better look.

Lu Gu hid behind Shen Xuanqing, peeking out only; he tugged on Shen Xuanqing’s sleeve with one hand and asked, “What about Guaizi?”

“He will be fine,” Shen Xuanqing replied without turning his head, still watching the puppy.

The puppy was clever, using his size to bite the other dogs. If a dog bigger than he came to bite him, he would dodge quickly and skillfully. He didn’t suffer much thanks to his thick fur. Besides, there were three big dogs in the village who belonged to the same faction as Da Hui. The dogs that had tasted blood were more ferocious. To prevent Da Hui from fighting and biting too hard, Shen Xuanqing took the wooden stick behind the door and kept it in hand; not only for Da Hui—sometimes a dog would bite and wouldn’t let go. It wasn’t easy to raise a dog, and you couldn’t afford to kill one in a fight.

Of course, the most important thing was to prevent his dogs from getting bitten, so that he could intervene in time.

Soon, Da Hui pressed the provocative dog beneath his body; the dog broke free two or three times, but the tail was obviously tucked, and Da Hui bared his teeth again, and the dog ran away with the others.

This was the first time Lu Gu saw Da Hui’s ferocious look, and he was startled. But when Da Hui turned around and walked home, Lu Gu regained his composure, and Da Hui even rubbed against him and Shen Xuanqing’s legs as he passed by.

The puppy was very proud of their victory; he held his head high, barked a few times, and even ran over to whine at Lu Gu, as if to show off.

“Don’t pay attention to him, ger—if you praise him after a fight, he will think you’re rewarding him, and when he meets another dog in the village, he will go and bite the dog.” Shen Xuanqing stopped Lu Gu.

Although the village dogs were free to roam, there had been incidents where one or two dogs were killed in fights before. The dogs his family raised were all hunting dogs and bore no grudges against the villagers. But they couldn’t let the puppy get used to fighting, or when their dogs killed or maimed another dog, the owner would come to the door to demand an explanation. Some idlers would deliberately provoke a dog to bite; if the dog was injured, the hunter would suffer, and besides, the dog he raised would go hunting in the mountains. It wasn’t easy to hunt when the dog was injured, especially a long-legged dog that was easy to injure, if not become lame.

Lu Gu had no idea about raising dogs, but he listened to Shen Xuanqing and ignored the puppy’s plea for praise. He put the pot in the kitchen, and as soon as he stepped out again, he saw Li Wanyun standing outside the door with a pale, pinched expression.

“Guzi, is Aunt Lanxiang here?” When she saw someone coming out, Li Wanyun dared to enter. She’d just seen the dogs fight and was too frightened to go in and out. She stood at the door and watched Da Hui lying in the yard panting; she didn’t dare to go in and out.

“Mother,” Lu Gu called toward the main room, then hurried over to stop the family’s dogs from surrounding her.

Li Wanyun felt a little relieved after he came over.

“Wanyun.” Wei Lanxiang appeared from the room and asked what was the matter.

Li Wanyun held a bowl of mixed flour in her hand; she was embarrassed, wanting to smile but unable to, and spoke in a low voice: “My mother says she wants salted duck eggs. I, I want to exchange one with Aunt Lanxiang.”

Comparing a bowl of mixed flour with duck eggs, the flour was worthless, especially when pitted against salted duck eggs cured with salt, but she really couldn’t help it.

Wei Lanxiang saw that Li Wanyun was still dressed thinly, her face pale ashen, and she seemed exhausted. Although she felt a bit disadvantaged, she still took Li Wanyun’s bowl with a sigh. If others tried to take advantage of her in this way, Wei Lanxiang wouldn’t agree, but Li Wanyun was clearly embarrassed; she was poor, so it didn’t matter to her.

As Li Wanyun waited for Wei Lanxiang to fetch the duck eggs, she lowered her head and noticed the silver bracelet on Lu Gu’s wrist. Earlier, she had gone out and wanted to come over, but she’d seen the dogs fight, and before she returned home she’d witnessed Shen Xuanqing rush to protect Lu Gu. She felt a touch of envy in her heart, but she knew she didn’t have such a blessing.
