Chapter 2 Part 1


Part 1

For a moment, Lu Gu forgot to breathe. The cold, murderous look on Shen Xuanqing’s face made his brain buzz, as if he were slipping into a hallucination right in front of everyone. He had already pictured dying under the stick and a beating, and the fear seized him so hard that he couldn’t tell where he was.

In a daze, he didn’t know when Shen Xuanqing had slammed the door behind him, until an anxious outcry pierced the air and pulled him back to reality.

There were several people at the doorway. When they saw that the person standing in the room wasn’t Lu Wen, Wei Lanxiang fainted.

“Mother! Mother, don’t worry, don’t worry,” Shen Yan and sister-in-law Ji Qiuyue cried in unison, hurriedly supporting Wei Lanxiang as she collapsed. The village aunt who helped in the Shen household came over to assist as well, and the Shen family, who had been so joyful just moments before, suddenly descended into chaos.

Wei Lanxiang woke with a start, gasping for air, her eyes blazing as she scanned the room and fell upon Lu Gu. “Who are you?” she snapped, tears streaming down her face, her breath ragged.

A tremble ran through Lu Gu as he stammered, but someone nearby recognized him. She was Lu Daxiang’s first wife’s son or daughter from a nearby village near Anjia Village, not yet married. People often walked through Anjia Village with the girls from that area, and after five or six years they only had a vague impression of Lu Gu. It took a moment for recognition to crystallize.

Hearing the identification, Wei Lanxiang’s anger burned hotter. For the sake of this marriage arrangement, the family had bustled for a long time, and she had not spared a copper—twenty taels of dowry, not to mention the things she’d hired at the outset. Each item had cost money. Since her husband had died, their family had only been getting by more and more.

Shen Shunfu had slipped and fallen while climbing the mountain three winters ago, breaking his leg and plunging into the icy water. Although he fell on the shore of shallow water and managed to pull himself up, the freezing water in winter was enough to give anyone a fever. His clothes were soaked, and with a broken leg he could barely crawl; afterward, some villagers who went up the mountain to fetch firewood heard a faint sound and rescued Shen Shunfu, who was blue and purple from the cold.

The injury cost a fortune just to consult doctors and buy medicines, and much of the family’s possessions were sold. Despite every effort, no one could save him. All sorts of good medicinal materials were hung up, and the recovery took more than a month and a half.

At ten, Shen Xuanqing pledged himself to an old hunter in the mountains as a teacher. Fortunately, he had learned the skill. After his father died, he left the teacher and took to hunting in the mountains, sometimes going to a nearby town or county as a day laborer, carrying a heavy bag. After two or three years, their hands stopped being so tight.

Life at home grew hard for a while due to lack of money. Fortunately, Shen Xuanqing and his elder brother Shen Yaoqing both endured. At that time they were fifteen and twenty years old, sturdy young men who could bear hardships.

Seeing life getting better, Wei Lanxiang considered her second son’s prospects—he was eighteen now, and the marriage should have been settled long ago. But delays over the past few years meant no decent family would want to tolerate a son with no money. Besides, Shen Xuanqing had only been fifteen when he tightened his belt and worked hard with his brother; he was still a child in many people’s eyes.

When Shen Shunfu was still alive, their family life was fairly stable, and even Shen Yaoqing had never known real hardship at fifteen. Wei Lanxiang’s heart softened for her second son, and she refused to treat his marriage with mere perfunctory words—until this year, she fell in love with Lu Wen and betrothed him to Shen Xuanqing.

That twenty taels of dowry were intended for Lu Wen, who was good-looking and knowledgeable. She spent the twenty taels, and yet the Lu family had married someone else. How could she swallow this?

“Let’s go! Let’s go to your Lu family and set things straight with them. I want to hear what Lu Daxiang and Du Hehua have to say!” Wei Lanxiang pulled away the people supporting her, dragged Lu Gu along, and marched out, uncaring that Lu Gu was being dragged and stumbling.

Halfway there, she turned to Shen Xuanqing. “Go and bring me the marriage certificate!”

The good wedding had collapsed into chaos, and the rest of the Shen family could hardly bear it. After Shen Xuanqing retrieved the marriage certificate, they all headed to Lu’s house.

The leftovers in the yard hadn’t been cleaned up yet, and Wei Lanxiang’s anger grew worse when she saw them. She reminded herself that the dishes prepared for today’s feast had been arranged by her son, who had worked for more than a month to prepare them.

Shen Xuanqing, with hunting skills and a generous dowry, had spent heavily on the betrothal; he couldn’t help but feel the pinch. To save on the wedding banquet, he had begun preparing early.

Every table offered ten dishes and one soup: stewed chicken nuggets and wild boar, a dish of sliced pig heart and lungs, a bowl of liver assortment, spicy fried rabbit, and a plate of steamed fish. There were six meat dishes alone. In addition, there were four vegetarian dishes and a pot of meatball soup. It was an extravagant spread, even by the standards of well-to-do villages.

The Shen family’s banquet hall impressed many villagers, who found it hard to finish their meals without wiping their mouths. Not only the children, but even some adults, ate with unseemly gusto, fearing they would be left behind.

Most of the meat dishes were Shen Xuanqing’s doing. In addition to the chickens he raised at home, he slaughtered five or six pheasants on the mountain, chopped them, and mixed them with the chickens, so as not to waste the laying hens. The rabbit meat was caught by him with two hounds on the mountains. It was hot in the summer, and to prevent the rabbits and pheasants from spoiling, he spent extra time capturing them alive and keeping them at home for a while.

He even hunted wild boars. The mountain boars were no easy targets—especially the boars with tusks. Fortunately, after seven or eight days in the deep wilderness, he used the traps he had dug to kill a big one.

There weren’t enough pig hearts, lungs, and livers for more than twenty tables, so Shen Xuanqing went to town to buy more. And more than thirty fish had been fished from the Qingxi River.

It hadn’t been money from the mountains or rivers that was the difficulty; it was the effort required.

To prepare all this, he hadn’t stopped for more than a month. He grew dark and thin, nearly falling asleep late at night. But when he thought of marrying a fulang, his mood rose, and although his expression was limited, his heart burned hot with anticipation.

Ever since they had started to do better as a family, Shen Xuanqing still hunted in the mountains. When his mother gave him dry food, he kept silent. But if he could marry a wife or a fulang, he would at least be able to eat a proper hot meal.

Like any man, he had no other dream aside from working hard to keep the family fed and sheltered. As he grew older, seeing peers marry and have children one after another, and as his mother spoke of it from time to time, Shen Xuanqing slowly began to want a fulang.

Perhaps two people living together in the mountains would be livelier; at least he could talk to someone.

So, after Lu Wen’s marriage was settled, he didn’t protest openly, but he was happy in his heart and would endure any fatigue.

But the moment the red veil was lifted and he laid eyes on another shuang’er, all his earlier joy seemed to be washed away by a cold, clinical washbasin. He didn’t understand why.

Lu Gu wore red; Shen Xuanqing’s frown remained, and he hadn’t changed his clothes, drawing immediate attention as they walked out.

Seeing Wei Lanxiang drag the bridegroom’s face with anger, villagers looked on with puzzlement: how could the Shen family act this way on a happy day?

At the village’s gate they met a few young men chatting, and Shen Yaoqing, left behind, spoke briefly with them, after which they joined.

If they went to other villages to seek trouble, they would pay a price if they were weak. Moreover, he had heard that the people from Qingxi Village had already been fooled; these people had good relations with the Shen brothers and would naturally share a common enemy.

Lu Gu’s eyes were red with fear, his thin body stretched under the loose clothing. This outfit had been made for Lu Wen; it was looser on him and did not fit at all. He poked his head out, then quickly drew back at the stern rebuke.

A group of people charged toward Anjia Village.

When they arrived, they saw the bridegroom and the groom in red. Some people muttered that it wasn’t time to go home yet, but as soon as they saw Lu Gu’s face, they suddenly understood.

He had been dragged almost all the way; Lu Gu stumbled and almost fell, but he didn’t.

The Lu family was not far from the village entrance, and even if someone tried to warn them, it would be too late. Moreover, the group of young men kept their eyes fixed on them; hardly anyone dared to meddle in anything.

The courtyard door was kicked open with a thump, and Lu Gu’s heart trembled. Wei Lanxiang pulled him by the arm and walked into the Lu family home with a furious expression: “From the Lu family, come out for me!”

Du Hehua appeared at the window.

“Du Hehua! You clarified today that my son married Lu Wen from your family, so how could he become Lu Gu?”

In her fury, Wei Lanxiang, who usually spoke softly, raised her voice so that the surrounding people from Anjia Village could hear clearly.

She unfolded the red paper bearing the marriage certificate and announced, “It’s clearly written on it—the fiancé is Lu Wen, not Lu Gu!”

“I want to ask you, Lu family—what’s in your heart?” she continued, fighting to maintain her composure. “Do you want to bully orphans and widowed mothers? I tell you, Du Hehua, you cannot get away with this!”

Looking at this crowd, she felt a twinge of fear, but she refused to be ripped apart by their words. There were so many people watching; she could not let her face be ruined.

Anxious and embarrassed, she pointed at Lu Gu and shouted: “You shameless fellow! I told you I couldn’t find anyone, and you had to carry yourself in on the sedan chair. You shameless, skinless thing—today I’ll beat you to death!”

Du Hehua, who had long grown accustomed to taking Lu Gu’s abuse, grabbed a wooden stick from the corner and rushed forward to strike.

Lu Gu, seeing the stick, instinctively tried to hide, his face turning whiter than before. But before she could strike, Du Hehua faltered under the glare of a few young men who had stepped forward to protect him.
