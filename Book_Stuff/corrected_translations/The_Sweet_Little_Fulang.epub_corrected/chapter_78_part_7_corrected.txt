Chapter 78 Part 7


Hearing his words, Lu Gu couldn’t help but smile, thinking that the name of this soup was really long. Seeing Lu Gu smile, Shen Xuanqing’s own expression brightened as well.

After breakfast, with so much snow in the courtyard, Shen Yan gathered several snowballs to play with the puppy. Shen Yaoqing saw that she had nothing to do, so he smiled and said, “I don’t think the snow will melt for the next couple of days; it’s better to pile it up and make a snow lion.”

When he said this, even Wei Lanxiang praised him. This year, if they had money, they wouldn’t worry about food and clothing. Compared with the previous two years, they could still make snow lions, which looked rather leisurely.

There would be two stone lions like the ones in front of many city houses, and they would shape them with different appearances.

In previous years, when Lu Gu was in Anjia Village, she could only look at other people's snow lions, but she couldn’t do it herself. Now she stood beside Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing, her eyes full of curiosity.

They piled and patted the snow, and even poked and carved the lion’s head with wooden sticks. In the end, they really made a decent snow lion lying on the ground. Although it was rough, it wasn’t as exquisite as the city’s stone lions, but it was unmistakably a lion and looked quite good.

Lu Gu was overjoyed, her face full of smiles. Shen Xuanqing handed her the burnt firewood and asked her to outline the lion’s eyes, nose, and mouth. It didn’t matter if the outline wasn’t perfect; it was just for fun. Shen Yan was still outlining the lion’s tail.

The weather brightened a little, and the tofu shop didn’t sell tofu in the various villages today, or perhaps Shen Xuanqing had gone out to buy tofu before they came to their village, and now the snow gradually stopped.

The snow in front of the courtyard had been shoveled clean, but it had snowed lightly before, and a thin layer of snow fell again. Lu Gu sent him out; the ground was slippery, and she told him to walk slowly on the road. Shen Xuanqing agreed and went to the tofu shop. After leaving the house, Dahui followed behind, tail flicking, and they went out together.

Lu Gu didn’t stay idle after calling Guaizi back. She picked up the big broom leaning against the wall and swept away the thin layer of snow that had fallen on the ground, otherwise it would be painful enough if her feet slipped and fell. If she was pregnant, she would need to be more careful when going in and out.

Shen Yaoqing was tidying up the backyard and the passage; there was only the front yard left to sweep, from the yard to the gate. He was already dressed thickly for winter, and the front yard wasn’t too small to sweep, though he was sweating a little.

Almost every household was clearing snow and shoveling. Standing in front of their doors and looking to the side, there were quite a few people who climbed up onto their roofs with ladders and raked down the snow for fear that the snow would pile up too much and become too thick, causing the roof to collapse.

The Shen family’s second room didn’t need to worry about a roof collapse. Shen Shunfu had spent a lot of money to build the house back then and put in strong beams. The roof was made from mud and rice tiles instead of a thatched shed. They didn’t need to fear collapse since the snow would flow down naturally when it melted, so there was no need to rake the snow.

Lu Gu was about to go back after sweeping when she saw Aunt Miao from the neighboring house come out. There was a road between Miao’s house and theirs, with a persimmon tree planted on Miao’s side. Both families also planted autumn vegetables and winter vegetables beside their houses. In spring and summer, there would be one or two rows of garlic or other vegetables.

“Aunt,” Lu Gu called.

Aunt Miao shoveled the snow to the side, straightened her waist, and said to Lu Gu with a smile, “You folks got up early; the front of your door has already been swept.”

Lu Gu was about to answer with a smile when suddenly there was the sound of beating and scolding from the Zhang family, three houses away from theirs. An old woman seemed very angry, and the voice of rebuke came through: “Beat you! Beat you to death! This shameless thing, dare to steal food, there’s no family law!”

The door of Zhang’s courtyard was open, and the village wasn’t very noisy in the early morning; even though it was three houses away, Lu Gu seemed to hear the muffled sound of sticks striking someone’s body, and her expression turned pale.

Zhang Zhengzi beat and cursed Xiao Zhang Shi from time to time under the instigation of Lao Zhang Shi. The lightest was swearing; the worst was physical violence. Xiao Zhang had still smiled when she first married, but she became more and more reticent afterwards, not daring to scream when beaten. Today perhaps Zhang Zhengzi would make a serious move. After a while, they heard crying and indistinct begging for mercy in the courtyard, saying that she would never dare again.

[Zhang Shi: After marrying, a woman’s name becomes her husband’s surname with the affix “shi,” meaning clan/maiden name; Xiao: young; Lao: old (of people).]

When Lao Zhang Shi heard her daughter-in-law crying like this, fearing that others would hear the joke, she said angrily, “If you dare to bark again, I’ll hit you!”

“Wow!” The puppy continued barking because of the commotion.

Aunt Miao was growing old; she felt palpitations when she heard the miserable cry. She patted her chest while looking toward Zhang’s house and cursed softly, “That old woman has no conscience!”

She also looked down on Zhang Zhengzi, that bastard who couldn’t think straight. He only knew how to beat his wife in the nest, spit on the ground, and then turn his back.

Speaking of it, Aunt Miao was also kind-hearted; she was so close to the Zhang family that she often looked at them from across the street. Xiao Zhang was an honest person; she didn’t know much about the townsfolk, but if a wife in the countryside made a mistake, it wasn’t unusual for her husband to beat her, and others didn’t need to persuade him. Most people in the village were like this, and she was no exception.
