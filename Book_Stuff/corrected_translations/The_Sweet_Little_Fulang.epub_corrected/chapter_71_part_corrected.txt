Chapter 71


Shen Xuanqing set down the cart that leaned against the wall and tidied it up, knowing they could depart the next morning.

Lu Gu had already woven two long chicken coops, about the same length as the cart, and seven ducks and eight chickens could squeeze inside; if they couldn’t fit, they could be carried down in bamboo baskets. They would place the chicken cages on the cart first, and in the morning they would grab the chickens and ducks and stuff them in.

There was still a gap in the cart. Shen Xuanqing picked up the sack containing wild soapberries and set it on top; Lu Gu fetched two baskets and poured them into the sack, which was more than half full, making it really heavy. Although the berries weren’t particularly valuable, a copper coin could buy a lot, and every family couldn’t live without them. In winter, the soapberry tree bore few knots, so they could sell them for some money.

After tying up the bag of mushrooms, Lu Gu looked at the wild vegetables he had dried; machicais were the most plentiful. He had dried a full sack—enough to feed six, even ten people. There were other dried wild vegetables as well, but there weren’t many sacks to separate them, so they were packed together. Dipicai and black mushrooms were rarer than the wild vegetables. Lu Gu packed them in clean cloth bags separately, though there were fewer black mushrooms. He had picked up dipicai many times after the rain, and the cloth bag was almost full after drying.

After Lu Gu dried the medicinal materials in the sun, the bundles that needed tying were already tied up. The most common one was a sack of mugwort leaves. Fortunately, it would lighten after drying and not be so heavy.

Goji berries and dogwood fruits were used as medicine, so he put them in small bags. Goji berries had gradually waned during this period, and there were not so many. He placed four small bags under the bamboo basket, then added camellia and other medicinal materials. A bamboo basket could carry a lot.

As for tianwogens, Lu Gu hadn’t dug much afterward; there were only six leftovers to eat in the past few days, and they were steamed when brought home, so there was no need to take them out after eating.

“Do you want to take chestnuts and hazelnuts?” Lu Gu asked as he pulled out the bamboo basket containing medicinal materials. Except for the chestnuts they had picked up with their family before, the two of them could go to the forest to pick some if they wanted to eat them, and there were still some left over to eat today.

“There are still some at home—how many do we have? If not too many, take them,” Shen Xuanqing said as he walked over. “There aren’t many, there’s only a little left, then I’ll pack it now,” Lu Gu replied, knowing there was some at home, so he asked. “En.” Shen Xuanqing agreed; seeing that the bamboo basket was full of medicinal materials, he took it and said, “It’s too cold at night, put it in the main room first, so you can see it anytime and you won’t be afraid to forget it.” “Okay,” Lu Gu nodded, and a few cloth bags from the low house were brought out.

A bag of dried mushrooms, half a bag of black mushrooms, a bag of dried dipicai, and a bag of wild tea. The dry goods were relatively light. He glanced at the cart; it was too heavy to pull. He simply put these four bags into the bamboo basket for him to carry.

Badgers, rabbits, and pheasants would be taken down tomorrow, and putting these wild animals into the sacks would be fine, but there must be a gap, otherwise it would be difficult to carry.

Shen Xuanqing came to the low house to take another look and said, “Machicais and mugwort leaves aren’t heavy; put them on the top of the chicken coop, and when the cart is loaded tomorrow, take a bundle of hemp rope so you won’t be afraid of things falling.” After he finished speaking, he set to work. After removing the two sacks, Lu Gu saw that there was only food left in the low house, and there was nothing else to bring, so he brought out the bamboo basket, closed and locked the door, and the low house was finished.

In the kitchen, you didn’t need to take salt, dried peppers, and the like, but you had to take half a jar of honey with you. As for the jar of pickles, Lu Gu opened it and looked; there was still a smaller jar. He had pickled sour cress several times before, and he pickled it with salt instead.

Country people bought less, so they liked to pickle more vegetables. But pickles required a lot of salt, and most people couldn’t afford them. Lu Gu only learned how to pickle pickles after he came to Shen’s house. When he was in Lu’s house, most of their pickles were the old-fashioned kind. But the hot-and-sour cowpeas pickled in lotus, they still kept an eye on him.

After the salty lumps (xian geda) were marinated, they could be sliced and shredded. The sliced ones were usually eaten with steamed buns by hand, and the shredded ones were eaten with chopsticks.

Last time they went home, Ji Qiuyue also cut the salty lumps into small cubes, then chopped some ginger, poured hot oil on it, and mixed the ginger and pickles evenly; it was delicious and salty. They broke open the hot, white, flour-steamed buns and dipped them in. It was delicious to eat this alone, without vegetables.

Ginger was good for dispelling the cold and warming the stomach, but there wasn’t much minced ginger, and its taste was rather light. The pickles required a lot of salt, which was precious. Last time they went to a restaurant, though they only ordered meat, Lu Gu heard someone asking for pickles to go with wine. He couldn’t hear clearly, but it seemed to be about fifteen copper coins, which was precious.

He was a bit reluctant, so he squatted in front of the jar, and when he saw Shen Xuanqing coming in, he said, “We can eat it today and tomorrow, and I want to take it with me.” He looked reluctant, and Shen Xuanqing couldn’t help laughing and said, “Just take it with you.”

The mountain road was far away; Dahui and Dabai would have to run back for a while, and Shen Yaoqing and the others would have to walk part of the way. Lu Gu made extra for lunch, and they would eat first.

In the afternoon, while Lu Gu was packing their clothes, Dahei barked in the yard. They hurried outside the courtyard with Shen Xuanqing, and soon they saw Wei Lanxiang, Shen Yaoqing, and Shen Yan arriving, with Dahei and the other dogs running beside them, tails wagging happily. “Mother, brother,” Shen Xuanqing shouted, and Lu Gu echoed. Dahui and Dabai couldn’t keep up, and the dogs were tired from running back and forth over such a long distance, so they were left to rest at home.

Tired and hungry after walking all the way, with the food in the pot, Lu Gu first went to the kitchen and lit the stove to heat it up. It was better to eat the warm food when it was cold, so it wouldn’t hurt your stomach.
