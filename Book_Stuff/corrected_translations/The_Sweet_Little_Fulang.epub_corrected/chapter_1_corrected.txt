Chapter 1


Qingxi Village lay at the foot of the mountains, named after the Qingxi River that ran beside it.

Today the Shen family’s west-side second room was unusually bustling.

Two years had passed since Shen Shunfu had left. With Wei Lanxiang’s help, he found a match for his second son, Shen Xuanqing, and arranged for him to wed Lu Wen from Anjia village, three villages away. Lu Wen, the Lu family’s shuang’er child, was famed for being extraordinarily handsome. He’d attended school in the town, and no matter how he spoke or dressed, he looked different—refined, readable, capable. Wei Lanxiang believed the match with her second son was natural, and she asked Shen Xuanqing what he thought. Seeing his son’s willingness, she was firm and promised a dowry of 20 taels of silver. The marriage was set, and today was the wedding day.

Gongs and drums thundered, the suona cried, and the welcoming procession circled back. When the sedan chair stopped, it was swarmed by people clamoring for money, the shouts and music mingling into a riotous din.

The more guests there were, the happier the host. The Shen family did not skimp on the celebration. Someone drew copper coins from a red cloth and showered them into the crowd. For a time the scene grew even more lively, as everyone surged to grab the jingling coins.

 Amid the crackle of firecrackers and festive songs, the tall Shen Xuanqing, clothed in red, carried out the groom—with a red veil—from the sedan, walked across the courtyard into the main room, crossed the brazier, paid homage, and led everyone toward the new house. He was not usually very expressive, but today, as the big day to marry his fulang arrived, the booing of the young men around him only sharpened his satisfied smile.

In the new room, Shen Xuanqing guided Lu Wen to sit beside the bed, then went to close the door. By local custom, he could not remove his veil yet, and he could not enter until after toasting the guests outside.

The person beside the bed kept clutching the fabric on his lap after he sat down. Shen Xuanqing didn’t think much of it, assuming Lu Wen must be nervous. He studied the thin hands, thinking Lu Wen really did such work at home, the hands bearing old scars where they’d likely been scratched.

After the marriage was settled, someone told him the groom hadn’t even begun to work. It sounded absurd.

When the matchmaker discussed the marriage, she said Lu Wen from the Lu family was a little delicate; though he couldn’t do heavy labor, he was diligent and quick, good at cooking and laundry, and he even made medical sachets that sold in town, either to earn a little debt for himself or to support the family.

Shen Xuanqing never intended to let his fulang do heavy work for money. He knew how to cook and do laundry, and he even hunted, often living alone in a wooden house in the mountains for ten days or half a month. If he couldn’t cook, he could only live on dry rations.

After entering, he stood by the bed for a moment, unsure what to do, as if the idea of his fulang made him nervous. He shuffled a few steps in place, unsure what to do with his hands. Anyone else who saw him would have teased him mercilessly.

“I’ll go out to toast first.” At last Shen Xuanqing remembered the ceremony, rose, and prepared to leave.

Just as he opened the door, he hesitated, turned back, and said to the person by the bed, “I’ll ask Shen Yan to bring you something to eat, so let’s set up a cushion first.”

Shen Xuanqing saw that Lu Wen heard him—perhaps he was too shy to speak—and didn’t press the matter, closing the door smoothly.

When he left, the others in the room grew stiff, and once he was the only one left, it seemed as though a breath left the room and Lu Gu relaxed his posture, bowing slightly.

Not long after, the door was pushed open again, and a girl in a fresh cyan cloth walked in with a bowl of noodle soup. It was Shen Yan, the younger sister of Shen Xuanqing and Shen Yaoqing. She looked to be over twelve, with a round face and almond eyes. She smiled upon seeing the newlyweds beside the bed, still curious about this fulang whom she had only met two or three times. He was the best-looking of the two. Her brother Lu Wen was already handsome enough, and she didn’t know what the day would bring for them.

“Brother Wen, I’ve brought you noodle soup, and my mother asked the aunt who helps the cook to add eggs. Eat it while it’s hot.”

Shen Yan spoke, and placed the bowl and chopsticks into Lu Wen’s hands. No one stirred. Lu Wen’s hand tightened around the chopsticks. Shen Yan smiled again. “Brother Wen, I’ll go out first. You can set it on the table after you finish eating.”

She was young and didn’t think too much, so she closed the door and left.

With no one else in the room, the person beside the bed carefully placed the bowl and chopsticks on the edge, then lifted a corner of the veil and looked around the room in secret.

If Shen Yan had been present, she would surely recognize that the person in the room was not the Lu Wen she had seen before.

Compared to Lu Wen’s refined aura, Lu Gu looked thin and frail, worn from not eating enough to sustain day-to-day work.

The window faced the front yard, and to keep the bride and groom from being seen by outsiders, the window had been shut and bolted from the inside.

Outside, the festivities raged on. People were toasting Shen Xuanqing; it seemed he had finished his drink first, and the crowd cheered.

Lu Gu’s nerves prickled at the noise: the thought of being placed in the Shen family’s sedan chair sent his face pale. He didn’t know the Shen family well, but he knew Shen Xuanqing. He remembered the previous year, when he and a few others had gone to the tofu shop to buy tofu, and a scuffle broke out. Shen Xuanqing had tall, strong arms and a fist that had sent the other man to the ground.

Lu Gu, whipped by his stepmother’s beatings, had always lived in fear. Three months ago, the matchmaker told his elder brother Lu Wen about the marriage, and it was Shen Xuanqing.

Lu Wen was the Lu family’s shuang’er child, born to his stepmother Du Hehua and her former husband. After that man died, he brought Lu Wen into the Lu family.

Lu Gu felt a tremor at the thought of Shen Xuanqing, but he told himself it had nothing to do with him. It was for Lu Wen. At home, his stepmother called him to work, and even his father paid little attention to him. Starvation and beatings hung over his head.

Lu Wen’s marriage was known to Lu Gu only through whispers. But even knowing, he hadn’t spoken a word when it came, lowering his head to work and keeping his movements light, afraid that any slip would anger his stepmother.

In Fenggu Town, once the matchmaker and both families agreed on the arrangement, the two youngsters would meet.

There were rules for meeting. They would meet in another family’s house that had a daughter or a shuang’er present, to allow them to see each other without becoming the subject of gossip.

Logically, how could Shen Xuanqing go to Anjia Village to meet Lu Wen? Yet when they met, it was Shen Xuanqing who came to Anjia Village.

That day, Lu Gu had been left to wash clothes by his stepmother when he caught sight of the tall figure in the distance and slipped around to avoid him.

The stepmother often called him an unlucky ghost. Lu Gu sometimes believed he was truly unlucky. He worried that angering Shen Xuanqing would bring a beating. It was miserable, so he avoided him if possible.

Now, it felt like misfortune had followed him here. Even the absurd plan of marrying his brother’s betrothed to the hunter of Qingxi Village had landed on him.

Lu Gu did not know what his stepmother intended. He could not read, but he knew Lu Wen and Shen Xuanqing had arranged someone to draw up a marriage contract—Lu Wen’s name, not his. How could anyone push through such a thing?

It wasn’t that he didn’t understand his stepmother’s motive. She merely coveted the 20 taels of dowry and did not want to return it to the Shen family.

People said the Shen family offered a substantial dowry, a heavy gift in any village, which showed the family’s standing, and everyone thought it a good match, but Lu Wen wanted to back out of the marriage.

Nobody knew for sure, but Lu Gu had overheard Lu Wen and his stepmother talking six or seven days earlier. Lu Wen seemed to be in contact with people in town; he was already engaged. It was bad luck, and the stepmother had discovered this by accident.

She had Lu Wen marry the hunter in Qingxi Village, and Lu Gu had shaken his head at the time. His stepmother had glared, scolded him for not knowing what to do, and pinched his flesh so hard that it hurt.

Since that day, the stepmother forbade him from going out to wash clothes, forcing him to stay in the yard to do needlework. She watched him constantly, afraid he would run away or tell someone.

Outside, the wedding scene remained lively.

Lu Gu sat on the edge of the bed, not daring to move, but his stomach groaned for meat as the smells from outside wafted in.

Earlier that morning, his stepmother had dragged him to wash and to dress him in wedding clothes, then to place him in the sedan chair, without a stutter. He hadn’t resisted; he had even leapt over the wall the night before to avoid being found and beaten by Shen Xuanqing, but he couldn’t get away. He was caught by his stepmother and father.

In the woodshed, his cries had been muffled by rags; fear and despair had soaked him through, and he’d been beaten badly. For two days afterward he could only eat coarse food. He didn’t even have the strength to run far.

The noodle soup steamed hot, with oil and chopped green onions floating in its top layer. The poached egg lay beneath.

Lu Gu watched the bowl of noodles. He hadn’t eaten such hot noodles in a long time. The noodles were made of fine white flour, and the soup contained an egg.

He lifted the bowl and sipped the hot broth first. The oil, chives, and shallots filled his mouth with fragrance. He hadn’t tasted such flavor since he was a child.

There weren’t many noodles—just enough to fill his stomach. After finishing the noodles and the egg, Lu Gu held the bowl and didn’t want to put it down, slurping the fragrant green onion soup.

He was alone in the room now; no one would take the bowl from him, no stick would fall, so he drank the aromatic broth slowly, trying to remember this taste.

A single tear dripped into the soup as he wiped his eyes, thinking that at least he could eat a bowl of hot noodle soup before he died, so that he wouldn’t be a starving ghost.

What fate lay ahead for Lu Wen? Lu Gu knew that if the Shen family discovered he wasn’t Lu Wen, they would not stop. He’d heard stories of men beating their wives or fulang to death. He believed Shen Xuanqing was fierce and cruel, and today might really be his death.

No matter how much noodle soup remained, Lu Gu felt a twinge of reluctance as he heard someone outside gossiping about the bridal chamber. His face paled once more, and he pulled the veil over his head.

He clutched the bed’s fabric, terrified and trembling, but the noise outside eventually quieted as Shen Xuanqing and a few others teased that the groom was timid and shameless, that his body wasn’t strong, and that he should enjoy the party and drink more. The bridal chamber’s laughter faded, and Lu Gu felt a fleeting sense of escape from death.

When the door opened again and someone walked toward the bed, Lu Gu saw a pair of boots—the sturdy, large shoes of a man—sturdy enough to show his opponent’s frame.

Lu Gu trembled, his knuckles whitening as he clutched the clothing. The red veil was lifted, and his blood ran cold, his mind going blank.

Shen Xuanqing, who had lifted the veil, looked upon the fresh fulang; the smile disappeared from his face, and he stiffened.
