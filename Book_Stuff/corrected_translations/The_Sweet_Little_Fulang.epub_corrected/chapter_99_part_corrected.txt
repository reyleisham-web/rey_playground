Chapter 99


When Shen Xuanqing shouldered two heavy bundles of firewood, Lu Gu’s bamboo basket was already full as well. Seeing that Shen Xuanqing had only tied five pieces of firewood for him with the short rope, Lu Gu frowned slightly and opened his mouth to speak.

“That’s all,” Shen Xuanqing cut him off. “There are still many not cut, these few can be sold, and the ones on your back too.”

Shen Xuanqing didn’t give him a chance to reply. He carried the rope and hatchet on his back and signaled toward home. Lu Gu had no choice but to concede; he shouldered the bamboo basket, loaded the firewood onto it, clutched the hemp rope on his chest, and started down the mountain.

Dahui and Guaizi ran ahead. They saw Shen Yan and the others by the river behind the house on the gentle slope, and they hurried over.

The ducks were swimming in the pond. Besides Wei Lanxiang’s big ducks, Lu Gu’s seven ducks were also there. They were fully feathered; though smaller in size, they still looked like big ducks.

“Second brother has gathered so much firewood.” Shen Yan remarked when she saw Shen Xuanqing’s haul. When she spotted Lu Gu, she went to look at the wild vegetables in the bamboo basket.

“Brother Guzi, I also dug some wild vegetables,” Shen Yan said, and Lu Gu saw the pile of wild vegetables on the ground beside him.

Shen Yan added, “Mother is also digging wild vegetables. Go to the town this afternoon and take me to sell them together.”

“Yes.” Lu Gu nodded in agreement.

The two had to go up the mountain to fetch good firewood for sale. Shen Xuanqing had chopped so much that bringing it all down the mountain took a lot of effort. They had left in the morning; it was already noon now.

“Mother has already cooked the meal. Let’s go back and eat after Brother Daqing collects the fish cage.” Shen Yan squatted down and placed the wild vegetables into Lu Gu’s bamboo basket.

Together with her, Lu Gu quickly packed the wild vegetables, pressing them down because there were too many.

“Sister-in-law, you stand behind; I’ll pull it.” Shen Xuanqing unloaded the load of firewood on his shoulders, walked to the river to help Ji Qiuyue back up. The river was cold, and it wouldn’t be funny if she fell.

Shen Yaoqing rowed the wooden boat, and the place where the fish cage lay was really not far from the river bank, but it wasn’t summer, so going into the water barefoot wasn’t easy.

One side of the fish cage was tied with a fine hemp rope, and the cage could be pulled back after the stones pressing on it were removed, so Shen Yaoqing didn’t need to load the cages one by one into the boat. The wooden boat was small, and eight fish cages wouldn’t fit easily.

“It’s no trouble; this fish cage isn’t heavy,” Ji Qiuyue said, but since Shen Xuanqing stood up, she had to give up her seat anyway.

The fish cages had been in the river all morning, and Shen Yan didn’t dare to leave them unattended. If someone saw the fish cage rope on the river bank and pulled it up to steal, their efforts would be wasted.

Fortunately, it was behind her house, not far away. If she got thirsty or hungry, she could run to the backyard wall to call, and Wei Lanxiang would bring her a bowl of hot water.

After the first fish cage was pulled, Shen Xuanqing looked at the lively fish inside. Because of the nature of the fish cage, all the fish caught were small; big fish couldn’t get in. Among them, most were winter- and spring-season fish, and the rest were some small newly hatched fish that hadn’t grown up yet.

Lu Gu and Shen Yan came over after loading the wild vegetables, took the fish cage, and dumped the small fish on the ground. They selected the winter- and spring-fish and put them into the wooden bucket filled with water, while the small fish were tossed back into the river.

The river water was cold, and the fish were cold too, so they didn’t let Ji Qiuyue deal with it. Besides, there weren’t many fish in a cage, so two people could handle it.

The eight fish cages yielded only small fish, and when brought home it was only about half a bucket. The small fish were only as long as a human finger. But fortunately, the price was higher, at around eighteen copper coins per catty.

Winter- and spring-fish were the best and most tender fresh fish. People who cared about food would buy some to make soup so their children could grow taller. Therefore these fish could fetch a price similar to pork.

After returning, Shen Yaoqing selected eight of the smallest fish and saved them to make fresh soup for the family. After lunch, with the fresh wild vegetables and fresh fish, they went to town with Lu Gu and Shen Xuanqing.

Lu Gu carried a basket on his arm, which held sachets, handkerchiefs, and laozis. A cart of firewood was tied securely with long ropes, and two baskets of wild vegetables were placed on it, the cart pulled by Shen Xuanqing. Shen Yaoqing carried the bucket of fish, because there was no room for it in the cart; besides, the fish were in the bucket, and if the cart jolted, the water would spill, so it was better to carry it.

The firewood was heavy; even thin pieces weren’t light. The two bundles Shen Xuanqing had brought back today weighed more than forty catties. Naturally there would be gaps when stacked, and they couldn’t be packed tight.

A load of firewood was the cheapest among firewood, rice, oil, and salt, and a load of firewood was only forty copper coins. If they were lucky, the firewood in this cart would fetch about sixty copper coins.

Farmers lived like this: they spent the morning collecting firewood and then sold it for less than sixty copper coins.

The firewood was indeed cheap, but the advantage was that you only needed to chop it, unlike fishing with fish cages where the catch depended on luck and Heaven’s will.

After the start of spring, all kinds of shops in the town opened, and there were a lot of people. Lu Gu followed Shen Xuanqing through the streets and alleys, and Shen Yaoqing hawked alongside them. They sold many things, such as firewood, fish, and wild vegetables.

People in town also liked sachets, handkerchiefs, and laozis. Now Lu Gu sold things differently than before; he had learned from Shen Xuanqing and now dared to quote prices a copper coin or two higher. Shepherd’s purse had just appeared and was rare.

There were some sellers who offered it for three or four copper coins; two copper coins weren’t enough for the labor. When others came to ask, he would say four or five copper coins, and after bargaining, he would settle on three copper coins—as long as it wasn’t lower than the market price.
