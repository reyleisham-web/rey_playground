Chapter 17


Speaking of which, during Lu Wen’s stay at the Lu residence, he never beat or scolded Lu Gu. Yet because of Du Hehua and his substitute marriage, Lu Gu still dreaded running into him, and Lu Wen paid him little heed.

After a glance, Lu Gu narrowed his eyes, lowered his head slightly, and said nothing.

“Fortunately, things are better now. Don’t forget to rest and recuperate in the future. You are still young. You’ve been running a short life for the past few years. Fortunately, nothing serious has taken root.” The words of Wang Langzhong drifted to Lu Wen at the doorway, causing his expression to shift slightly, as if someone else’s words might fall on his mother’s head. Didn’t Shen Xuanqing glance over at that moment?

Lu Wen clearly saw that Shen Xuanqing only looked over after Wang Langzhong had finished speaking. The face of Shen Xuanqing, who had always looked so handsome, seemed as if pricked by a needle, yet he never surrendered to embarrassment, maintaining his composure and elegance.

“Pregnancy shouldn’t be a big problem. You two are still young, and it won’t be too late to have a healthier baby. It’s good for both of you and the one in your stomach.” Wang Langzhong continued, noticing the slightly redder marks between Lu Gu’s eyebrows, and he turned to Shen Xuanqing with a cheerful tone: “If you eat like this, it will be easy to raise, and giving birth to a big, sturdy boy won’t be a problem.”

It isn’t easy for a country folk to marry and have children. Wang Langzhong worried that Lu Gu might need to rest and that this would upset others, so he offered comforting words, hoping to give him some hope and, in turn, a little kindness from Lu Gu.

Besides, his words weren’t merely empty talk; the red mark between Lu Gu’s eyebrows was a touch redder than a few days ago. It seemed possible that Lu Gu could bear children, and this also made him think the Shen family might be treating Lu Gu well.

“Then Uncle Wang, don’t you need to take the medicine today?” Shen Xuanqing asked, pretending to look away from Lu Wen and meeting Lu Gu’s gaze instead.

“No, go back and rest, eat well, sleep well—this is better than anything else. And don’t be a nuisance,” Wang Langzhong waved his hand.

The villagers cared about medical care, but farmers didn’t have much spare money. Besides, he had been diagnosed, and Lu Gu had no other serious problems, except that he often did not eat enough, which made his body weak. It would heal slowly as he rested and ate properly.

“Thank you so much, Uncle Wang.” Shen Xuanqing thanked him, then Lu Gu rose and walked out with him.

Wang Langzhong gave Lu Gu his full attention while diagnosing the pulse, and only then did he glance toward Lu Wen in the courtyard.

“Uncle Wang, my father has finished taking the medicine, so I’ll grab a few.” Lu Wen explained why he came, without waiting for anyone to ask. He entered the main room as he spoke, sidestepping Shen Xuanqing and Lu Gu.

Lu Daxiang had finished taking his medicine yesterday. If he had known Lu Gu would be here today, he would have come yesterday so as not to run into him.

Facing Shen Xuanqing, Lu Wen felt a twinge of guilt. After all, he had initiated their marriage. If Shen Xuanqing hadn’t remained still and silent, Lu Wen might have looked at him with pure coldness or simply walked away.

As they left Wang Langzhong’s house, Lu Gu felt that Shen Xuanqing’s mood was not right, for some reason. He suspected it involved Lu Wen; even the sight of Lu Wen brought back the past’s unpleasant memories.

Lu Daxiang was indeed his father. Since Lu Wen followed Du Hehua to remarry, Lu Daxiang looked at Lu Wen as if he were his own son, almost like a real father.

Lu Gu, who was almost eleven at that time, wasn’t truly young anymore. He had lost his mother, and his father treated Lu Wen better than him. How could he not feel uncomfortable?

Beyond Lu Wen, Du Hehua later bore a son. Now that boy was seven years old this year. He was Lu Daxiang’s own son, and it wasn’t difficult for Lu Daxiang to love him.

Lu Wen had always been good-looking and mouth-watering, calling him “dad” or “daddy” constantly, and whenever he went out, he claimed him as his father, earning praise from others.

This was exactly what Lu Gu couldn’t understand. He was, after all, Lu Gu’s father. Yet when people asked about his father, Lu Wen spoke of him as if he were someone else’s dear father, and Lu Daxiang seemed to like Lu Wen even more.

Later, Lu Gu heard cruel rumors from some villagers. The rumors were so harsh that he was stunned, and he looked pale as he tried to understand. That gossip still echoed in his ears, and Lu Gu didn’t dare dwell on it.

And Shen Xuanqing, who walked beside him, hadn’t stayed with the Lu family for long. Seeing Lu Wen, he grew more upset, his face sour as a result.
