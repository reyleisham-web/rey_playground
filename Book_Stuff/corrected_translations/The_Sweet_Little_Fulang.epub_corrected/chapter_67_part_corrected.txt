Chapter 67


Shen Xuanqing was always by his side, and when he bent down to hug him, he did it with quick certainty. Lu Gu had no defense, and his body suddenly rose into the air. He couldn’t help being a little surprised, and the wild fruit he was biting almost dropped, so he quickly caught it.

Hugging Lu Gu came easily to Shen Xuanqing, and seeing Lu Gu pick up the fruit with a panicked expression, he smiled even more.

An intimate hug like this was a bit too close, but there was no one else on the mountain. Lu Gu raised his eyes to look at Shen Xuanqing, thinking. When the two pairs of eyes met, he unconsciously blinked, and never expected that Shen Xuanqing would lower his head and kiss his eyelids. Although it was only a light touch, the warmth on his lids seemed to linger for a long time, making Lu Gu’s cheeks burn and his ears turn red.

What happened that day still made him fear too much physical contact with Shen Xuanqing. The fear gradually faded, but the memory could not be erased or forgotten. He had been terrified by what Shen Xuanqing did, so every time Shen Xuanqing wanted to hug him, he couldn’t help shrinking back.

After one or two such instances, Shen Xuanqing gave up and asked him to close his eyes and go to sleep.

At that moment, Lu Gu blushed. After feeling the warmth and intimacy, he couldn’t control himself and remembered what had happened that day. He was actually very afraid, afraid that Shen Xuanqing would find out he was thinking about those disturbing things. So he struggled to get down, stammering.

“I, I’ll go down.” Lu Gu didn’t forget to hold the fruit in his hand while he was struggling.

Shen Xuanqing laughed, set him down as he spoke, and said: “Take a break, and leave after you’ve eaten.”

Shen Xuanqing remained standing. Lu Gu stretched out his hand, lightly tugged Shen Xuanqing’s pants, quickly let go, and whispered: “You sit too.”

After walking in the mountains for so long, a rest would do them good.

“Okay.” Shen Xuanqing’s smile brightened, and he sat down on a clean stone beside him.

Dahui and Dabai lowered their heads to drink water by the mountain stream, and when they came over, Lu Gu gave them the fruits.

The mountain road was long, and there weren’t many people around. They had walked through several forests in the morning and picked a bamboo basket of mushrooms and some black mushrooms. They could be considered to have returned with a full load.

Today they picked several kinds of mushrooms. Lu Gu singled out what he called torn mushrooms from the countryside. These mushrooms needed to be blanched before frying, because if some mushrooms were left too long and grew too large, they would crack—like a torn umbrella—that was why they were called that.

There was only one big pot in the kitchen, so he lit the medicine stove, brought the earthenware pot to blanch the torn mushrooms, cut some garlic and dried chilies while cooking, and then fried them with lard; they were particularly fragrant and tender.

The water boiled for a while. Lu Gu fished out the torn mushrooms, added some lard to the pot, which melted quickly, poured in the chopped garlic and dried chilies, and stir-fried. The garlic and chili aromas overflowed, and then the mushrooms were fried on high heat, and it smelled delicious just from the scent.

Shen Xuanqing came in from the courtyard. He locked the chickens and ducks in the fence outside to let them graze. After running all morning, he felt hungry when he smelled the food.

As soon as he entered the kitchen and saw the medicine stove on the ground, he remembered that in the past two years there was a youzhong ladle (有种马勺) sold in town, which was smaller than the iron pot and had a long handle to hold it up. The big-belly pot was usually used to cook rice, boil water and stew soup, but sometimes the big pot was occupied by steamed buns and rice porridge, and it wasn’t very good to use clay pots for cooking.

He had seen a youzhong ladle in an iron shop. It had a shallow and flat mouth, and the large spoon could be moved back and forth without restraint. It was just suitable for stir-frying. He didn’t usually cook, so he hadn’t thought about it. Now that he had money, he could afford the youzhong ladle.

Shen Xuanqing didn’t say anything, but he wrote it down in his heart.

When Lu Gu saw him coming in, he knew he was hungry, so he quickly took out the mushrooms, the rice wine was ready, and placed it in a bowl on the chopping board, and covered the steamed buns and steamed sweet corn in the drawer.

Shen Xuanqing brought two bowls of rice wine into the main room, and Lu Gu brought over the remaining vegetables and steamed buns, along with a bowl of small pickles, and fried enough mushrooms to fill a big bowl for the two of them.

After lunch, there was nothing else to do, so Lu Gu drove the ducks to swim. The river was colder, but the ducks had thick feathers, so they could swim in winter when the river wasn’t frozen. They weren’t afraid.

He also brought over a bunch of bamboo strips and planned to weave a few baskets. When the family harvested grain next year, in addition to the cart, they could carry things in the baskets with a pole. He could also use them to collect other things—more is better than nothing.

Shen Xuanqing had nothing to do; he took the puppy and the big dogs around the nearby forest, hoping to teach the puppy hunting skills. It would be three months before the puppy could learn anything, but he could start now. Let him watch how the big dogs chased rabbits and caught pheasants; he couldn’t play with Lu Gu all day long.

Lu Gu sat by the pond making the basket; Dabai turned his head and lowered his snout to sniff around him. Even though he couldn’t see the people and dogs in the forest, the forest was open, and the occasional dog barking could be heard.

Shen Xuanqing didn’t usually go to the northern forest, but today he came here to check the traps, and in passing to see if there were any traces of wild boars and old bears. Secondly, there were fewer people here, and there should be more wild rabbits digging holes than in other places. He checked carefully and swept a large area in the northern forest, but found no traces. He thought that perhaps this year the vegetation was lush, and the wild boars and old bears could find enough food in deeper and farther forests, so they hadn’t ventured out.
