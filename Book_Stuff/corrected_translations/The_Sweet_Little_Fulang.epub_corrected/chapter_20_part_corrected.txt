Chapter 20


The doors and windows were all open, and not long after noon, the sun poured in bright and radiant from outside.

Shen Xuanqing slept in the west room, while the east room had once been the old hunter’s place. He was used to living here and had never changed it. Because of the mountain cold, only one window on the south wall was kept open, the window facing the front yard. The relatively sturdy wooden bed in the room sat on the west side, away from the window, facing the door.

Lu Gu slept on the innermost side, with a narrow gap between the bed and the mud wall. It was different from the bottom of the mountain; Shen Xuanqing’s room had elaborate bed curtains, but here there was only a bare board bed. Afraid of dirtying the mattress by leaning against the mud wall, Lu Gu kept farther away.

The sun shone through the window, bright and majestic, and fine dust danced in the beams. It wasn’t like working or standing up. When he lay down under the eaves, he felt cool after a short while, but Lu Gu lay straight and didn’t even move, let alone pull the quilt over himself.

Both of them were nervous, and Shen Xuanqing was a little nervous too; they remained silent. The mountain outside was quiet, but a few dogs barked now and then. After they came up the mountain, the three dogs hadn’t been tied up; they didn’t wander far. They ran around and played nearby, and when thirsty, they went to the river to drink. Lying on the ground, Shen Xuanqing knew it would be all right after hearing it, but the dogs only barked and then paid no attention to what was going on outside.

He noticed Lu Gu’s stiffness and remembered that when the two of them slept together at night, Lu Gu was the same—so cautious that he wouldn’t turn over even in sleep. He wondered what Lu Gu looked like when sleeping, and what he looked like when waking; it rarely changed.

Feeling cold, Shen Xuanqing sat up and pulled the new quilt from the foot of the bed, covering both him and Lu Gu. The quilt was still in its sack; he was still young and a little shy, and his shyness hadn’t faded yet. He quietly looked down at the quilt. Fortunately, it had been exposed to the sun for a while, so it didn’t smell bad or dirty.

“Get up early, don’t do anything else today, it’s okay to sleep for a while.” After he lay down, he finally found the words.

Hearing his deep, steady voice, Lu Gu gradually relaxed for some reason and finally hummed softly; his stiff body relaxed a little.

Chasing the mountain road all morning hadn’t been easy for anyone. After physical and mental relief, Lu Gu closed his eyes and gradually fell asleep. Shen Xuanqing also closed his eyes and slept. After a while, when Lu Gu’s breathing became steady, he opened his eyes again.

The hand that had pressed against his back and shoulder when applying medicine was so soft that Shen Xuanqing remained half-dazed, thinking about it. Lu Gu slept beside him, his arm not far from his own; if he moved a little, he could touch that hand. The lightly clenched hands were hidden under the quilt—no one could see them, not even the person whose hand was clenched knew.

This sleep lasted more than half an hour. When Lu Gu woke up, his fatigue had faded substantially and his spirit returned.

In the afternoon, the sun began to tilt toward the upper part of the west, still not setting. He boiled medicines for repelling snakes and insects that Shen Xuanqing had bought, stirring them with a wooden stick until it turned into a half-pot of black, steaming liquid. Shen Xuanqing found some unused cloth strips for him and wrapped them around wooden sticks so the potion could be applied to the doors and windows. One layer wasn’t enough; after it dried a little, he had to apply it twice so the potion could seep into the wood. There would always be a smell of medicine, which would deter snakes, insects, rats, and ants from crawling into the room and biting people.

The mountains’ substances were not the same as outside—more or less poisonous. If things were fine during the day, they could be seen, killed, and discarded; but at night, when people were asleep, they were harder to find. The pain of being bitten was mild but red, swollen, and hard for days; needless to say, the pain and itching were uncomfortable.

Not counting the firewood room, there were five rooms in total, including the kitchen and the small room with sundries. All of them had doors and windows. Lu Gu had applied the repellent to all of them. He had bought it with money and wasn’t willing to lose, so he went to reapply the firewood door.

Although the firewood room had no people and no food, if something was hiding among the firewood and hay, there was reason to worry, so he might as well wipe it all down.

The courtyard door stood wide open, and Lu Gu was busy inside. Shen Xuanqing took the shovel outside and dug a narrow trench around the courtyard. The pit was about as deep as the head of the shovel—not very deep. He planned to bury the medicine in it. When he finished digging and sprinkled the medicine powder inside, Lu Gu came out.

Seeing Shen Xuanqing tilt the medicine bag while walking and pour it into the pit, he picked up the shovel on the ground, followed behind, and began to fill in the soil. “No, I’ll do it myself.” Filling the soil was a laborious task, so Shen Xuanqing toggled with him.

Dahui and the two small dogs watched them work, seeming to understand what Shen Xuanqing was doing. The three dogs straddled the narrow pit, half playing, half helping, using their claws to dig the soil. It looked quite stubborn.

This scene drew a soft, shallow smile from Lu Gu. He rarely smiled, and this time the smile lived only briefly before vanishing.

Shen Xuanqing filled the soil, and because of Dahui’s lively interference, he couldn’t help laughing. He looked up inadvertently at Lu Gu, and that look made him notice the smile again.

There seemed to be a pang in his heart that broke the strings, leaving him stunned, ripples spreading like a spring in a quiet pool.

He was used to Lu Gu’s cowardly appearance. Had he ever seen Lu Gu like this before? He felt lost. When he returned to his senses, he only knew Lu Gu was his ger—his husband. Fortunately, he was his ger.
