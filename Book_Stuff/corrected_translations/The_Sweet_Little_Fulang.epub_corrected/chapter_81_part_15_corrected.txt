Chapter 81 Part 15


Lin Zhongcai nodded, and Zhang Zhengzi’s mother confirmed that he had indeed gone out drinking, which made the matter clear. Lao Zhang fell silent when she caught the scent of urine on him.

Lin Zhongcai walked over to look at Zhang Zhengzi lying on the bed. His breath was extremely weak, and bloodstains spread across the pillow, oozing out quickly. He was shaken. “Hurry up and fetch some medicine to stop the bleeding!” Lin Zhongcai said, withdrawing his hand immediately.

Seeing that Lin Zhongcai’s face wasn’t right, Lao Zhang began to cry again. She patted her left leg while crying and cursing. Lin Zhongcai was disturbed by her cries and stopped. “Shut up!”

“There’s no medicine at home,” Li Wanyun said, at a loss, her eyes dull.

Shen Xuanqing hadn’t wanted to deal with the Zhang family’s affairs at first, but when he saw the back of Zhang Zhengzi’s head bleeding, he remembered how he looked a moment ago when he was carried in—now even paler—and he said, “I have a small thistle root (小蓟根). I’ll go get it.”

Jinchuang medicine (金创药) was the best for stopping bleeding, but it was expensive. Ordinary people couldn’t spare the money to buy it. Shen Xuanqing thought about buying some when he had money. Hunting in the mountains was dangerous; since he had come down the mountain in winter, he couldn’t rely on Jinchuang medicine at home, so he planned to wait until spring to buy it.

At that moment, Quanzi’s father summoned Zhang Musheng’s family over, and soon many people arrived. They kept asking questions, the room grew chaotic, and Lao Zhang cried out and wailed in pain from her leg.

Wei Lanxiang and Lin Zhongcai said they would go back with Shen Yaoqing first; they were outsiders, at most they could bring some medicinal materials, but they couldn’t do much else.

Small thistle root could stop the bleeding, and at least they could do something right now. The chaos in the Zhang household had nothing to do with him. Shen Xuanqing didn’t stay long; he handed over the medicine and left.

Early the next morning, news of Zhang Zhengzi’s family spread through the village.

The snow had stopped, and Lu Gu was cooking in the kitchen around noon. Their house was only three houses away from Zhang’s, so he could hear the activity from there, with people coming in and out. Last night, Shen Xuanqing told him that Zhang Zhengzi had been drinking, stepped on his own urine, slipped, and hit his head. Lu Gu was stunned. Shen Xuanqing explained that all sorts of strange things happened to drunk people—some lay down to sleep after drinking and died from something in their stomach, while some choked to death when they couldn’t spit it out.

After listening to what Shen Xuanqing said, Lu Gu thought that getting drunk was indeed a dangerous thing.

“Brother Guzi, here you go.” Shen Yan came in and handed him two red dates, then sat down in front of the stove and lit the fire.

The dried dates were sweet, and Lu Gu chopped cabbage as he ate. Winter vegetables were scarce, so they couldn’t eat this every day. Sometimes it was fried and stewed with tofu, sometimes with meat.

Shen Yan added some firewood to the stove, then looked outside and said, “The sun’s out. Brother Guzi, if it clears up tomorrow, can we make steamed buns and eat them?”

She craved steamed buns all day.

Lu Gu turned his head and said, “That’s fine. There aren’t many steamed buns left, so make some more dough for tomorrow, and we’ll eat steamed stuffed buns and steamed buns together.”

Shen Xuanqing brushed the snow from the trees in front of and behind the house to avoid breaking the branches, especially the persimmon tree. When he came back and heard them talking about steamed buns, he suggested, “There are two catties of meat that have been stored for three days. Why not cut it into thick slices and make steaming bowls (蒸碗), with some fried tofu in them? If you want hot meat, you can just heat it up; if you want fresh meat, go buy more.”

Ji Qiuyue sat in the main room weaving a net and laughed at his suggestion: “That’s good. Steamed meat is soft and delicious, and it can be tucked into steamed buns. If it feels greasy, add some chili oil and sour cowpea.”

“Okay, let’s steam them together tomorrow.” Lu Gu smiled slightly.

While they were talking, Wei Lanxiang returned. Everyone had gone to the Zhang family, so she went to see what was happening there. Lao Zhang was famous for his sharp tongue and stinginess, but he was connected to the dozen or so families around the village. There was a relationship, but they were reluctant to part with eggs and meat. After all, Lao Zhang had never given anything to others, and no one else needed to bring anything to him, but still, you had to drop off some rice flour, just enough to offer something.

“Mother, how’s it going?” Shen Xuanqing asked.

Wei Lanxiang sighed and replied, “Zhengzi hasn’t woken up, and he can’t even drink the medicine. Langzhong hasn’t said anything, but…” She trailed off. If anyone heard her, it would sound like she was speaking ill of Zhang Zhengzi and wishing him harm.

Seeing Ji Qiuyue weaving a net, she walked over and said, “Don’t go out these days. There are so many people outside—what if you bump into someone?”

“Understood, mother,” Ji Qiuyue replied.

Lu Gu’s chopping slowed. He understood Wei Lanxiang’s meaning. The memory of Zhang Zhengzi’s ferocious beating of Li Wanyun yesterday still lingered, but now Zhengzi lay in bed all night and couldn’t even take the medicine. What would Li Wanyun do at that time? But his worries were quickly interrupted.

“Brother Guzi, let’s also make some stuffed machicai. I’ll soak some in a wooden basin tonight, otherwise it will just become cabbage buns.” Shen Yan still hadn’t given up on her buns.

It wasn’t that she was heartless; Zhang Zhengzi had beaten people many times. Shen Yaoqing had told her that a man who beats his wife or fulang to vent his anger is not a man. She didn’t like him; Lao Zhang scolded his daughter-in-law all day long, and she didn’t like her either. She usually kept away from the Zhang family and wouldn’t step forward, and now that something had happened, she didn’t want to pay more attention. Their family had just begun to have a good life, and she didn’t understand these things.

“Okay,” Lu Gu agreed, cutting vegetables a bit faster. There were plenty of machicai for the family to eat, and the buns with it would be delicious.
