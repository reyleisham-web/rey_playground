Chapter 85


The five-year-old girls and the little Shuang’ers were mostly quiet, sitting on small stools, bricks, or stones, teasing one another’s braids or playing with flower ropes. In the open space beyond the big tree, several seven-year-olds were playing spinning tops; the small whips in their hands crackled as the tops spun on the ground. For the better players, their tops hardly stopped, and even the adults watching from the side shouted encouragement. Some men, with itchy hands, even snatched the tiny whips from the children to whip the tops for a while.

The idle men lounged, squatting or standing, while a few gathered to chat and amuse themselves. Under the trees and along the wall, the old people basked in the warmth and gossip. Married women and fulangs sat together, doing needlework and trading stories as they worked. There wasn’t much to do today, so Lu Gu, Wei Lanxiang, Shen Yan, and Ji Qiuyue went to the village entrance to join in the fun; otherwise staying at home all day would be dull.

Fifteen was the age when the girls—Shuang’ers—began to talk about marriage. Even if they were not yet married, they were no longer children, and there were many men in the area, so it wasn’t prudent for them to wander about unaccompanied here. Shen Yan was not very young; she had nothing to say to the children at the village entrance, so she carried her embroidered bag and went to find Caixia and the others. A horse’s head pasted on a bamboo pole made a bamboo horse; five boys rode it and wielded twigs as swords, sweating and shouting as they played.

After the autumn harvest, many people sold their yields for money. Winter was a time of idleness, and the well-off families would send their nine-year-old children to private schools. At that moment, Lu Gu sat beside Ji Qiuyue and heard several women saying that their sons had gone to private schools and could recognize a few words on a few pages of a book.

Shen Xuanqing and Boss Lu had risen early to go to Fucheng to handle the land deed, and it was expected they wouldn’t return until evening. Earlier at home, Shen Yaoqing had taken the three of them to recite the Three-Character Classic, and they didn’t come out until they had finished.

They headed to the village entrance to have fun, while Shen Yaoqing, enjoying the warmth, pulled a cart toward the town to sell firewood, jujubes, and winter bamboo shoots. A bamboo dragonfly flew overhead, brushed Lu Gu’s shoulder, and fell. He instinctively picked it up and saw a little girl wearing a tiger hat approaching. She looked about three years old, her small, dark face flushed, her eyes large and black.

“Is it yours?” Lu Gu asked softly.

The shy girl only nodded and remained silent. Lu Gu smiled and handed her the bamboo dragonfly. The child took it and ran off with several other little girls and little Shuang’ers. They rolled it back and forth in their hands for a while, then released it; the bamboo dragonflies shot into the sky.

All the children wore tiger-head hats, meant to keep out the winter cold. But in the village, the tiger-head hat was mostly worn by little boys, because the villagers valued men more. This particular girl, clearly loved by her mother, wore patched clothes like many others, yet her clothes were clean, unlike the dust-covered kids who played all day. The tiger-head hat on her head was well made, and even Ji Qiuyue told Lu Gu that it looked good.

The sun warmed the world and drove away the winter chill.

Yet not everyone shared in the bustle. In the Zhang family’s room, Li Wanyun still cooked in thin clothes, her fingers red and swollen, her expression sluggish and gloomy. Zhang Zhengzi’s first seven days had passed, and Lao Zhang still had a broken leg and couldn’t go out. She was a woman and couldn’t manage alone; at most, she called Quanzi’s mother to help her carry Lao Zhang out to sit in the yard and bask in the sun. The only son was dead, and Lao Zhang would drift into daze for a long time, until she saw Li Wanyun; as if Li Wanyun were someone she could vent her anger on, she would snap at her, looking a little crazier than before.

From outside the kitchen, Lao Zhang’s tirades gradually subsided. She was old, and her body had lost vitality from injuries, so she needed to rest after cursing for a while. Although Zhang Zhengzi no longer beat her, Li Wanyun’s life remained difficult. Yesterday Lao Zhang’s natal family came to visit the old lady. Li Wanyun poured tea for them, but before she could say a word, Lao Zhang began to cry, lamenting that she was not filial, that she first killed Zhang Zhengzi, and then refused to feed the old woman.

There was another commotion over this matter, and Lao Zhang’s second sister-in-law from the Zhang family also came over. Since the old lady was old and had lost her son, the blame fell entirely on her.

“Hiss.” Li Wanyun stopped chopping the vegetables, so distracted that she hardly knew what she was thinking. A small cut on her finger started to bleed. She pressed the wounded finger to her mouth, her eyes dull and lifeless.

The days drifted on, and there seemed no way forward. The sun couldn’t reach the kitchen, and she shivered in the cold.
